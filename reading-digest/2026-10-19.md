## 1. OfficeTown: Generative Organizations for Evolving Agent Intelligence
- **Authors:** Hongchen Wei, Daohong Ying, Zhi Hong, et al.
- **Summary:** The authors build a simulation of persistent professional organizations in which LLM agents hold roles, delegate work, and review each other's deliverables across repeated project cycles. From the simulated trajectories they assemble OfficeTown Bench (406 tasks, 4,836 fine-grained criteria) and use it as supervised data to fine-tune Qwen3.5-9B. The resulting model lifts scores on external professional-work benchmarks like GDPval, showing that organizational-level multi-agent orchestration generates training signal that transfers back into base-model capability.
- **Theme:** Multi-agent frameworks & orchestration
- **Relevance:** First-class role differentiation, delegation, and peer review with explicit benchmark + data loop — directly on the orchestration/architecture theme and demonstrates a closed loop between coordination design and base-model improvement.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23194

## 2. PRISM: PRIvileged Sibling-guided Multi-Agent Topology Design
- **Authors:** Phuong Tuan Dat, Qi Li, Xinchao Wang
- **Summary:** PRISM frames the multi-agent topology problem as choosing which agent talks to which per-query, and trains a generator with a privileged "sibling" topology (available only at training time) acting as a teacher that recovers a per-query preference signal normally lost to sparse reward. The learned topology generator transfers at inference without the sibling and outperforms prior topology-design baselines across six benchmarks. The paper gives one of the cleaner formulations yet of "what should the agent graph look like, per task."
- **Theme:** Multi-agent frameworks & orchestration
- **Relevance:** Treats agent communication topology as a first-class learned object (not a fixed scaffold) — core to the coordination/role-assignment thread.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20628

## 3. Dream2Discern: Learning from Hard Latent Variants for Runtime Failure Prediction in Multi-Agent Systems
- **Authors:** Boxuan Zhang, Jianing Zhu, Xiaotian Han, Ruixiang Tang
- **Summary:** The system is a runtime auditor that reads an in-flight multi-agent trajectory prefix and predicts whether the overall run will fail, which agent will fail, and at which step. To avoid the cost of collecting hard failure trajectories, it perturbs its own hidden representations to generate "hard latent variants" that increase prediction loss, then trains on them adversarially. The resulting auditor exceeds 90% weighted trajectory-level accuracy on AFTraj-2K and StepShield.
- **Theme:** Multi-agent frameworks & orchestration
- **Relevance:** Addresses the practical "when and where will my multi-agent system silently go off the rails" problem — a missing piece for shipping orchestration, and the latent-perturbation trick is reusable.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16071

## 4. DiscoveryRSI: Recursively Learning to Discover Valuable Hypotheses
- **Authors:** Jianyuan Zhong, Chenwei Wu, Zhaochen Yu, et al.
- **Summary:** DiscoveryRSI formalizes what makes a research hypothesis "valuable and testable" and runs a multi-agent evolutionary search over hypotheses, recursively updating the generator from critique rounds. Evaluated on DFM-Bench across four life-science domains, the quality of generated hypotheses rises across iterations, and a 27B model trained this way beats several frontier models under expert and human review.
- **Theme:** Multi-agent frameworks & orchestration
- **Relevance:** Concrete role split (proposer/critic/aggregator) with a recursive self-improvement loop — a specific orchestration pattern tied to a measurable domain outcome.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23839

## 5. Tree-Aware Posterior Distillation for Long-Horizon Agentic Reinforcement Learning
- **Authors:** Heming Zou, Qi Wang, Yixiu Mao, et al.
- **Summary:** The paper proposes a critic-free RL method for long-horizon agents in which rollouts are expanded as a shared tree and turn-level credit is assigned by distilling from the posterior over successful branches, with a bounded self-distillation term that keeps the student from collapsing. On long-horizon agent benchmarks the method improves accuracy over GRPO-style baselines without a learned value function, and shows that the branching structure itself is what carries the credit-assignment signal.
- **Theme:** Core LLM advances (reasoning, planning, agentic RL)
- **Relevance:** Attacks the real bottleneck for agent RL — turn-level credit over long trajectories — with a mechanism (tree-aware posterior) that is simpler than GAE+critic stacks.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23181

## 6. LEV: Thinking Fast and Deep with Looped Decision Models
- **Authors:** Dingjie Song, Zhiling Yan, Yuxuan Zhang, Lichao Sun
- **Summary:** LEV turns reasoning depth into a resource that is spent on the model's layers rather than on tokens: the same block is re-executed in a loop, and a learned stopper decides per question how many loops to spend before emitting an answer. Compared with chain-of-thought at matched compute, LEV improves accuracy on hard questions while cheaper questions exit early, giving a cleaner compute-adaptive reasoning policy than static CoT length.
- **Theme:** Core LLM advances (reasoning)
- **Relevance:** Clean reframe of "test-time compute" into layer-recurrence with learned stopping — a plausible primitive for agent planners that today pay for depth in tokens.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20951

## 7. BizBench: Unveiling Hidden Challenges in Deploying Long-Horizon Agents for Real-World Business
- **Authors:** Enhan Zhao, Yongqi Tong, Xuejun Yu, et al.
- **Summary:** BizBench evaluates agents on multi-step payments-domain workflows and scores not only end-task success but policy-compliance at every intermediate step. The design shows that frontier agents often reach the correct final answer by routes that violate business rules a human operator could not take, exposing a gap that outcome-only benchmarks miss. The authors release both the benchmark and a trace-level diagnostic to localize where compliance drops.
- **Theme:** Core LLM advances (agentic-system evaluation)
- **Relevance:** Pushes agent evaluation past "did it finish" toward "did it stay inside the rules the whole way" — directly aligned with correctness-of-process concerns.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20424

## 8. FC-SWE: Failure-Conditioned RL for Long-Horizon Software Engineering Agents
- **Authors:** Jia Liufu, Bin Hu, Linglin Jing, et al.
- **Summary:** FC-SWE extends GRPO for software-engineering agents by resetting the repository after each failed patch and conditioning the next attempt on the verifier's feedback as explicit context, so the policy learns from grounded failure signals rather than from random rollouts. Applied to Qwen3.5-4B it reports gains on SWE-bench Verified and shows that treating the test/CI verdict as a first-class training signal is more productive than raw reward.
- **Theme:** Spec-driven development & formal verification for coding agents
- **Relevance:** Verifier-as-oracle for code-correctness training — a concrete step toward spec-driven, test-validated agent code generation.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07898

## 9. Failure Recovery via Belief Updates and World Modeling for Long-Horizon Agents
- **Authors:** Xiyang Wu, Zhichao Liu, Shengxin Zhang, et al.
- **Summary:** BELIEFWEAVER maintains an explicit relational belief state about the task world and updates it across repair attempts using an implicit world model, so each retry is evaluated against the agent's current best model of what is true rather than against raw history. The reasoning is distilled into a 9B student that preserves most of the teacher's recovery accuracy at a fraction of the API cost, giving a cheap deployable failure-recovery policy.
- **Theme:** Spec-driven development & formal verification for coding agents
- **Relevance:** Belief-state + world-model check is the practical substrate for "did the agent's action actually bring the system into the state the spec expected?" — relevant to coding-agent correctness.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18082

## 10. DuoDiscover: Scaling Autonomous Discovery Beyond the Evaluation Bottleneck
- **Authors:** Shubham Agarwal, Akshat Gupta, Mike Heddes, et al.
- **Summary:** DuoDiscover pairs a coding agent that proposes an objective with a second agent that constructs a cheap proxy evaluator for it, then hill-climbs the proxy while periodically running fidelity tests that recheck proxy judgments against the true objective. This lets autonomous discovery continue past the point where real evaluation is too expensive, and the paper shows gains on software and GPU-kernel optimization benchmarks and a CPU microarchitecture task.
- **Theme:** Spec-driven development & formal verification for coding agents
- **Relevance:** Explicitly separates "what should be true" (objective) from "how we cheaply check it" (proxy evaluator) with fidelity recalibration — a verification-oriented architecture for coding agents.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17184

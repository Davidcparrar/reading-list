# Reading digest — 2026-10-01

Found 10 paper(s) in the last 48h window.

## 1. Mid-Harness: Scaling Actions Between Model and Harness for Terminal Agents
- **Authors:** Minki Kang, Ryo Hachiuma, Shaokun Zhang, et al.
- **Summary:** Mid-Harness tackles the gap between terminal agents being able to *generate* a reasonable shell action and that action actually *executing* reliably. The method keeps the policy and the surrounding harness frozen but inserts a sampling + verification layer that proposes several candidate commands per step and lets a stronger verifier model pick one before it hits the terminal. On TerminalBench-Lite this lifts Pass@1 from 50.0% to 68.0% with eight candidate actions, without any fine-tuning.
- **Theme:** Multi-agent frameworks & orchestration (also touches spec/verification for coding).
- **Relevance:** Direct hit on orchestration-level correctness for coding agents — a lightweight verification check between generator and executor, exactly the shape of spec-driven correctness for agent outputs.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39982

## 2. AREX-2: Advancing Self-Improving Agents through Long-Horizon Reflective Tasks
- **Authors:** Hongjin Qian, Chaofan Li, Kun Luo, et al.
- **Summary:** AREX-2 iteratively refines its own solutions at test time by chaining long-horizon reflection with verifiable-feedback environments, so the agent treats each attempt as a draft that a critic step revises before the next tool call. Trained on Qwen3.8-27B, the system reports 81.8 on MLE-bench Lite and 70.7 on Frontier-CS, and the authors show the same reflection recipe transfers to deep-research tasks without retraining.
- **Theme:** Multi-agent frameworks & orchestration + core LLM reasoning/planning.
- **Relevance:** Clean take on reflection-as-policy for long-horizon agents; verifiable-feedback training signal is a plausible route to spec-grounded agent behaviour.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38288

## 3. Learning Meta-Skills for Agent Harness Design in Test-Time AI4AI
- **Authors:** Cheng Qian, Kunlun Zhu, Beibin Li, Zhenhailong Wang, Heng Ji
- **Summary:** The paper studies a "Builder" model that assembles a bespoke harness (tools, scaffolding, resources) for a frozen "Target" agent at test time, rather than fine-tuning either model. Meta-skills encode when the Target needs support and which resources to inject; across Harness-Bench and NewtonBench this adds +8.95 pp macro-average over naive construction and +12.02 pp over simply handing the resources to the Target.
- **Theme:** Multi-agent frameworks & orchestration.
- **Relevance:** Framework-level finding that *harness composition* can outperform model-level upgrades — important design evidence for agent architectures.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38143

## 4. Breaking Babel: A Self-Evolving Multi-Agent System for Long-Form Subtitle Translation (SMART)
- **Authors:** Haibo Jin, Xinjie Li, Najmeh Sadoughi, et al.
- **Summary:** SMART routes long-form subtitle translation through a dynamic router over a Mixture-of-Agents layer, with a judge–refiner loop that scores candidates and rewrites agent prompts rather than retraining the LLMs underneath. Persistent series-level memory lets the system reuse character/style decisions across episodes; across 15 Subtitle Arena directions it achieves the best overall MQM score, cutting average penalty by 6.9% vs competing systems.
- **Theme:** Multi-agent frameworks & orchestration.
- **Relevance:** Nice concrete instance of judge-refiner coordination with role assignment and persistent shared memory — a reusable pattern well beyond translation.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38660

## 5. EvoDuet: Bilevel Co-Evolution of Web Searching and Task Solving for Scientific Discovery
- **Authors:** Young-Jun Lee, Jinheon Baek, Soyeong Jeong, et al.
- **Summary:** EvoDuet treats search-query generation and task solving as a joint bi-level optimisation with frozen LLM weights: at every step a retrieval gate asks the model whether to fetch new documents, reuse cached ones, or skip retrieval, and both the query and the candidate solution evolve together. On 21 OpenEvolve optimisation tasks it raises normalised discovery gain from 74.1→78.0% with GPT-5.6-Luna and 61.3→82.3% with Gemini-3.8-Flash.
- **Theme:** Multi-agent frameworks & orchestration.
- **Relevance:** Shows coordination between a retriever role and a solver role beating single-agent prompting — strong evidence for structured role assignment inside agent systems.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.40340

## 6. False Frontiers: Diagnosing and Mitigating Co-Cheating in Self-Evolving Search Agents
- **Authors:** Meijia Chen, Hao Li, Zheng Lu, et al.
- **Summary:** When a proposer and a solver are co-trained on each other's outputs, the authors show they can drift into "co-cheating": both agents converge on shared misconceptions, so the internal reward keeps rising while external correctness plateaus. CrossFit mitigates this by partitioning proposer source documents into disjoint groups and using cross-fitted agreement so the solver cannot silently collude with the proposer on fabricated truths.
- **Theme:** Multi-agent frameworks & orchestration (also core LLM evaluation).
- **Relevance:** Important failure-mode analysis for self-evolving agent setups — directly informs correctness criteria and evaluation of multi-agent training loops.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39102

## 7. The Teacher Is a Direction, Not a Destination: Extrapolating RL-Induced Representation Residuals (RIDE)
- **Authors:** Hao Li, MeiJia Chen, Weijie Ren, et al.
- **Summary:** RIDE reframes RL-to-SFT distillation in representation space: it takes the teacher's post-RL activations, subtracts the teacher's pre-RL activations to isolate the RL "direction", then regresses the student's hidden states to a point *past* the teacher along that same direction. Across four base/RL-teacher pairs and multiple scales, RIDE matches or beats the RL teacher — the only method whose mean does so — suggesting the teacher's weights are a waypoint, not the ceiling.
- **Theme:** Core LLM advances (fine-tuning / PEFT / distillation).
- **Relevance:** Novel, concrete recipe for transferring RL-gained reasoning cheaply via residuals; a candidate alternative to full RL for reasoning upgrades.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36484

## 8. SAKI: Maximal-Coupling-Routed Teacher Supervision for On-Policy Distillation
- **Authors:** Miteto Wei, Xiaohan Wang, Zehao Chen, et al.
- **Summary:** SAKI uses maximal coupling between teacher and student samplers to decide, token by token, whether the student's own sample is "close enough" to the teacher — if yes, train on reverse-KL; if no, snap to the teacher's top token. The same KL radius that bounds rollout drift also bounds how often corrections fire, and the authors add a speculative verifier that keeps the exact-q trajectory distribution while running 4.22× faster. Gains on seven math-reasoning benchmarks for 0.6B and 1.7B students.
- **Theme:** Core LLM advances (fine-tuning / PEFT / distillation).
- **Relevance:** Tight theoretical story linking trust-region radius to supervision frequency — reusable primitive for any teacher-student reasoning pipeline.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36601

## 9. EVOKE: Eliciting World Knowledge in Agents for Transferable Decision-Making
- **Authors:** Yuhan Guo, Jinming Liu, Liang Xu, et al.
- **Summary:** EVOKE is a post-training method that holds environment state and history fixed and perturbs only the agent's *goal*, then supervises the policy on how action preferences should shift under each alternative goal. The idea is to force the model to retrieve its pretrained world knowledge rather than memorise state→action mappings, and the authors report improved transfer to unseen environments and better data efficiency.
- **Theme:** Core LLM advances (post-training, planning/transfer).
- **Relevance:** A clean, low-cost post-training recipe that targets planning transfer rather than new-domain fine-tuning — relevant for building portable agent cores.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38334

## 10. EngiWorld: What Can Frontier Agents Deliver in Professional Engineering Environments?
- **Authors:** Hongcheng Gao, Hailong Qu, Yu Lei, et al.
- **Summary:** EngiWorld is a 1,301-task benchmark covering six engineering domains (CAD, CAE, CAM, BIM, EDA, 3D viz) across 26 pro tools, with both GUI and CLI task variants. Its evaluation harness is artifact-centric: a domain-verifier suite programmatically checks geometric validity, physical feasibility, and rule compliance on both final and intermediate artifacts, scoring quantitative tasks continuously by specification attainment. Even the strongest frontier model scores only 44.3 EngiScore, with just 3.6% of multi-software runs succeeding.
- **Theme:** Spec-driven dev / formal verification for coding + agentic-system evaluation.
- **Relevance:** The closest fit in this window to spec-driven correctness: verifier-based evaluation of agent artifacts against formal domain rules, scored by specification attainment rather than binary success.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37686

## 1. Just-in-Time Memory: Learning to Curate Task-Adaptive Memory for LLM Agents
- **Authors:** Yefan Zhou, Yang Li, Zeyu Leo Liu, et al.
- **Summary:** Inverts the usual write-time distillation of agent trajectories by retaining raw traces and only synthesizing a compact memory payload at *read time*, once the new query is known. Because the payload is consumed on the same task, the curator can be trained directly from immediate task success instead of grappling with long-horizon credit assignment, and even an untrained read-time curator matches learned write-time baselines on ALFWorld, WebShop, and τ²-bench (with the trained variant adding +16.2/+16.3/+3.9 success points over the strongest baseline).
- **Theme:** (1) Core LLM — agent memory / agentic-system evaluation
- **Relevance:** Directly on agentic memory, the top-priority sub-theme; reframes a widely used design pattern and shows measurable gains on standard benchmarks.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27334

## 2. Qwen-Planner-Agent: A Closed-Loop AI-for-AI Framework for Real-World Mobile Planner Agents
- **Authors:** Tingyu Qu, Weigao Sun, Yuecheng Liu, et al.
- **Summary:** Presents a three-part orchestration framework where specialized sub-agents build training data, a hybrid-environment online RL loop with a new Competence-Aware Reward-and-Advantage Engineering (CARE) objective trains the planner, and an execution-evidence loop co-evolves the model and its runtime harness (memory, skills, tool routing). On MobilePA-Bench, Qwen-Planner-Agent leads the evaluated field with gains attributed to sub-agent coordination and tool-use costs, and improvements transfer to non-mobile agentic benchmarks.
- **Theme:** (2) Multi-agent frameworks & orchestration
- **Relevance:** Concrete multi-agent architecture with a shared action-feedback-verification contract between data, training, and deployment stages — exactly the coordination/role/tool-assignment axis.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29892

## 3. IterSynth: Rethinking Deep Search Agents via Role-Decoupled Iterative Synthesis
- **Authors:** Xingyu Wu, Yuchen Yan, Zhengxi Lu, et al.
- **Summary:** Replaces monolithic ReAct with a two-role loop: a Planner identifies information needs and a Synthesizer folds retrieved evidence into an evolving summary state that serves as persistent memory, cutting both role coupling and context noise. A Role-Decoupled Policy Optimization (RDPO) objective mixes terminal outcome rewards with turn-level rubric evaluations to compute role-specific advantages, and IterSynth-8B reaches a 50.7 average on five long-horizon deep-search benchmarks including BrowseComp and Xbench-DS, beating the strongest ≤8B baseline by +4.2%.
- **Theme:** (2) Multi-agent frameworks & orchestration
- **Relevance:** Formalises role decoupling and per-role credit assignment for a common multi-agent pattern; useful for anyone building planner/synthesizer or planner/executor stacks.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29444

## 4. Agent-Editing World Model: Rethinking World Modeling for LLM Agents
- **Authors:** Shuang Sun, Guoxin Chen, Fanzhe Meng, et al.
- **Summary:** Instead of predicting environment observations, AEWM models how the agent's own reasoning and actions shape task state, pairing an Action Judge (Critical/Exploratory/Noisy) with State Revision that rewrites noisy reasoning-action continuations from the same observed history. The EditAct component then integrates these edits with real execution to change the state underlying subsequent decisions rather than acting as a post-hoc critic, delivering 70.5% macro-F1 on the Action Judge benchmark and +3.2–6.7 point average gains across six benchmarks and three backbones.
- **Theme:** (2) Multi-agent frameworks & orchestration (agent architecture)
- **Relevance:** Novel agent-architecture take on world models tailored to language agents; addresses task-state contamination that plagues long-horizon multi-step pipelines.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28416

## 5. Coding Agents for Generalized Task and Motion Planning Problems
- **Authors:** Matteo Merler, Bowen Li, Josh Roy, et al.
- **Summary:** Frames generalized TAMP as *program synthesis by coding agents*: given a task description and simulator access, an agent iteratively interacts with the environment and writes a program under a fixed synthesis budget, then the frozen program is scored on unseen instances. Across 98,000 evaluation episodes on KinDER and PDDLStream with three coding-agent configurations, mean success ranges 56–95% vs. 47% for hand-engineered planners, and the synthesised programs generalise to larger object counts with an order-of-magnitude less per-instance compute.
- **Theme:** (3) Spec-driven development / correctness-of-outputs for coding agents
- **Relevance:** Concrete instance of spec-driven coding-agent evaluation — the simulator provides the executable specification, and agent outputs are verified against held-out instances at scale.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30233

## 6. EmbodiedSWE: Coding Agents for Long Horizon Dexterous Robotics
- **Authors:** Haoxiang You, Zeyu Shen, Yilang Liu, et al.
- **Summary:** Introduces EMBODIEDSWE-BENCH — a simulation benchmark of contact-rich, deformable-object, and long-horizon robotics tasks — and finds frontier coding agents can solve tasks requiring up to half an hour of interaction and transfer solutions across embodiments. To turn verified single-instance solutions into scalable supervision, EMBODIEDSWE-GEN expands them into diverse trajectories that train VLAs, with a coding-agent-supervised VLA even completing a long-horizon task on a real robot.
- **Theme:** (3) Spec-driven development / correctness-of-outputs for coding agents
- **Relevance:** Uses simulator-verifiable outcomes to define "correct" coding-agent output and pipelines those verified programs into downstream training — a template for spec-driven coding-agent workflows.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27308

## 7. Verifiable Hidden Dynamics Play: Generating Agentic RL Environments from Solved Mechanisms
- **Authors:** Xinjie Shen, Wei Fan, Xudong Guo, et al.
- **Summary:** VHD-Play flips the usual pipeline by sampling and solving a mathematical model *first*, then having a corpus-grounded setter render its decision process as stateful tools whose dynamics and scoring reference are inherited from the same solved model. It produces 3,300 diverse environments at cents each; training Qwen3.6-35B-A3B lifts its mean agentic score from 0.204 to 0.815 in-family, with transfer to unseen mechanism families and external benchmarks (function calling, travel planning, e-commerce).
- **Theme:** (1) Core LLM — agentic-system evaluation & training data
- **Relevance:** Verifiable-by-construction environments address a persistent gap in agent training; the stateful-vs-static comparison isolates *interactive* learning from problem solving.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27321

## 8. WhatWorkedBench: Benchmarking Experimental Understanding in AI Agents
- **Authors:** Jingjie Ning, Xueqi Li, Yibo Kong, et al.
- **Summary:** Measures whether research agents can accurately predict how component changes affect outcomes by having them inspect code, choose measurements under a budget, and submit a full response-surface table, then scoring against exhaustive CPU-executed ground truth across 36 tasks and 8 workflow families. Fitting a Gaussian process to the same agent observations raises effect recovery from 0.632→0.698 and 0.621→0.720 in two cohorts, and encoding code-equivalence structure lifts GP recovery from 0.248→0.462 on binary-option workflows.
- **Theme:** (1) Core LLM — agentic-system evaluation
- **Relevance:** Rare benchmark aimed squarely at *causal* experimental understanding — a capability agentic-eval work usually skips.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27490

## 9. ExplorationBench: Measuring AI Systems' Exploration in Verifiable Alien Worlds
- **Authors:** Ming Zhang, Zhenghao Xiang, Peizhong Gao, et al.
- **Summary:** Builds two sandboxes (AlienCode with 31 discovery targets and AlienLogic with 24) whose rules are executable but conflict with familiar knowledge, so tasks cannot be solved from pretraining recall; each ships with a flawed manual, task-specific feedback, and a tool-call schema. Evaluating 10 systems shows top models can acquire and apply unfamiliar rules but exhibit high trajectory variance and stalls or reversals with continued exploration.
- **Theme:** (1) Core LLM — agentic-system evaluation / planning
- **Relevance:** Cleanly separates recall from genuine exploration — an evaluation methodology directly relevant to reasoning/planning claims about frontier agents.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30199

## 10. Rufus-Air: An Open LLM Post-Training Recipe
- **Authors:** Chia-Yuan Chang, Renyuan Cheng, Rui Feng, et al.
- **Summary:** Documents a fully reproducible eight-stage post-training pipeline on GLM-4.5-Air-Base (106B-A12B) — SFT, then Reasoning RL, Coding RL, Instruction-Following RL, General/Coding/Search agent stages, and finally RLHF — using only open components and public data. The paper's takeaways: high-quality SFT sets a strong capability floor, difficulty filtering keeps RL prompts productive, reward reliability dictates stage ordering, and infrastructure/engineering choices are treated as first-class recipe ingredients; the result beats the official GLM-4.5-Air post-trained release.
- **Theme:** (1) Core LLM — fine-tuning / post-training
- **Relevance:** Concrete, open, end-to-end recipe covering reasoning, coding, and multiple agent RL stages — a practical reference for the "how do you actually post-train an agentic model" question.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29421

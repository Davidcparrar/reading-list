## 1. ClawGym II: Exploring Black-Box RL on Agent Harness
- **Authors:** Huatong Song, Fei Bai, Ming Yang, et al.
- **Summary:** The paper proposes a black-box reinforcement-learning loop that treats a complete agent harness (LLM, tools, scaffolding) as an opaque environment and optimizes it through sandboxed rollouts. Trajectories are reconstructed via a prefix tree so that partial-credit signals from long runs can be recycled into stable updates, and the resulting recipe reports Pass@1 gains of roughly 10–17 points on general-agent benchmarks without touching model internals.
- **Theme:** (1) Core LLM advances — agentic-system training/evaluation
- **Relevance:** Directly targets the "fine-tune the whole harness, not just the model" problem central to shipping robust LLM agents.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16798

## 2. HarnessEval-W: Agentifying the Evaluation of Visual Worlds
- **Authors:** Weiliang Chen, Haowen Sun, Jun Gao, et al.
- **Summary:** HarnessEval-W turns evaluation itself into an agentic pipeline: a top-level judge dispatches sub-agents that each verify one facet of a generated world, and their intermediate findings are stitched into an explicit evidence tree that grounds the final verdict. Across 330 cases and 18 systems the framework reproduces human preference rankings while exposing which sub-check drove each score, replacing scalar leaderboard numbers with auditable diagnoses.
- **Theme:** (1) Core LLM advances — agentic-system evaluation
- **Relevance:** A concrete pattern for evaluation harnesses that stay legible as agent behaviour gets more compositional.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16859

## 3. Learn What's Left, Not What's Mastered: Saturation-Aware Advantage Reweighting for Multi-Reward Policy Optimization (SA-MRPO)
- **Authors:** Yixuan Wang, Yifei Chen, Haichao Zhang, et al.
- **Summary:** SA-MRPO tackles the failure mode where multi-reward RLHF stalls once one objective is nearly satisfied and starves the others of gradient. Each reward stream is standardized independently, and per-objective advantages are down-weighted as their saturation rises, so policy updates redirect capacity toward the objectives that still have headroom. The authors report consistent gains on multi-goal reasoning tasks where prior GRPO/DPO variants plateau.
- **Theme:** (1) Core LLM advances — fine-tuning for reasoning
- **Relevance:** A drop-in tweak for anyone running multi-reward RL on reasoning stacks; addresses a real, common pathology.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16072

## 4. R³-Bench: LLMs Struggle with Resource-Rational Reasoning under Shared Budgets
- **Authors:** Peisong Wang, Zhiwei Ma, Bowen Liu, et al.
- **Summary:** R³-Bench probes whether frontier LLMs can plan under a shared thinking/token budget that must be spent across sub-problems in math, code and general reasoning. The authors find a wide gap between what models can solve unconstrained and what they actually achieve when forced to allocate compute — over-thinking the first problems and starving the rest — and use the benchmark to compare budget-allocation heuristics.
- **Theme:** (1) Core LLM advances — reasoning and planning
- **Relevance:** Formalises "compute is a first-class resource" for reasoning agents, which is exactly what agent orchestration has to model.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16033

## 5. MELD: A Protocol for Merging Knowledge Across Distributed Agentic Memories
- **Authors:** Lauri Lovén, Jaakko Sauvola, Jukka Riekki, et al.
- **Summary:** MELD proposes a self-managing coherence layer for agents that each maintain private memories but must reconcile overlapping claims. Reconciliation combines three signals — scoped claim-key identity, embedding similarity, and a natural-language-inference verdict — merged through a status CRDT so that conflicting facts converge without a central coordinator, and the authors show partition-recovery with ~11% less storage than centralised memory.
- **Theme:** (2) Multi-agent frameworks & orchestration — communication/memory protocols
- **Relevance:** A concrete, distributed-systems-flavoured take on the "shared memory for a fleet of agents" problem.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16357

## 6. GenRouter: Unified Workflow Routing for Agentic Image Generation
- **Authors:** Harold Haodong Chen, Zhiyu Hou, Wen-Jie Shu, et al.
- **Summary:** GenRouter treats a zoo of image-generation pipelines (T2I, edit, inpaint, control-net stacks) as a set of workflows and learns a router that profiles each prompt's demands and Pareto-filters candidate workflows before executing one. On heterogeneous prompt mixes it reports large cost and latency reductions versus running a single generic pipeline, and the framing generalises beyond images to any agent system that must pick a tool chain per request.
- **Theme:** (2) Multi-agent frameworks & orchestration — role/tool assignment
- **Relevance:** A clean instance of "which sub-agent/workflow should handle this task", the core routing problem in orchestration.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16721

## 7. Physics of Agents: Statistical Mechanics Predicts Collective Behavior of AI Agents
- **Authors:** Batu El, Jinhee Paeng, Fatih Dinc, et al.
- **Summary:** The authors simulate more than 10,000 populations of LLM agents exchanging messages and fit a statistical-mechanics model that identifies three phases — indifference, polarisation, and consensus — parameterised by a "social temperature." The fitted model predicts individual agent trajectories better than message-level baselines and offers an interpretable account of how conviction accumulates in multi-agent chats.
- **Theme:** (2) Multi-agent frameworks & orchestration — architectures/coordination
- **Relevance:** Rare quantitative theory for what large agent societies actually do, useful for designers who need to reason about emergent dynamics.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16578

## 8. A Policy Algebra for Trust-Preserving Agentic AI Execution
- **Authors:** Bhaskar Tripathi, Anurag Kumar, Ramendra Kumar, et al.
- **Summary:** The paper defines a small algebra of composable trust policies (admissible actions, path constraints, obligation operators) and proves that "reliable capability" can be expressed as a path property over an agent's action trace. A runtime monitor built on the algebra intercepted ~95% of policy violations in the evaluation while retaining ~87% task completion, giving a formal handle on what an agent is allowed to do.
- **Theme:** (3) Spec-driven development & formal verification
- **Relevance:** One of the cleanest recent attempts to give agent-behaviour specifications a real algebraic footing rather than ad-hoc guardrails.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16402

## 9. The Specification Paradox: Rethinking Requirements Engineering in the Age of AI
- **Authors:** Tassio Sirqueira, Jessica Faciroli, et al.
- **Summary:** A position paper arguing that as LLMs erode coding cost, the binding constraint on software quality shifts to specification — correctness, completeness, verifiability, explainability — and that current practice is underinvested there. It names failure modes ("automation bias," "ambiguity propagation," "specification debt") and sketches what a requirements-engineering discipline for AI-generated code would need to cover.
- **Theme:** (3) Spec-driven development & formal verification
- **Relevance:** Framing piece that sharpens the case for treating specs, not code, as the primary artefact in an LLM-coding workflow.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16618

## 10. OpenHarmony Bench: Evaluating LLMs and Coding Agents on OpenHarmony App Development
- **Authors:** Li Li, Han Hu, Tianjian Zhang, et al.
- **Summary:** OpenHarmony Bench evaluates coding agents on end-to-end app tasks (UI, persistence, platform APIs) on the OpenHarmony stack, including a spec-driven track where models must satisfy a structured scenario specification. Buildability saturates near 100% across models, but behavioural correctness under the spec-driven track stays under 35%, quantifying the gap between "compiles" and "meets the spec" for current agents.
- **Theme:** (3) Spec-driven development & formal verification
- **Relevance:** Empirical evidence for the specification paradox above — the same failure mode measured on a real app-development harness.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16022

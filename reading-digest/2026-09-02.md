## 1. Control-Data Flow Separation: Stable Prompt Optimization in Multi-Agent LLMs
- **Authors:** Wentao Zhang, Syed Shariyar Murtaza, Junaid Ahmad Bhatti
- **Summary:** End-to-end prompt optimizers routinely break multi-agent pipelines because they overwrite tool signatures, role definitions, and inter-agent handoffs in the process of rewriting task instructions. The paper factors each agent prompt into a fixed "control-flow" surface (protocol, tools, roles) and a mutable "data-flow" surface (natural-language content), and only lets the optimizer touch the latter, yielding stable pipelines under aggressive prompt search.
- **Theme:** (2) Multi-agent frameworks & orchestration
- **Relevance:** Direct hit on orchestration + coordination protocols — cleanly separates the parts of an agent contract you want to keep formal from the parts you want to learn.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00621

## 2. Harness-of-Harness: Multi-Day Autonomous Software Development with Continual Improvement
- **Authors:** Haoyang Yan, Min-le Su, Hangfan Zhang
- **Summary:** Wraps an LLM coding agent inside an outer planning-coding-testing loop that runs across multiple days and folds each run's test outcomes back into the plan for the next iteration. Reports substantial gains across coding benchmarks vs. single-shot agent runs, arguing that "harness of harnesses" self-improvement matters more than a stronger inner model.
- **Theme:** (3) Spec-driven / verification-adjacent coding agents
- **Relevance:** Closest fit to defining and enforcing correctness of a coding agent's output — the harness treats tests as the executable spec that drives the next iteration.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01481

## 3. Agents in the Large: Perception-Centered Architecture for Persistent Agents (Pera)
- **Authors:** Shihan Dou, Haoxiang Jia, Shichun Liu
- **Summary:** Architectural proposal for long-lived ("persistent") language agents that treats perception and control as first-class subsystems continually reading environmental signals rather than a static plan-then-execute loop. Shows the persistent-agent architecture keeps service quality stable as the environment drifts, where a fixed planner-plus-memory degrades.
- **Theme:** (2) Multi-agent frameworks & orchestration
- **Relevance:** Agent architecture piece explicitly aimed at the long-horizon, always-on regime that most orchestration papers duck.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30478

## 4. DiagEvo: Diagnosis-Guided Self-Evolution via Hierarchical Error Memory
- **Authors:** Xincheng Wei, Yifan Ding, Yoshua Li
- **Summary:** Self-play curricula plateau because the trainer can't tell why the solver failed. DiagEvo keeps a hierarchical error-cause memory and uses it to synthesize the next batch of training problems targeted at the solver's actual failure modes; the resulting curriculum outperforms prior self-play baselines on nine mathematical reasoning benchmarks.
- **Theme:** (1) Core LLM reasoning
- **Relevance:** Concrete method paper for self-improving reasoning models with an actionable diagnosis signal instead of vague pass/fail.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00768

## 5. Scaling Large Reasoning Models beyond Human Supervision: A Path toward Superintelligence
- **Authors:** Zhiqin Yang, Jingwen Fu, Yuhan Liu
- **Summary:** Position paper mapping the design space for continuing to scale reasoning models once human-written supervision is exhausted, covering autonomous reward signals, self-generated experience, and iterative refinement. Enumerates the specific failure modes any such recipe has to defend against (reward hacking, curriculum collapse) and where current systems already stumble.
- **Theme:** (1) Core LLM reasoning
- **Relevance:** Useful frame for the "what breaks when you take the human out of the loop" question that shows up in every agentic RL paper this month.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.31075

## 6. PaperGym: Rubric-Centered Evolution for Research-Plan Generation
- **Authors:** Yuhan Wang, Zhengxi Lu, Yuchen Yan
- **Summary:** Converts published research papers into RL-style environments by decoupling the research question from an explicit evaluation rubric, so an agent's proposed research plan can be scored objectively rather than by ad-hoc LLM-as-judge. Reports +5.6 / +5.0 / +4.8 average improvements across five agentic-research benchmarks after training under this environment.
- **Theme:** (1) Core LLM agentic-system evaluation
- **Relevance:** Rubric-first evaluation is exactly the kind of "written-down spec for what a good answer looks like" that the coding-agent side of the reading list keeps needing.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.31119

## 7. Learning to Evaluate Before Improving: Automatic Rubric Induction for Research Agents
- **Authors:** Xuehai Wang, Haowei Qin, Tongxin Liu
- **Summary:** Argues an autonomous research agent should first induce a task-specific rubric and only then attempt the task, giving iterative improvement a stable target. Their induction module lifts average scores 2–17 points across the evaluation suite, with the biggest gains on tasks whose default rubrics are vague.
- **Theme:** (1) Core LLM agentic-system evaluation
- **Relevance:** Complements PaperGym — instead of assuming a rubric is provided, the agent writes one, which is the operational form of "spec-driven" for open-ended tasks.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.31076

## 8. Normalized Low-Rank Adaptation
- **Authors:** Jiale Kang, Ziyin Yue, Zheng Zhan
- **Summary:** Identifies that the down-projection matrix in standard LoRA drifts in scale during training and destabilizes updates. A row-wise normalization of that matrix (no extra parameters, no changed hyperparameters) improves convergence for both continued pretraining and RL fine-tuning across their sweep.
- **Theme:** (1) Core LLM PEFT
- **Relevance:** Tiny-diff, high-signal PEFT paper — the kind of change you can port into an existing LoRA training script immediately.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.31036

## 9. Does On-Policy Distillation Really Distill? From Noisy Teacher to Self-Improvement
- **Authors:** Yi Ding, Ruqi Zhang, et al.
- **Summary:** Probes what an on-policy distillation student actually learns from a noisy teacher and finds gradient signal concentrates almost entirely on low-probability tokens, not high-confidence teacher preferences. Uses that observation to propose an entropy-adaptive, supervision-free objective that matches or exceeds teacher-supervised distillation.
- **Theme:** (1) Core LLM fine-tuning
- **Relevance:** Sharp mechanistic critique of a widely-used training pipeline, with a drop-in alternative.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.31046

## 10. Lies We Can See: Joint Verbal and Non-Verbal Deception by VLM Agents in Embodied Social Interactions
- **Authors:** Jaewoo Ahn, Junseo Kim, Hyunseo Kim
- **Summary:** Puts VLM-driven embodied agents in a 3D social-interaction testbed where both spoken dialogue and non-verbal cues (gaze, motion, positioning) are available deception channels. The non-verbal channels turn out to be the decisive contributor to successful deception, which matters for anyone building multi-agent systems that reason over each other's behavior rather than only their words.
- **Theme:** (2) Multi-agent frameworks & orchestration (agent behavior in embodied multi-agent settings)
- **Relevance:** Reminder that agent communication protocols are not just their text channel; useful counterweight to purely-textual multi-agent design.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30428

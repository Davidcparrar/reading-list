## 1. CoworkOS: Scaling Agent Coworkers for Real-world Automation

- **Authors:** Jiajie Jin, Guanting Dong, Chengjun Pan et al.
- **Summary:** CoworkOS is an agent harness that factors long-horizon work into a planning layer that holds the task strategy and an execution layer that drives tools, with first-class support for spawning subagents, sharing artifacts between them, and keeping a persistent memory across sessions. The authors evaluate it on 12 benchmarks covering browsing, computer-use, and research tasks, and run a self-improvement case study where the harness rewrites its own prompts.
- **Theme:** Multi-agent frameworks & orchestration.
- **Relevance:** Direct match for theme (2) — it is a concrete coordination architecture with a role/tool assignment contract and a persistent-artifact substrate, which is exactly the orchestration surface the digest targets.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03178

## 2. ToolSpace: Scaling Tool-Centric Environment Synthesis for Long-Horizon Agents

- **Authors:** Guanting Dong, Zhichao Sun, Xinyu Yang et al.
- **Summary:** ToolSpace mines real tool-call execution traces from logs, then synthesises replayable multi-step tasks on top of them, with each synthesized task verified end-to-end before it enters the training pool. The result is a tool-use environment family that can grow without human-authored tasks, aimed at training long-horizon agents whose plans span many tool invocations.
- **Theme:** Multi-agent frameworks & orchestration (tool-use environments for agent training).
- **Relevance:** Fills in the environments side of theme (2): orchestration research needs reproducible task generators, and ToolSpace is one of the cleanest recent attempts at verified, replayable, tool-centric synthesis.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05036

## 3. WebHarbor: Docking Real Websites for Evolving GUI Agent Environments

- **Authors:** Zhaoyang Wang, Qianhui Wu, Boyu Gou et al.
- **Summary:** WebHarbor packages 153 real websites into dockerized environments with deterministic control knobs (fixed inventories, pinned DOMs, replayable backends) so GUI-agent evaluation and RL training don't drift when the live web changes. The authors curate 3,518 web tasks on top, including multi-step and post-login workflows, and show that a 4B GUI agent trained inside WebHarbor generalises to unseen tasks.
- **Theme:** Multi-agent frameworks & orchestration (agent evaluation substrate).
- **Relevance:** Evaluation infrastructure is a known bottleneck for the orchestration theme; a deterministic multi-site harness is exactly the piece a reader following theme (2) should know about.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03634

## 4. CUA-Master: Scaling Computer-use Agents through Open-World Recursive Improvements

- **Authors:** Xinlei Yu, Gen Li, Ji Zhao et al.
- **Summary:** CUA-Master is a nested self-improvement loop for computer-use agents: it mines workflows from instructional videos, uses feedback-guided retries to generate diverse training tasks, and recurses on the outputs. The released 27B model reports competitive numbers across several GUI and hybrid benchmarks without relying on privileged observations.
- **Theme:** Multi-agent frameworks & orchestration (self-improving agent architecture).
- **Relevance:** Shows a practical loop between data-generation agents and policy agents — the kind of coordination pattern theme (2) is most concerned with.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03126

## 5. Axion 1.0: Open Agent Foundation Models, Simplified

- **Authors:** Yuqi Jia, Yunjie Tian, Jiayu Zheng et al.
- **Summary:** Axion 1.0 is an open agent foundation model trained with a three-stage recipe (two supervised fine-tuning stages, then a reinforcement learning stage) that the authors release with the training data and code. The design explicitly targets agentic skills — tool use, multi-turn planning, and self-correction — rather than general chat.
- **Theme:** Multi-agent frameworks & orchestration (open base for agent stacks).
- **Relevance:** Open, reproducible agent bases are rare; an SFT+SFT+RL recipe with released artifacts is directly useful for the orchestration community.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03233

## 6. Recursa: Toward Recursively Self-Improving Foundation Model Systems

- **Authors:** Erkang Zhu, Yin Lin, Xiaorong Shi et al.
- **Summary:** Recursa adapts a foundation model to a new domain by writing reflections and distilled skills into a knowledge repository, verifying candidate self-generated improvements against held-out tasks, then merging the ones that survive back into the agent. On Harvey LAB's Firm Knowledge benchmark it raises the held-out all-pass rate over a non-recursive baseline.
- **Theme:** Multi-agent frameworks & orchestration (meta-level agent architecture).
- **Relevance:** Treats the agent system itself as the artifact being optimized, which is the architectural frontier theme (2) is converging on.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04035

## 7. Measuring AI Agents as Teammates with Collaborative Intelligence

- **Authors:** Sean Wu, Aneesh Pappu, Jiaru Zou et al.
- **Summary:** A 240-task benchmark that scores agents along eight teamwork dimensions (planning handoff, context carry, interpersonal pressure, etc.) using verifiable outcomes drawn from real work records. Evaluating 18 models, the authors find that general single-agent capability does not predict teamwork quality and that most models degrade sharply under interpersonal pressure.
- **Theme:** Core LLM advances (agentic-system evaluation).
- **Relevance:** Theme (1) explicitly lists "agentic-system evaluation"; this is the first serious attempt I've seen to decouple teamwork competence from base model skill.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02633

## 8. Can Coding Agents Sustain Continual Evolution without Forgetting?

- **Authors:** Shangziqi Zhao, Yitong Zhang, Jia Li et al.
- **Summary:** An empirical study of coding-agent harnesses that evolve across successive software-engineering tasks, measuring whether later evolution preserves resolution rates on earlier benchmarks. The finding is that unchecked self-evolution sharply degrades scores on SWE-Bench-style evaluations, i.e. coding agents exhibit catastrophic forgetting at the harness level.
- **Theme:** Multi-agent frameworks & orchestration (closest-in-window stand-in for theme 3, with coding-agent correctness angle).
- **Relevance:** The one paper in the window that addresses correctness drift of coding agents over time — the practical problem that spec-driven and formal-verification work is meant to constrain.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03219

## 9. Think Less, Locally: Local Preference Learning for Reasoning Compression

- **Authors:** Muyao Yuan, Liyan Xu, Weizhan Zhang et al.
- **Summary:** LoCo scans a model's long reasoning traces for "overthinking points" — states where the model begins to loop or over-elaborate — and trains with DPO on preferred shorter continuations from exactly those states instead of on whole-trace preferences. On math and code benchmarks, completions shrink noticeably while accuracy is preserved.
- **Theme:** Core LLM advances (reasoning, fine-tuning).
- **Relevance:** Surgical reasoning compression is a cleaner fit for theme (1) than most generic chain-of-thought work this week; the local-DPO framing is reusable.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04814

## 10. Route Anything: A Study of Model and Policy Routing for General-Purpose AI Systems

- **Authors:** Pengfei Zhou, Yixing Ma, Hexin Wang et al.
- **Summary:** A systematic study of routing inputs across many specialized models and policies, framed as a quality-versus-cost trade-off and measured on 25 benchmarks. The paper separates the "route the model" and "route the decoding policy" problems and shows that a learned router dominates fixed-best-model baselines once cost is in the objective.
- **Theme:** Core LLM advances (agentic-system evaluation, system-level planning).
- **Relevance:** Routing is the control plane that any multi-model orchestration stack inherits; this is the most methodologically careful recent treatment of it.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05275

1. **AgentGrad: Intervention-guided Prompt Optimization for Multi Agent Systems**
   - Authors: Jaewon Chu, Jinwoo Seo, Jaewon Cho, et al.
   - Summary: Proposes a gradient-free optimizer that treats a multi-agent system as a computation graph and uses sequential interventions to isolate which agent in the pipeline is responsible for downstream failures, then clusters those failure signals semantically so unrelated error modes don't collapse into one confused prompt edit. The method feeds those localized signals back as targeted per-agent prompt updates, which lets the framework improve overall pipeline accuracy without joint fine-tuning of the underlying LLMs.
   - Theme: (2) multi-agent frameworks & orchestration
   - Relevance: Directly on the highest-priority multi-agent-orchestration angle — credit assignment across agents in a pipeline is a hard, understudied problem, and the intervention framing is a novel take.
   - Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08572

2. **T1: Terminal Agent Reinforcement Learning for Long-Horizon Tasks**
   - Authors: Junyao Yang, Yucheng Shi, Zhongzhi Li, et al.
   - Summary: Trains a 122B mixture-of-experts model end-to-end with reinforcement learning on shell/terminal tasks, targeting the long-horizon regime where reward is sparse and trajectory length is high. The paper reports gains attributed to stability tricks in the RL optimizer and to training on deliberately out-of-distribution task mixes so the policy generalizes across unfamiliar CLI environments rather than memorizing benchmark shells.
   - Theme: (1) core LLM advances — planning, agentic RL
   - Relevance: A rare open recipe for RL on long-horizon coding-agent tasks at MoE scale; the OOD-training claim is worth verifying.
   - Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11042

3. **Φ-Bench: Can Large Language Models Engineer the Infrastructure That Powers Them?**
   - Authors: Leilei Ding, Shumin Wang, Yuting Huang, et al.
   - Summary: A benchmark of open-ended infrastructure-engineering tasks spanning GPU-kernel optimization, distributed-training glue, and end-to-end system design, scored by whether the produced artifact actually runs and hits performance targets rather than by string match. The construction is explicit that current frontier models leave large gaps on the hardest tiers, which lets it act as a scaling probe for coding-agent capability.
   - Theme: (1) core LLM advances — agentic-system evaluation
   - Relevance: Executable, performance-graded infra tasks are a much stronger signal than yet another Q&A leaderboard, and dovetails with theme (3) on correctness verification.
   - Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10226

4. **SWE-Bench Pro Verified: A Reliable Benchmark for Software Engineering Agents**
   - Authors: Pujun Zheng, Zixin Shang, Shufan Jiang, et al.
   - Summary: Re-audits SWE-Bench Pro tasks to catch benchmark-hacking paths (e.g., trivial patches that pass hidden tests without solving the issue) and refines task specs so verification is faithful to the original bug. On the cleaned split, several agents whose original scores looked strong drop substantially, quantifying how much of prior "SWE-agent" progress was benchmark contamination or under-specified oracles.
   - Theme: (1) agentic-system evaluation / (3) formal correctness of agent outputs for coding
   - Relevance: Directly relevant to the correctness-of-coding-agents theme; sets a better bar for anyone measuring coding agents.
   - Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08149

5. **DRG-MAPPO: Hierarchical Dynamic Role-Graph Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning for Cooperative Air Combat**
   - Authors: Junlin Liu, Chengwei Li, Yang Gao, et al.
   - Summary: Layers a graph-attention role assigner on top of MAPPO so agents' roles (and therefore their observation/action masks and reward shaping) can change during an episode, driven by a learned role graph over teammates. In a cooperative air-combat testbed the dynamic-role scheme outperforms fixed-role MAPPO baselines and shows better transfer when team composition changes.
   - Theme: (2) multi-agent frameworks & orchestration
   - Relevance: A concrete architecture for role/tool assignment in cooperative multi-agent systems — abstracts cleanly to LLM-agent teams where roles must be reassigned mid-task.
   - Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11155

6. **An Open Recipe for IMO Gold: Training Nemotron for Olympiad Mathematics**
   - Authors: Ivan Moshkov, Stephen Ge, George Armstrong, et al.
   - Summary: Describes a natural-language proof-generation pipeline built on Nemotron that iterates draft-then-verify with a checker over generated proofs, and reports IMO-2026-gold-level results on Olympiad problems. The recipe emphasizes verifier-guided rejection sampling and a curated training mix rather than a monolithic new pretraining pass, i.e. formal-methods-style spec-checking wired into the training loop.
   - Theme: (1) core LLM reasoning / (3) verification of generated artifacts
   - Relevance: Verifier-in-the-loop for structured natural-language artifacts is the same shape as spec-checking for code, which is why it lands in the digest despite being math-flavored.
   - Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10712

7. **Show-Harness: Just a VLM Agent Can Play Robots**
   - Authors: Yanzhe Chen, Zechen Bai, Zhijun Cao, et al.
   - Summary: An "embodied harness" wraps a vision-language model with a small, discrete semantic-action vocabulary and thin per-embodiment adapters, so the VLM emits high-level actions the harness translates into low-level control. Both zero-shot and lightly fine-tuned deployments transfer across different robot embodiments, arguing the harness (not the VLM itself) is the right integration surface for generalist agents.
   - Theme: (2) multi-agent frameworks & orchestration (agent architectures)
   - Relevance: Fits the "agent architecture / tool assignment" slot of theme 2 with a clean separation of concerns between policy (LLM) and environment adapter.
   - Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10522

8. **Recursive Code World Models: Building Complex Worlds through Recursive Scene Programs**
   - Authors: Zhiqi Li, Yuxuan Liao, Bo Zhu
   - Summary: Uses a vision-language agent to reconstruct a 3D scene from a single image as an executable program that recursively expands sub-scenes on demand, so the "world model" is code rather than latent tensors. Because the program is executable, its outputs can be re-rendered, diffed, and unit-tested against the original image — a spec-checkable representation for perception.
   - Theme: (2) agent architectures / (3) spec-driven, executable representations
   - Relevance: Executable scene programs give a concrete example of "specification as code" for a perception agent — a bridge from theme 2 to theme 3.
   - Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11499

9. **Programmable World Model**
   - Authors: Zheng-Hui Huang, Guixu Lin, Jiacheng Lin, et al.
   - Summary: Splits a video/world model into a symbolic state simulator (executable programs plus explicit 3D bounding boxes) and a visual generator that only has to render each frame conditional on that state, giving persistent, controllable long-horizon rollouts. The symbolic layer acts as the spec — you can inspect, edit, and constrain state transitions directly instead of hoping the video model stays consistent.
   - Theme: (3) spec-driven development (executable programs as ground truth)
   - Relevance: One of the clearer recent instances of "make the spec executable, keep the neural net for rendering" — an architectural pattern worth borrowing for coding agents.
   - Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10540

10. **NCP-ArchPreview Technical Report: Moving towards Latent Space Language Models through Next Concept Prediction**
    - Authors: NCP Team, Jiaqi Cao, Chiyu Chen, et al.
    - Summary: Introduces a language-model architecture that mixes standard next-token prediction with next-concept prediction over a product-quantized concept vocabulary learned in latent space, so the model plans one abstraction level above tokens during pretraining. The technical report claims this yields more efficient pretraining and better downstream reasoning than an OLMo-3-7B-scale baseline trained on comparable data.
    - Theme: (1) core LLM advances (architecture / reasoning)
    - Relevance: Latent-concept planning is a live research direction for LLM reasoning; a public architecture report at ~7B scale is a rare data point.
    - Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10715

## 1. SWE-MISBEHAVIOR: Benchmarking and Mitigating Boundary Violations in Software Coding Agents
- Authors: Do Xuan Trong, Duc Anh Nguyen, Do Xuan Long, et al.
- Theme: (3) Spec-driven / formal verification for coding agents
- Summary: Proposes a taxonomy and automated benchmark (~7.4K samples) of 11 boundary-violating behaviors in coding agents — unauthorized file writes, scope-creeping edits, exfiltration-like tool calls — and treats each as a violation of an explicit behavioral spec rather than an unobserved failure. The authors evaluate open-source agents against the taxonomy and introduce CODE-KIT, a mitigation framework that reportedly cuts misbehavior rates by 52% for smolagents and 34% for OpenHands while improving overall task completion. The framing is directly spec-driven: "correctness" of a coding agent is defined as adherence to a declared boundary rulebook, not just end-task success.
- Relevance: Closest fit on the whole list to Theme (3); gives a reusable rulebook-and-measurement scaffold for agent-correctness specs in coding workflows.
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18206

## 2. ReTerm: Recovery Training for Self-Improving Terminal Agents
- Authors: Wei Xie, Kaituo Feng, Shiming Fu, et al.
- Theme: (3) Spec-driven / formal verification for coding agents
- Summary: Trains terminal/shell coding agents to recover from their own failures by combining a cold-start SFT corpus of recovery trajectories with an online RL loop that forks fresh rollouts from previously-failed states, instead of discarding the failure as a dead sample. The method targets the specific class of "command-level mis-step then stuck" errors that plague coding agents and reports measurable gains over standard GRPO-style RL on terminal task completion. The failure/recovery framing is a step toward specifying what "correct behavior" looks like after a wrong intermediate action.
- Relevance: Second Theme-3 pick — complements SWE-MISBEHAVIOR by focusing on *post-violation* correctness rather than ex-ante boundaries.
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18484

## 3. WorkRover: Scaling Long-Horizon Cowork Agent Training Via Recursive Environment Evolution
- Authors: Hang He, Linting Wang, Guanming Liu, et al.
- Theme: (2) Multi-agent frameworks & orchestration
- Summary: Introduces a recursive environment-evolution framework for training "cowork" agents that collaborate across long horizons, iteratively generating, validating, and repairing aligned work contexts, tasks, and verifiers so that environment complexity scales with the agent's capability. Credit is assigned per-turn at a finer granularity than prior cowork pipelines, and the authors report improvements on long-horizon multi-agent benchmarks over flat-training baselines. The core contribution is the closed loop between task generator, verifier, and policy — effectively an orchestration schema that keeps supervision aligned as agents improve.
- Relevance: Clean fit on Theme (2): a concrete orchestration/training protocol for collaborating agents.
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18067

## 4. Tev: Scaling Long-Horizon Agentic Environments with Dense Progress Rewards
- Authors: Wenbo Hu, Shraman Pramanick, Krishna Kumar Singh, et al.
- Theme: (2) Multi-agent frameworks & orchestration (also agentic eval)
- Summary: Releases 1,170 verified long-horizon agentic task environments annotated with per-step progress metrics, then converts those metrics into dense per-step rewards for RL training of a 27B agent. The resulting agent scores competitively across several agentic benchmarks, demonstrating that dense progress signals derived from verifiers can substitute for sparse end-of-episode rewards in multi-step tool-use training. The combined environment-plus-reward design is proposed as a reusable backbone for scaling agent training.
- Relevance: Theme (2) by way of orchestration infrastructure; the verifier-driven reward is also a Theme-3-adjacent spec signal.
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18868

## 5. DeltaTTG: Training Multi-Turn Agents with a Token-to-Go Progress Meter
- Authors: Xuyan Ye, Xiyan Fu, Shengda Fan, et al.
- Theme: (2) Multi-agent frameworks & orchestration
- Summary: Trains a learned "tokens-to-go" progress estimator alongside a multi-turn agent, predicting how many tokens remain until task completion, and uses that estimate to shape dense per-step rewards during RL. The redistribution of sparse end-of-task rewards across intermediate turns gives sharper credit assignment and reportedly accelerates convergence on tool-use and planning benchmarks. The progress meter is itself an orchestration primitive — a shared time-to-completion signal that other components can read.
- Relevance: A credit-assignment primitive directly usable in agent orchestration frameworks.
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17666

## 6. JevGPT: Thinking Fast and Slow in Autonomous Research
- Authors: Yasheng Sun, Yanning Dai, Yimeng Chen, et al.
- Theme: (2) Multi-agent frameworks & orchestration
- Summary: Proposes a dual-process research-agent architecture in which a slow, deliberative planner sets evidence-gathering goals while a parallel fast component judges candidate sources in-flight. The split reconciles Kahneman-style System-1/System-2 reasoning inside an autonomous research pipeline and reports leading accuracy on question answering and document ranking at lower cost than monolithic single-loop baselines. The paper offers a concrete two-role orchestration pattern worth stealing.
- Relevance: Theme (2) via explicit role assignment and inter-component protocol.
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18272

## 7. LifeSearch: Benchmarking Deep Research Agents for Real-World Life and Productivity
- Authors: Yibo Wang, Senjie Jin, Yansong Ning, et al.
- Theme: (1) Core LLM — agentic-system evaluation
- Summary: Builds a bilingual benchmark of 203 real-user "deep research" tasks drawn from seven life and productivity domains, each graded by over 4,000 expert rubrics covering process and outcome. Twelve commercial and open deep-research agents are tested; the strongest reaches only 63.4%, and the rubric-based grading correlates strongly with human expert rankings. The resulting yardstick gives the community a reproducible, process-aware way to benchmark agentic-system quality on messy real-world queries.
- Relevance: Clean Theme (1) agentic-eval contribution with transferable rubric methodology.
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18092

## 8. LongR: Unleashing Long-Context Reasoning via Reinforcement Learning with Dense Utility Rewards
- Authors: Qize Yu, Zijun Chen, Chen Wu, et al.
- Theme: (1) Core LLM — reasoning
- Summary: An RL framework for long-context reasoning that interleaves generation with document retrieval and rewards the agent based on the information gain of each retrieved chunk, eliminating the need for annotated evidence spans. The dense utility reward is algorithm-agnostic and reportedly compatible with multiple on-policy RL algorithms, improving long-context benchmarks over end-reward-only training.
- Relevance: Theme (1) — reasoning + fine-tuning with a reusable reward-shaping idea.
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.05758

## 9. Ares: Spectral-Driven Adaptive-Rank Optimizer for LLM RLVR
- Authors: Zhipeng Chen, Wayne Xin Zhao, Ji-Rong Wen, et al.
- Theme: (1) Core LLM — fine-tuning / PEFT
- Summary: Proposes an optimizer for RL with verifiable rewards (RLVR) that estimates the effective update rank from the gradient's spectral energy and adjusts the applied rank adaptively through training. Across model scales and RLVR algorithms, Ares improves accuracy over Adam/AdamW baselines while matching or reducing per-step compute. The spectral-rank trick is a clean, drop-in alternative to fixed low-rank adaptations for RL-stage fine-tuning.
- Relevance: Theme (1) — a tool for the RL fine-tuning stage of reasoning models.
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16533

## 10. ReVeR: Rubrics from the Task, Verdicts from Evidence — Pointwise Rubric Rewards with Tool-Augmented Judges
- Authors: Tianci Liu, Haoyu Wang
- Theme: (1) Core LLM — reasoning / reward modeling
- Summary: Introduces a pointwise reward model that derives task-specific rubrics directly from the user instruction (rather than pairwise preference labels) and scores each candidate response independently using tool-augmented judges. Trained via label-blind distillation, ReVeR beats pairwise rubric reward models on preference-accuracy benchmarks, with the largest gains on instruction-following. The pointwise, rubric-first design is a step toward spec-shaped reward signals for RL fine-tuning.
- Relevance: Theme (1) — reward modeling that also informs how to specify desired agent behavior.
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18766

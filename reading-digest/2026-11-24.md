## 1. From Rewarding What Models Say to How They Think: Attention Process Reward Model for LLM Reinforcement Learning
- **Authors:** Kairong Han, Nuanqiao Shan, Ziyu Zhao, et al.
- **Summary:** Points out that existing reward models only score surface text and therefore collapse different internal reasoning paths that produce the same final string ("textual reward aliasing"). The authors train a ~46M-parameter Attention Process Reward Model (APRM) that reads hidden states and attention maps from the generator, scoring reasoning quality step by step, and plug it into standard RL pipelines. Reports an average pass@8 gain of about 2.88 points across reasoning benchmarks while remaining tiny enough to deploy alongside the base policy.
- **Theme:** Core LLM advances (reasoning / RL reward modeling)
- **Relevance:** Shifts the reward signal from output text to the model's internal reasoning — directly relevant to fine-tuning and reasoning evaluation for agents.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30627

## 2. From Local Errors to System Failures: Modeling and Benchmarking Error Propagation in Multi-Agent LLM Systems
- **Authors:** Wenlong Yu, Bingxu Lu, Qilong Wang, et al.
- **Summary:** Proposes REPA, a structural model that decomposes each agent in a multi-agent workflow into an input-assembly stage and an agent-processing stage, so that locally measured error rates can be composed into a prediction of system-level failure. Releases ErrPropBench-10 (three Qwen bases × six MAS workflows × ten reasoning/knowledge settings) and shows REPA reaches a mean condition-level Spearman correlation of 0.84 vs. 0.47 for direct regression, enabling targeted interventions on the weakest hop.
- **Theme:** Multi-agent frameworks & orchestration (evaluation of coordination)
- **Relevance:** Provides a principled, benchmarked way to attribute multi-agent failures to specific channels — concrete tool for designing orchestrators that are correct by construction.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31063

## 3. AEP: Towards Trustworthy Agent Communication, Networking, and Collaboration
- **Authors:** Yuhao Wang, Shengfang Zhai, Xinyu Zhou, et al.
- **Summary:** Argues that current A2A protocols and shared-memory buses assume collaborators are known and mutually trusted, which breaks in open networks of privately owned agents. Introduces OPAC, an asset-centric collaboration paradigm where agents discover, exchange, audit, and integrate policy-controlled "context assets" rather than transient messages, with ownership-aware access control and auditability. Treats collaboration as a reusable, verifiable exchange of context rather than a free-form chat.
- **Theme:** Multi-agent frameworks & orchestration (communication protocols)
- **Relevance:** A concrete inter-agent communication protocol with policy enforcement — directly on theme for multi-agent coordination research.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29598

## 4. MetaSkill: Reusable Problem-Solving Methods for Adaptive Skill Orchestration in LLM Agents
- **Authors:** Tianxing Wang, Mingming Zhao, Shuai Huang, et al.
- **Summary:** Separates agent problem-solving into a method layer (26 curated reusable methods with abstract roles bound at runtime to task objects) and an execution layer that uses the instantiated method to drive intermediate goals, orchestrate native skills, and trigger repair or method reselection. On SkillsBench (89 tasks) and ToolHop (995 examples), it improves over the strongest baseline by 3.51–13.48% across six configurations, with ablations showing gains come from explicit method guidance rather than mere method prompting.
- **Theme:** Multi-agent frameworks & orchestration (role/tool assignment, skill orchestration)
- **Relevance:** Explicit two-layer orchestration architecture — exactly the kind of agent-architecture pattern worth tracking.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29274

## 5. VADE: A Verifiable Agent Data Engine from Professional Sources
- **Authors:** Xiangchao Chen, Zhiheng Lyu, Kai Zhang, et al.
- **Summary:** Targets the bottleneck where high-quality training tasks for agents outside software engineering are expert-authored and don't scale, because open-ended analysis tasks lack executable checks. Builds VADE around the asymmetry that professional analyses are hard to produce but much easier to verify when the authoritative source is available, automatically constructing tasks with machine-checkable verification from public professional corpora. Positions the pipeline as a scalable supervision generator for law, finance, and similar domains.
- **Theme:** Spec-driven development & formal verification (verifiable task specifications for agents)
- **Relevance:** Takes the executable-verification idea from coding agents and generalizes it — a template for scaling spec-driven correctness to new domains.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30202

## 6. Edit-Induced Dependency Mismatch: Propagation Omissions, Recovery, and Evaluation Blind Spots in Coding Agents
- **Authors:** Fei Ding
- **Summary:** Audits full execution trajectories from DeepSeek-v4-flash on DeepSWE and SWE-bench Verified (plus Qwen3.8-Flash on FeatureBench) and shows that coding agents often keep stale textual dependency judgments in context across edits, producing silent consumer-side defects that the original benchmark tests do not catch. Confirms every retained DeepSWE terminal event with supplementary execution checks, and documents cases where an agent recovers mid-trajectory while still leaving a terminal omission.
- **Theme:** Spec-driven development & formal verification (correctness of coding-agent outputs)
- **Relevance:** Hard evidence that current coding-agent evaluations miss real correctness bugs introduced by edits — motivates stronger specs/tests in benchmarks.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29242

## 7. What Makes Good Data for Training Coding Agents? A Systematic Study on SWE-Gym
- **Authors:** Yikai Zhang, Mai Xu, Yunbei Zhang, et al.
- **Summary:** Audits SWE-Gym with two LLM families and finds that "unsolved" tasks are heavily contaminated with mislabelled rewards: 74% of tasks a 9B base model never solves have a test that rejects correct fixes, versus 20% for sometimes-solved tasks. After repairing or removing these tasks the retained training set yields policies 2.4 and 2.7 points higher on SWE-bench Verified at equal size and matched difficulty, and the cleaned set is released as SWE-Gym-Verified.
- **Theme:** Spec-driven development & formal verification (correctness of coding-agent rewards)
- **Relevance:** Shows that the "specs" (hidden tests) behind popular coding-agent RL data are themselves wrong — a prerequisite for any correctness guarantee downstream.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30207

## 8. Generating Safety-Critical Driving Scenarios from Role-Typed Signal Temporal Logic Specifications Drafted by Large Language Models
- **Authors:** Zifan Zeng, Qunli Zhang, Peng Wang, et al.
- **Summary:** Represents each driving scenario as a signal-temporal-logic specification whose clauses are typed by role — each clause declares which agent it may change and which it may read, with the vehicle under test only allowed to read the attacker after attack onset. An LLM drafts the STL from a natural-language description, a type checker rejects malformed drafts, and a compiler turns the spec into the smallest diffusion-model edit that satisfies every clause without letting the ego cancel or attract the attack.
- **Theme:** Spec-driven development & formal verification (STL-typed specs drafted by LLMs)
- **Relevance:** Concrete pipeline where an LLM writes formal specs that a type checker and compiler then enforce — a crisp example of spec-driven development for agent behavior.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29800

## 9. Beyond Self-Consistency: Recursive Self-Improvement at Test Time
- **Authors:** Jianyu Wu, Yizhou Wang, Encheng Su, et al.
- **Summary:** Argues that self-consistency wastes earlier reasoning and can lock in a wrong majority. Proposes TT-RSI, a label-free test-time framework with two components: In-Context RSI re-solves each question using the previous full solution as a reference, and a complementary self-training loop updates the model from its own carried-forward reasoning rather than only final answers. Reports consistent gains over self-consistency on reasoning benchmarks.
- **Theme:** Core LLM advances (test-time reasoning, self-improvement)
- **Relevance:** A concrete test-time recipe that builds on earlier attempts instead of voting over independent ones — relevant to agentic reasoning loops.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30965

## 10. Wactorz: Self-Writing LLM Agents That Discover, Repair, and Migrate at the Edge
- **Authors:** Panagiotis Kasnesis, Lazaros Toumanidis, Amalia Contiero Syropoulou, Christos Chatzigeorgiou
- **Summary:** An open-source runtime where a planner compiles chat requests into Python, instantiates them as long-lived supervised "actors," and uses MQTT as the coordination fabric so that every internal event is a topic external systems can subscribe to. A supervisor restarts crashed actors alone with state intact, and actors can be live-migrated between edge nodes such as a Raspberry Pi, giving agents months-long lifetimes and in-place recovery on the same hardware as the sensors.
- **Theme:** Multi-agent frameworks & orchestration (agent architecture, communication fabric)
- **Relevance:** Treats agents as supervised long-running actors over a pub/sub bus — a practical multi-agent architecture pattern worth studying.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29619

## 1. AgentWorld: Benchmarking Long-Horizon Collaboration of Multi-agent LLMs
- **Authors:** Raphael Shu, Yusen Zhang, Young Min Cho, et al.
- **Summary:** The authors build an MMORPG sandbox with 100 human-annotated
  long-horizon tasks that force multiple LLM agents to coordinate, communicate,
  and hand off subgoals. They introduce a Causal Collaboration Effectiveness
  metric that traces action dependencies across agents rather than just scoring
  final outcomes. Even the strongest frontier model reaches only 52% task
  success, with failure modes dominated by communication breakdowns, role
  confusion, and inability to keep a shared plan.
- **Theme:** (2) Multi-agent frameworks & orchestration
- **Relevance:** Direct evaluation of multi-agent coordination in a
  long-horizon setting, with a coordination-specific metric rather than only
  end-task accuracy.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31590

## 2. Game Arena: Strategic LLM Evaluation in Competitive Environments
- **Authors:** Bovard Doerschuk-Tiberi, Yao Yan, Justin Chiu, et al.
- **Summary:** The paper introduces a Kaggle-hosted head-to-head arena that
  pits LLMs against each other in Chess, Poker, and Werewolf, spanning perfect
  information, imperfect information, and multiplayer social deduction. Because
  rankings are derived from live matchups rather than static test sets, the
  benchmark resists saturation and continues to differentiate models as
  capabilities improve. The authors report emergent strategic differences
  (planning depth, adaptation, robustness to bluffing) that static QA
  benchmarks miss.
- **Theme:** (1) Core LLM advances — agentic-system evaluation
- **Relevance:** A first-party evaluation framework from a well-known lab
  targeting exactly the "how do we score agents" gap the theme cares about.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31473

## 3. Learning to Stop without Learning to Stop: Self-Supervised Confidence Training Improves Reasoning Efficiency
- **Authors:** Parsa Hosseini, Akasha Tigalappanavara, Sumit Nawathe, et al.
- **Summary:** The method adds a self-supervised confidence target during
  training but never optimizes for length or an explicit stopping rule.
  Empirically, models trained this way learn to stop generating once their
  internal confidence is high enough, cutting reasoning tokens by up to 25%
  across several backbones. The result argues that reasoning-length control
  can be a byproduct of calibration rather than a separately engineered
  objective.
- **Theme:** (1) Core LLM advances — reasoning / fine-tuning
- **Relevance:** A cheap, orthogonal training signal that shortens
  chain-of-thought without a length reward — practical for reasoning-agent
  deployment.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31619

## 4. SLCA-GRPO: Resolving Cross-Segment Credit Misattribution in Tool-Calling RL
- **Authors:** Yan Zhan, Shaobo Liu, Qiunan Liu, et al.
- **Summary:** The authors argue that vanilla GRPO on tool-calling agents
  mis-attributes reward across "reason", "tool-call", and "summary" segments,
  drowning fine-grained tool signals in coarse trajectory returns. SLCA-GRPO
  splits the trajectory into structural segments and routes execution-level
  advantages to tool tokens while routing preference-level advantages to
  summary tokens. The result is faster convergence and higher function-calling
  accuracy on the tested benchmarks.
- **Theme:** (1) Core LLM advances — fine-tuning for agentic systems
- **Relevance:** A segment-aware RL fix specifically for tool-using agents,
  applicable to any RLHF-style pipeline that trains an agent policy over
  structured turns.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29050

## 5. Multi-agent Scaling Across Disjunctive and Compensatory Tasks
- **Authors:** Carolina Fortuna, Blaz Bertalanic
- **Summary:** The paper studies how team performance scales with agent count
  under two task structures: disjunctive (one member's answer wins) and
  compensatory (member answers combine). They show the aggregation mechanism
  is as decisive as raw model capability — some tasks stop improving quickly
  as agents are added, others continue to gain, and the pattern is predictable
  from the task type. Practical takeaway: pick your aggregator based on task
  structure before scaling agent count.
- **Theme:** (2) Multi-agent frameworks & orchestration
- **Relevance:** Concrete, minimalist framework connecting task structure to
  team size — useful when deciding how to route sub-tasks in an orchestration
  layer.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31563

## 6. Analyzing and Mitigating Cost-Inefficient Behaviors in Coding Agents
- **Authors:** Yi Hu, Nan Jiang, Sheng Liang, et al.
- **Summary:** The authors classify three recurring cost-inefficient patterns
  (redundant exploration, over-reading files, and re-planning loops) that
  affect 79–98% of coding-agent tasks in their traces. They compare
  developer-designed "skills" (tight, human-written sub-procedures) against
  agent-synthesized ones and find the human-written skills cut cost by up to
  41.73%, roughly twice the savings of the auto-generated ones. The study
  gives an evaluation lens plus a concrete intervention for expensive coding
  agents.
- **Theme:** (1) Core LLM advances — agentic-system evaluation (coding)
- **Relevance:** Bridges themes 1 and 3: rigorous evaluation of a coding-agent
  loop plus a concrete way to reduce spend before scaling.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30725

## 7. Beyond Approved Actions: Runtime Validation of Persistent Outcomes in Agent Workflows
- **Authors:** Han Zhang, Hui Zhang, Zejun Liang, et al.
- **Summary:** EffectMatch reframes agent safety around persistent effects
  rather than tool-call approvals: it watches which artifacts actually change
  during execution and refuses to commit outcomes that don't match an
  approved effect spec. This blocks classes of failure where an LLM invokes
  the right tool with the wrong side effect (e.g. writing to an unintended
  file). The runtime is designed to sit between the agent and downstream
  systems as an effect-gate.
- **Theme:** (3) Spec-driven development & formal verification for coding
- **Relevance:** A concrete correctness specification (persistent-effect
  match) enforced at runtime for LLM coding/workflow agents — squarely on the
  spec-driven theme.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31301

## 8. Towards Mitigating Fabricated Consensus: The Active Provenance Gate for Multi-Agent Debate Synthesis
- **Authors:** Jakub Masłowski, Jarosław A. Chudziak
- **Summary:** Multi-agent debate pipelines routinely collapse into a
  fabricated consensus when the synthesizer can't tell which claims are
  actually grounded. The authors add an Active Provenance Gate that
  post-processes the debate transcript, attributing each surviving claim back
  to a supporting agent turn and rejecting unsourced ones. On difficult
  scenarios the gate more than doubles average data-provenance fidelity
  versus the un-gated synthesizer.
- **Theme:** (2) Multi-agent frameworks & orchestration
- **Relevance:** Directly addresses a well-known failure of debate-style
  orchestration; the gate is a reusable component that other multi-agent
  frameworks could bolt on.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31422

## 9. NEUROTESTGEN: Neuro-Symbolic Guided Test Generation with Large Language Models
- **Authors:** Ruihao Zhang, Jaechang Shin, Hung Viet Pham, Song Wang
- **Summary:** The system pairs symbolic execution with an LLM synthesiser:
  Z3 constraints derived from the target program guide the LLM's next test
  case, and the LLM's proposals are checked back against those constraints
  before being accepted. Across Llama-3.3-70B, GPT-4o Mini, and Claude the
  hybrid pipeline beats each model's standalone test generator. The design
  is a clean template for combining formal solvers with LLM code synthesis
  for correctness-relevant outputs.
- **Theme:** (3) Spec-driven development & formal verification for coding
- **Relevance:** Uses formal constraints (Z3) to shape LLM-generated tests —
  exactly the "formal methods defining correctness of coding-agent outputs"
  angle in the theme.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30178

## 10. Formal Model Construction Guided by Model-Based Proof Sketches
- **Authors:** Hao Wang, Xiaoshan Zuo, Yang Cai, Neeraj K. Singh, Yamine A. Ameur, Jin Song Dong
- **Summary:** ProGS asks an LLM to first draft a hierarchical proof sketch
  of the intended system properties, then uses that sketch as a scaffold when
  generating the actual formal model (e.g., Event-B). Compared to prior
  end-to-end agentic approaches the pipeline improves syntactic validity,
  deductive verifiability, and behavioural correctness of the resulting
  models. It's a concrete pattern for making LLM-generated formal artifacts
  actually check.
- **Theme:** (3) Spec-driven development & formal verification for coding
- **Relevance:** Directly explores the "spec first, code/model second"
  workflow with LLMs — a template for correctness-oriented code and model
  generation.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29870

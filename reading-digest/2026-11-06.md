## 1. What Does Multi-Turn Reinforcement Learning Really Improve in LLM Agents?
- **Authors:** Kejian Zhu, Zhuoran Jin, Shangqing Tu, et al.
- **Summary:** The authors introduce a Pass@(K,T) axis that varies sampling breadth K and interaction depth T to decompose where multi-turn agentic RL actually helps. They show the gains from trajectory-level RL concentrate on error recovery and long-horizon resilience rather than single-step decision quality, and collapse when the turn budget shrinks. To stabilize deployment, they propose BudgetMix RL, which randomizes interaction budgets during training, and show it holds up across execution settings that would otherwise expose the budget-dependent RL advantage.
- **Theme:** Core LLM advances (agentic-system evaluation / fine-tuning)
- **Relevance:** Reframes the dominant agent-RL recipe around when it actually delivers, and fixes a concrete deployment pathology — exactly the kind of agentic evaluation signal the theme calls for.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06483

## 2. FrontierPhysics: Evaluating Agents for End-to-End Physics Research
- **Authors:** Bingran You, Wenjun Ke, Ria Rosenauer, et al.
- **Summary:** FrontierPhysics turns 42 physicists' real research into 56 end-to-end agent tasks that demand literature review, planning, experimentation, and a structured manuscript, scored by deterministic verifiers combined with expert-authored rubrics. Across 22 models in 26 configurations no run passes more than 46 of its 168 rollouts, and manual trace review surfaces where current agents break down on method selection and reporting. This gives one of the first benchmarks that checks whether an agent's research is both correct and publishable, not just whether its final answer matches.
- **Theme:** Core LLM advances (agentic-system evaluation)
- **Relevance:** Rubric-plus-verifier scaffolding for real research workflows is a sharp new instrument for agentic evaluation beyond paper-reproduction harnesses.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06910

## 3. Towards Foundation Decision Models: Drex
- **Authors:** Nischay Dhankhar, Alisher Amantay, Zhanibek Datbayev, et al.
- **Summary:** Drex is a family of four open "decision models" (8B/9B/35B-MoE/27B) that take a state plus multiple-choice, yes/no, or scored questions and return a probability distribution without autoregressive decoding, trained with SFT followed by RL on verifiable judgments. The authors argue this removes the output-parsing, latency, and inconsistency overheads of CoT-style LLMs for agent-decision calls while retaining calibrated confidence. Benchmarks report competitive accuracy against long-CoT baselines at a fraction of the inference cost, with a usable scoring interface for agent controllers.
- **Theme:** Core LLM advances (reasoning / planning / fine-tuning)
- **Relevance:** A concrete, open alternative to autoregressive reasoning for the discrete choices agents actually make — directly on the "planning + fine-tuning" axis of the core-LLM theme.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07854

## 4. PyRUA-REAL: Robot Exploration and Adaptive Learning through RSI in the Real World
- **Authors:** Qiang Wang, Jianxin Bi, Shulong Jiang, et al.
- **Summary:** PyRUA-REAL frames embodied recursive self-improvement as a loop in which coding agents explore the physical world, retain interaction experience as persistent memory, and distill reusable executable skills that later refinement calls on. The empirical study dissects how the agents coordinate, what kinds of execution feedback drive effective code edits, and how task specifications interact with the inherited skill library on robustness and transfer. The framework is explicitly set up so multiple coding agents and skill modules collaborate under a shared specification contract.
- **Theme:** Multi-agent frameworks & orchestration
- **Relevance:** Rare example of a multi-agent coding-agent orchestration driven by durable task specifications and reusable skills — spans multi-agent orchestration and the spec-driven-coding corner.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06768

## 5. SleuthVLM: Do We Really Need Audio-in-Video for Omni Video Understanding?
- **Authors:** Peiran Wu, Fangfei Gou, Zhaoxi Mu, et al.
- **Summary:** SleuthVLM wraps a plain VLM in an agent harness that never consumes audio directly: speech is transcribed to text and the VLM investigates the video with no fixed workflow, deciding what to look at and when. On four omni benchmarks with videos up to 90 minutes it posts the best average accuracy (75.0%) and tops Video-MME-v2 at 77.8%, outperforming native omni models including Gemini 3.8 Flash. The headline conclusion is that the orchestration layer, not direct audio tokens, is the dominant lever for long-video understanding.
- **Theme:** Multi-agent frameworks & orchestration
- **Relevance:** Treats agentic capability itself as the primary intervention over architecture and training-data scale — a crisp orchestration-first datapoint for the multi-agent theme.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06083

## 6. SAE Interprets MoE: Sparse Autoencoders Reveal That MoE Experts Exhibit Human-Interpretable Fine-Grained Specialization
- **Authors:** Anyuan Zhuo, Shaohua Zhang, Yuan Lin, et al.
- **Summary:** The paper trains a sparse autoencoder on the router-weighted output of a Qwen3-30B-A3B MoE layer and uses the affine encoder to decompose every latent's pre-activation exactly over the experts, moving beyond selection statistics to contribution. Across layers 4, 30, and 46 contribution is far more concentrated than gating, most latents are driven by one or two experts, and overlap across experts stays small. The result turns "what does each expert do" into an auditable mapping from experts to interpretable feature clusters.
- **Theme:** Core LLM advances (reasoning / interpretability for fine-tuning)
- **Relevance:** Provides a principled read-off from gating to actual expert function — relevant for routing-aware PEFT and reasoning-path analysis in MoE LLMs.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08089

## 7. Turns Matter: Advancing Streaming Video RL via Turn-Level Credit
- **Authors:** Shuai Dong, Kanghui Tian, Linghan Chen, et al.
- **Summary:** TurnWise replaces trajectory-level GRPO advantages with per-turn credit built from silence-completed prefix scoring, so each streaming decision learns from its own remaining return rather than a shared trajectory signal. This separates reinforcement from redundant replies after early wins and discourages giving up after an early failure, both common pathologies when a single advantage covers every turn. The method improves responsiveness and informativeness on streaming video benchmarks without changing reward design.
- **Theme:** Core LLM advances (fine-tuning / agentic RL)
- **Relevance:** A clean, general fix for a well-known trajectory-level credit failure mode in agentic GRPO — transferable beyond video to any multi-turn agent RL loop.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06728

## 8. Decode Before You Hear: Speculative Listening for Chunk-Interleaved Full-Duplex Speech Language Models
- **Authors:** Yutong Bian, Yanglin Zhang, Yuxiang Wang, et al.
- **Summary:** Measuring across four full-duplex speech LMs, the authors find that assuming silence for the next audio chunk leaves 95.3% of chunk outputs unchanged and that 88% of changes begin at the first output token. They exploit this with Speculative Listening, a training-free, lossless input-time self-speculative decoding scheme that consumes idle listen-chunk compute to decode ahead and then validates once the real audio lands. The result is a drop-in latency win for interactive speech agents without retraining or quality loss.
- **Theme:** Core LLM advances (efficient decoding / fine-tuning-free improvement)
- **Relevance:** Shows a concrete empirical regularity — chunk-to-chunk output stability — turned into a deployment-ready decoding pattern for real-time agent interfaces.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06982

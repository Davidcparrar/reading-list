1. **DAEDALUS: Bootstrapping Agent Memory from Self-Generated Tasks**
   Authors: Antoine Edy, Max Conti, Victor Xing, et al.
   Summary: Two cooperating agents — an *explorer* that proposes tasks in a new environment and a *solver* that attempts them — are used to build a reusable procedural-memory store without oracle verifiers or seed training data. Successes are canonicalised into skills that the solver later retrieves, and the loop converges on operational knowledge the base model lacked. Reported gains reach up to +15.9 points of success rate across several agent benchmarks, with no weight updates to the underlying LLM.
   Theme: (2) multi-agent frameworks & orchestration
   Relevance: Clean example of role-split orchestration (proposer/solver) with a shared memory artefact, directly on-brief for the multi-agent theme.
   Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08048

2. **MiniCorp: The Last Mile of the AI Agent Firm**
   Authors: Jingying Zeng, Zhenwei Dai, Jinning Li, et al.
   Summary: An office-simulator testbed in which many role-specialised LLM agents jointly run a (toy) e-commerce company against an external market model, generating longitudinal records of communications, decisions, and outcomes. Checkpointing lets the same situation be replayed under different decisions, so the trace serves as both a training corpus and a counterfactual benchmark. Experiments show the agents coordinating across roles, adapting to market feedback, and sustaining advertising exploration when given explicit long-horizon guidance.
   Theme: (2) multi-agent frameworks & orchestration
   Relevance: Multi-agent coordination with communication protocols and a replayable environment — useful both as a framework and as a source of agent-eval data.
   Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.05912

3. **Harness Engineering for Software Engineering via Modular Executable Dev-Primitives (HERMES)**
   Authors: Haibo Jin, Xinjie Li, Peng Kuang, Haohan Wang
   Summary: Proposes *Dev-Primitives*, modular executable abstractions that turn each repository component (files, configs, tests, deps) into an active sub-agent that reasons locally and communicates with neighbours, replacing the usual flat "shell + whole repo in context" harness. HERMES wires these primitives together with dependency-aware dynamic activation and a bug-diagnosis loop. Across four SWE benchmarks it beats matched baseline harnesses by 12.4% on average, and a mixed small-primitive / strong-controller configuration recovers near-parity with homogeneous large-model setups at much lower inference cost.
   Theme: (3) spec-driven / formal-verification for coding (as an agent-architecture lever for correctness) with heavy overlap into (2) multi-agent orchestration
   Relevance: Rare recent paper framing coding agents as a *structured* multi-component system rather than one monolithic LLM-in-a-loop — the closest fit to spec-driven coding in this window.
   Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07832

4. **VeriFine: Scaling Verification for Self-Improvement in Embodied Reasoning**
   Authors: Zewei Zhou, Rachel Luo, Yulong Cao, et al.
   Summary: Argues that a *fixed* judge caps both the signal available for RL and the diversity of training examples it surfaces, and instead co-evolves policy and verifier: a rubric-based judge both scores trajectories and is itself refined through *coactive* human feedback. The resulting loop keeps enlarging the verifier's rubric as the policy explores new behaviours, so verification coverage scales with the task distribution rather than lagging it. Experiments show continuous improvement in both policy success and judge agreement over successive rounds.
   Theme: (3) formal / rubric-based verification of agent outputs (adjacent to spec-driven correctness)
   Relevance: Treats verification as a first-class, evolving spec against which agent behaviour is checked — the design is domain-portable to code-agent correctness.
   Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08761

5. **From Evidence to Action: How Tool-Using Agents Fail**
   Authors: Hongzhan Lin, Shidong Cao, Ziyang Luo, et al.
   Summary: Introduces SafeActBench (656 cases, 6 operational domains, 5 protocols) that walks a tool-using agent from static action-judgment up through single actions and multi-action workflows, with a provenance-bound *Evidence Ledger* and a deterministic trajectory evaluator tracking what was established, when, and whether downstream prerequisites held. Across ten model-harness configurations, strong static judgment coexists with much weaker interactive execution: failures mostly start *before* the first write — agents stop with incomplete investigation or act before evidence is in. Once prerequisites are satisfied, single actions are usually reliable; multi-action workflows re-expose the gap through unresolved dependencies.
   Theme: (1) core LLM / agentic-system evaluation, with a strong formal-correctness-of-actions flavour
   Relevance: Explicit, auditable evidence model for *when* an action is justified — directly relevant to specifying correctness of agent outputs.
   Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07753

6. **UNREAL: Unifying Retrieval and Long-Context with a Single Model**
   Authors: Edan Kinderman, Elad Hoffer, Yochai Blau
   Summary: A model-native evidence-selection layer: chunks and queries are both derived from the frozen LLM's own internal representations, with fewer than 500K added trainable parameters and no backbone changes. On a 21M-chunk Wikipedia index it beats SOTA retriever-reranker stacks (e.g. HotpotQA recall 49.1→73.2%, MuSiQue 8.8→14.4%); applied in-context it also prunes distractors, lifting NoLiMa accuracy from 1.0% to 24.83% at 128K tokens and cutting FLOPs/TTFT past ~32K. The same mechanism therefore spans corpus retrieval and long-context inference.
   Theme: (1) core LLM advances (retrieval, long-context inference)
   Relevance: Reframes retrieval as an *internal* capability of the LLM — likely to influence how agentic RAG stacks are built.
   Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08463

7. **TRACE: Rollout-Guided Quantization-Aware Training for FP4 RL of MoE Language Models**
   Authors: Xin Wang, Hao Yu, Zhengyang Zhuge, et al.
   Summary: Prior FP4-RL work quantises the training path and the rollout path independently and then suffers from their mismatch; TRACE instead uses the rollout's actual quantisation outcomes to steer FP4 rounding during training, directly reducing the train-rollout gap, and caches mantissa/scale info only from deeper layers to keep overhead cheap. On four large MoE LMs across reasoning, coding and long-horizon RL tasks, joint FP4 weight/activation + FP4 KV-cache rollout matches BF16-rollout performance while delivering up to 5.4× rollout speed-up.
   Theme: (1) core LLM advances (RL post-training / efficient fine-tuning)
   Relevance: Makes FP4 agentic RL on MoE actually viable without the usual quality cliff.
   Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07767

8. **HuatuoGPT-3: RL-Only Domain Adaptation from Base Models (OnePO)**
   Authors: Junying Chen, Xinyuan Xie, Ziniu Li, et al.
   Summary: Argues SFT+RL is a convenient but exploration-shrinking crutch and pure on-policy RL cold-starts badly, then isolates two failure modes of naive mixed-policy RL — "gradient starvation" on informative low-probability teacher tokens, and "teacher-distribution anchoring" as the policy outgrows a stale teacher. OnePO treats teacher outputs as *transient* guidance, with adaptive-objective evolution and an explicit teacher-retirement step. On medical adaptation it hits HealthBench 67.2 with only 20K samples (beating SFT+RL by 2.7 and pure RL by 7.4), and the 27B variant reaches 70.1 Total / 71.4 Professional.
   Theme: (1) core LLM advances (fine-tuning / RL)
   Relevance: Clean recipe for RL-only domain adaptation — a concrete alternative to the SFT→RL default.
   Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.05966

9. **Rethinking Cross-Tokenizer On-Policy Distillation: From Alignment Coverage to Supervision Reliability**
   Authors: Bingxi Hou, Guochao Jiang, Guofeng Quan, et al.
   Summary: Studies cross-tokenizer on-policy distillation and shows that strict 1:1 token alignment already covers the vast majority of a student's generations, and that restricting reverse-KL to a student-selected top-16 shared-vocabulary subset at strict positions matches full shared-vocabulary OPD and beats evaluated cross-tokenizer baselines. Adding MSE supervision on span log-probs to fill mismatch groups — the obvious "cover everything" move — actually *reduces* accuracy because the extra gradients disagree with the strict ones. Reframes the research priority from maximising alignment coverage to prioritising supervision reliability.
   Theme: (1) core LLM advances (distillation / PEFT-adjacent training)
   Relevance: Counter-intuitive result with direct implications for anyone distilling across model families.
   Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08448

10. **EmbodiedSmith: Scaling Embodied Data through a Recursive Self-Improvement Flywheel in Simulation**
    Authors: Yikai Qin, Yifei Deng, Mingjian Liang, et al.
    Summary: A simulation-side generation pipeline where asset, scene and task generators iteratively refine each other: scene generation anticipates what tasks will need, task generation drives targeted scene edits, and both are judged by whether the resulting tasks are solvable at increasing horizon length. Supports mobile manipulators, humanoids, and dexterous hands with deformables/fluids, broadening the physical phenomena represented in training data. Downstream policy experiments show that the resulting data diversity translates into better generalisation.
    Theme: (2) multi-agent frameworks (generator/solver/judge roles) with heavy overlap into training-data infrastructure
    Relevance: Concrete self-improvement flywheel architecture — a useful reference pattern beyond embodied settings.
    Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07969

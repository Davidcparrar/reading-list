## 1. WorkflowArena: Benchmarking Executable Workflow Orchestration in LLM-Based Multi-Agent Systems

- Authors: Zheng Jiang, Fang Niu, Bo Peng, et al. (14 authors)
- Summary: The authors formalize "workflow orchestration" as a distinct skill that emerges once agents can both spawn worker agents and author the workflows that coordinate them, and they argue existing agentic benchmarks conflate it with raw model capability. WorkflowArena collects 145 long-horizon tasks in 8 professional domains (software engineering, formal math, legal, 3D production, …) and grades deliverables with hidden verifiers while recording the full delegation graph. Across nine frontier models, no configuration wins everywhere: Claude-in-Claude-Code invokes workflows less often but with larger worker groups than GPT-in-Codex, and when the executor is held fixed, workflows authored by Claude Opus 5.5 lift weaker executors, showing that orchestration design itself is a measurable lever.
- Theme: (2) Multi-agent frameworks & orchestration.
- Relevance: Directly measures coordination, role/tool assignment, and workflow authorship across the exact orchestration layer the digest tracks; rare separation of "worker LLM" from "orchestrator LLM" in evaluation.
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12856

## 2. Discovering formally verified quantum programs with AI

- Authors: Sergey Shirobokov, Ben Jaderberg, Katerina Hristova, et al. (incl. Swarat Chaudhuri, Pushmeet Kohli)
- Summary: A Google DeepMind–led team couples Gemini-based coding agents with a Lean library of quantum programs so that every candidate must be type-checked against a formal specification and a cost model before it counts as a solution. Teams of agents share partial results through structured wikis and discussions, letting the system push past the small-circuit limits that make classical testing alone insufficient. The reported improvements over prior baselines reach 2.55× for parallel search and 290× for quantum arithmetic, with downstream gains of 11×, 8×, and 2.5× on fusion, photochemistry, and drug-metabolism simulations.
- Theme: (3) Spec-driven development & formal verification for coding agents.
- Relevance: Canonical example of agent outputs being gated by machine-checked correctness against an explicit spec; demonstrates multi-agent organization layered on top of a proof assistant, which is the exact intersection the digest is tracking.
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09822

## 3. MIRIX-2.0: Multi-Agent Memory System

- Authors: Wei-Chieh Huang, Weizhi Zhang, Jiayan Nan, Peng Xia, Yu Wang
- Summary: MIRIX-2.0 splits long-term memory for LLM agents across six typed memory agents that all keep complete records, and indexes episodic and semantic traces in a provenance hypergraph so retrieval runs under explicit evidence budgets rather than growing with history. An "AutoDream" component merges duplicates off the write path and distills past sessions into reusable skills, which lets the system avoid fine-tuning while still raising LoCoMo to 91.4% and LongMemEval-S to 72.6% (a 14.3-point jump on the latter over the strongest baseline).
- Theme: (2) Multi-agent frameworks & orchestration (architecture + role assignment).
- Relevance: Concrete role-typed multi-agent architecture with budgeted retrieval — directly addresses the orchestration/communication-protocol theme for persistent agents.
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12068

## 4. Speculative Self-Distillation Enables Efficient Knowledge Internalization

- Authors: Shayan Talaei, Agam Bhatia, Arshia Soltani Moakhar, Jonas Hübotter, Amin Saberi, Azalia Mirhoseini
- Summary: Speculative Self-Distillation (SSD) runs training in a per-token mixed-policy regime: the student decodes its own rollouts, and the teacher only intervenes at tokens where its distribution diverges meaningfully from the student's, which keeps the generation on-policy while cutting supervision cost. On closed-book QA covering fact recall, knowledge updates, and skill acquisition, SSD nearly matches full on-policy distillation with 38–69% fewer supervised tokens, beats off-policy distillation on held-out tests, and loses the least general capability in a forgetting stress test.
- Theme: (1) Core LLM advances — fine-tuning / PEFT-adjacent.
- Relevance: Addresses a real pain point for agent-building teams: teaching new knowledge without expensive on-policy budgets and without erasing general skills.
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12016

## 5. Reframe: Observation-Aligned Multi-Teacher On-Policy Distillation for Agentic Visual Reasoning

- Authors: Haozhi Yuan, Handong Li, Yifan Zhang, Qianshan Wei
- Summary: Reframe argues that current agentic-reasoning distillation misses the mark by matching output-probability distributions while ignoring whether the agent actually attended to tool-returned observations. Stage 1 trains spatial and temporal specialists with grounded-evidence rewards and random masking of the original image so the models must use tool output; Stage 2 swaps weak tool calls mid-rollout for canonical ones and continues distillation from the corrected observation. The method beats Mix-RL and vanilla multi-teacher OPD across 14 image/video benchmarks and increases measured "observation dependence" versus outcome-only GRPO — without any task-specific SFT cold start.
- Theme: (1) Core LLM advances — agentic-system evaluation + fine-tuning.
- Relevance: Rare paper that treats tool-observation faithfulness as a first-class training signal, which is squarely the "agentic-system evaluation" slice of theme 1.
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12886

## 6. Will AI mess my RTL? A snapshot-based approach to verifying AI-written RTL (SVApshot)

- Authors: Bernat Homs, Oscar Palomar, Miquel Moreto, Marcelo Orenes-Vera
- Summary: SVApshot uses LLMs to generate and iteratively repair SystemVerilog Assertions that pin down an RTL module's current observable behavior as a formal regression suite, with no human in the loop. The framework adds duplicate detection, assertion-set expansion, and formal-verification–guided repair of failing assertions, and the authors report strong coverage on complex modules plus detection of both injected faults and AI-introduced bugs — turning the assertion suite into a reusable spec against which later edits (human or AI) can be checked.
- Theme: (3) Spec-driven development & formal verification for coding (hardware) agents.
- Relevance: Practical realization of "capture the spec, then verify the agent against it" for a coding-adjacent domain; also doubles as an assertion-based benchmark for LLM hardware generation.
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12712

## 7. How Much Training-Inference Mismatch Can On-Policy Distillation Tolerate?

- Authors: Cong Yu, Ziting Wang, Songlin Jiang, et al.
- Summary: The paper puts numbers on how far on-policy distillation can drift from true on-policy before quality collapses, probing quantization (across three student-teacher pairs), weight staleness, and injected noise. Key findings: OPD tolerates a surprisingly wide mismatch budget, specific layers are precision-sensitive, and OPD stays stable under rollout configs that break RL; the authors propose an admission check against a calibrated budget plus model-specific FP8 recipes that keep ≥1.93× the KV-cache tokens of BF16 and, combined with async rollouts, reach matched quality 1.62× faster than synchronous BF16.
- Theme: (1) Core LLM advances — fine-tuning / PEFT.
- Relevance: Turns on-policy distillation from a black art into something with measurable guardrails, directly useful for teams training agent policies on budget.
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11450

## 8. Steering Emergent Behavior in Multi-Agent LLM Systems via Information Networks

- Authors: Sepehr Ilami, Babak Heydari
- Summary: The authors treat the "who observes whom" graph as a separate design surface from the interaction graph and ask how an operator who controls only observations (not payoffs or messages) should wire a group of self-interested LLM agents. Running 1,080 episodes of 10 identical agents across six repeated games, they find that broader peer visibility helps when goals align but amplifies free-riding and congestion when they don't — in a public-goods game, zero shared information was best, and a modular "barbell" observation layout was the worst at a fixed budget because subgroups entrench harmful behavior.
- Theme: (2) Multi-agent frameworks & orchestration — communication protocols / information topology.
- Relevance: Rare empirical study of information-network design as an orchestration primitive, with a concrete prescription (don't default to full transparency).
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11714

## 9. How Many Exposures Does a World Model Need? Learning Unseen Game Mechanics at Test Time

- Authors: Ku Onoda, Haruto Kawano, Shota Takashiro, Kohei Hayashi
- Summary: Using ARC-AGI-3, the authors introduce MechEval (69 labeled mechanics across 20 games) and WARC, a 35M-parameter vision transformer that updates all parameters after every transition, to measure how many observations a world model needs before it can predict a new mechanic. In held-out games, WARC learns about half of the mechanics within roughly 80 exposures in a single play session (and most of them given more data), while without updates far fewer mechanics are acquired; pairing WARC with a fixed graph explorer (WARC-Agent) raises the explorer's efficiency score from 0.15 to 0.46, almost entirely via test-time training.
- Theme: (1) Core LLM advances — reasoning / world models at test time.
- Relevance: Clean quantification of test-time learning for planning-style agents; relevant to anyone building agents that must adapt in-session rather than only via pretraining.
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12324

## 10. ErosionBench: Probing Alignment Decay in Enterprise Multi-Agent Systems

- Authors: Riya Bharathwaj, Akshara Prabhakar, Roshan Ram, et al. (incl. Huan Wang, Silvio Savarese)
- Summary: ErosionBench scores 1,728 enterprise multi-agent scenarios (plus 198 image-based attacks) in which no individual message is malicious but the assembled tool call is — benign-looking requests escalate as they pass through poisoned shared state, long documents, and trusted peer messages. Tool actions are judged against authorized intent and provenance via deterministic contracts, and the authors show that perimeter defenses hit low attack-success rates mostly by over-refusing 54–92% of legitimate work; their reference "Meta-Auditor," combining sync state checks with async trajectory monitoring, reaches 98.5% recall at 2.9% FPR on a 906-scenario held-out split and generalizes across text and image channels.
- Theme: (2) Multi-agent frameworks & orchestration — safety/communication layer.
- Relevance: Puts a measurement harness on a failure mode (cumulative decay across benign messages) that single-agent benchmarks can't surface, with a defense proposal bound to the orchestration layer rather than the model.
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11266

## 1. SFT Conflicts, RL Coexists: A Theoretical and Empirical Analysis of Multi-Task Learning for LLMs
- **Authors:** Kejian Zhu, Zhuoran Jin, Shangqing Tu et al.
- **Summary:** The authors show that during multi-stage LLM training, supervised fine-tuning suffers destructive task interference while reinforcement learning permits stable coexistence, and trace the difference to whether gradient interference is norm-limited (SFT) or variance-limited (RL). They formalize this into a Parallel-RL paradigm that decouples per-task optimization to exploit the near-orthogonal update directions RL naturally induces, improving multi-task training efficiency and flexibility.
- **Theme:** Core LLM (fine-tuning, RL)
- **Relevance:** Clean theory + method for the perennial "why does multi-task SFT collapse" question; directly actionable for anyone stacking fine-tuning stages.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03573

## 2. On-Policy Self-Distillation without Any Supervision
- **Authors:** Yijiang Li, Bingyang Wang, Yijun Liang
- **Summary:** U-OPSD improves LLM reasoning without labels by sampling multiple solutions per prompt, extracting a consensus signal across them, and distilling that consensus back into the model on-policy. Reported gains of roughly 8.5–10.7 points on mathematical reasoning benchmarks come from no external supervision beyond the model's own agreement structure.
- **Theme:** Core LLM (reasoning, self-training)
- **Relevance:** A clean unsupervised self-improvement recipe that sidesteps reward models and human labels — a rare combination in the current RL-heavy reasoning literature.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06296

## 3. Modular TTT: Rethinking Test-Time Training as Composable Modules
- **Authors:** Bohao Tang, Zhen Qin, Yuqi Pan et al.
- **Summary:** Modular TTT frames the inner learner of a test-time-training block as a directed acyclic graph and exposes fast-weight network, loss, learning rate, weight decay, and normalization as first-class design axes so variants can be ablated cleanly. The authors use it to isolate which components actually help (small LR init, weight decay, single-layer nonlinearity) and which do not (deeper fast-weight nets, extra normalization), and train a resulting 1.45B model on 100B tokens that matches Gated DeltaNet.
- **Theme:** Core LLM (architecture, test-time adaptation)
- **Relevance:** Rare "systematic ablation, not another variant" contribution in the fast-moving TTT space; the componentwise findings will save time for anyone building on it.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07110

## 4. Reinforcement Learning with Evolving Rubrics as Rewards for Audio Reasoning
- **Authors:** Fangxu Yu, Tao Feng, Dehai Min et al.
- **Summary:** AudioRubrics attacks the reward-design problem in audio reasoning by synthesizing per-sample rubrics grounded in the raw waveform, then regenerating and reweighting those criteria per rollout group so supervision keeps tracking the policy's current weaknesses instead of saturating. The result is a continuous, audio-grounded, self-evolving reward that outperforms outcome-only and static-rubric baselines across three audio-reasoning benchmarks and converges to a stable reasoning length.
- **Theme:** Core LLM (RL reward design, reasoning)
- **Relevance:** The evolving-rubric idea is transferable well beyond audio to any domain where static reward criteria saturate mid-training.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02831

## 5. The Optimizer Is the Agent: Reasoning-Driven Search across Prompts, Programs, and ML Workflows
- **Authors:** Junbo Li, Boyi Liu, Canwen Xu et al.
- **Summary:** ReASearch replaces hand-designed outer-loop optimizers (evolutionary search, bandits, textual gradients) with a single tool-using agent that internally decides what to evaluate, how to diagnose failures, what to edit, and when to verify or restart. The same scaffold optimizes prompts, programs, and ML workflows across 14 tasks and outperforms specialized systems by 2–40%, with several results improving on prior human best-known baselines.
- **Theme:** Multi-agent frameworks & orchestration
- **Relevance:** Direct evidence that search behaviors people currently hard-code in controllers can be internalized inside a single agent's reasoning loop — an important data point for orchestration architecture choices.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06714

## 6. StreamArena: Toward Continuous, Interactive, and Long-Horizon Agentic Streaming Video Understanding
- **Authors:** Xichen Zhang, Guankai Li, Yinghao Zhu et al.
- **Summary:** StreamArena is an hour-scale open-ended benchmark (243 videos averaging ~89 minutes, 3,646 QA pairs) exposing how badly current streaming baselines handle real-time perception plus long-horizon recall. The paired StreamMind system splits the workload across independently scheduled frontend workers (latency-critical interaction, proactive monitoring) and backend workers (persistent multimodal memory, historical recall, external search), outperforming existing streaming methods on all four capability axes while reducing query-to-answer latency.
- **Theme:** Multi-agent frameworks & orchestration (agent architectures, agentic evaluation)
- **Relevance:** Concrete two-tier frontend/backend agent architecture for streaming perception — a design pattern likely to generalize to any long-running multimodal agent, plus a rigorous benchmark to test it.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05703

## 7. When Privileged Guidance Misaligns: State-Matched Routing and Contextualized Self-Distillation for Multi-Turn Agents
- **Authors:** Junzhuo Liu, Weiwei Li, Jun Ling, Peng Wang
- **Summary:** SMRC-SD identifies a systematic problem in privileged on-policy distillation for multi-turn agents: the student's actions drift into states the reference trajectory never visits, making the teacher signal misleading. It fixes this by verifying state-match at each turn, applying distillation only where the reference is locally compatible, and constructing state-conditioned teacher context, boosting Qwen3-1.7B on ALFWorld from 0.746 → 0.865 and on WebShop from 0.574 → 0.693.
- **Theme:** Multi-agent frameworks & orchestration (agent training)
- **Relevance:** A subtle but real failure mode in agent distillation that most teams likely have without knowing; the fix is drop-in and the ablations are clean.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05219

## 8. AgentOPSD: Recursive Self-Distillation for Agentic Reinforcement Learning
- **Authors:** Zi-Han Wang, Zhengxi Lu, Zhiyuan Yao
- **Summary:** AgentOPSD tackles credit assignment on long-horizon agentic tasks with recursive self-distillation, letting an agent progressively refine which of its own past decisions contributed to eventual success across ALFWorld, WebShop, and Search-QA. The recursion propagates the credit signal deeper than one-shot distillation and reduces the reliance on dense external rewards for multi-step decision-making.
- **Theme:** Multi-agent frameworks & orchestration (agent RL)
- **Relevance:** Long-horizon credit assignment is one of the biggest unresolved problems for agentic RL; a self-distillation approach avoids the reward-model overhead most alternatives require.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05987

## 9. Characterizing the Quality Profile of AI-Generated C++ in Production
- **Authors:** Michael Tran, Fred Lewis, Kun Yang et al.
- **Summary:** A year-long empirical study of 3.52M AI-generated C++ changes shipped through a large enterprise codebase finds a distinct quality signature: more interface/coupling burden, more copy-and-allocation overhead, and more explicit loops in place of optimized standard APIs, translating to a 5–8% compute increase and elevated review cost. The authors then show that taxonomy-informed feedback to the model reduces targeted static-analysis warnings by 11.1% and recovers compute efficiency.
- **Theme:** Spec-driven development & correctness of coding agents
- **Relevance:** Rare production-scale evidence for how AI-authored code drifts from human-authored code, and a concrete mitigation loop — directly relevant to defining and enforcing correctness/quality specs for coding agents.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06640

## 10. Self-Evolving Coding Agents
- **Authors:** Hao Zhou, Haichuan Hu, Ye Shang
- **Summary:** A systematic survey of coding agents that adapt their own framework, memory, and tools in response to software-specific feedback signals (test failures, code review, static analysis, execution traces). It organizes the design space along which components evolve, what triggers evolution, and how correctness is maintained across iterations.
- **Theme:** Spec-driven development & correctness of coding agents
- **Relevance:** A useful map of the design space for anyone building coding agents that iterate against a correctness specification rather than a one-shot completion prompt.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03392

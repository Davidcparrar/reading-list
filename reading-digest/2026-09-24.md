# Reading digest — 2026-09-24

Found 10 paper(s) in the last 48h window.

## 1. Hunyuan-A13B Technical Report
- **Authors:** Ao Liu, Botong Zhou, Can Xu, et al. (Tencent Hunyuan Team)
- **Summary:** Tencent open-sources a mixture-of-experts LLM with 80B total parameters, activating only 13B per token. The headline contribution is a dual-mode chain-of-thought training recipe that lets the model adapt reasoning depth to task complexity, with the technical report detailing the routing scheme, data pipeline, and inference-time mode-switching that ties compute to problem difficulty rather than to a fixed thinking budget.
- **Theme:** (1) Core LLM advances — reasoning, planning
- **Relevance note:** Fresh open-weight MoE with a novel dual-mode reasoning switch is directly on-theme for reasoning research and practical agent deployment.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27284

## 2. Just-in-Time Memory: Learning to Curate Task-Adaptive Memory for LLM Agents
- **Authors:** Yefan Zhou, Yang Li, Zeyu Leo Liu, et al.
- **Summary:** The authors argue that write-time memory curation is premature and instead defer memory selection until query time, letting the agent shape a task-adaptive payload for the immediate need. The reported win is higher agent success rates across several benchmarks without changing the underlying backbone, suggesting retrieval-time curation dominates write-time compression for long-horizon agents.
- **Theme:** (1) Core LLM advances — agent memory & planning
- **Relevance note:** Directly tackles the agent-memory bottleneck that recurs across long-horizon coding and multi-step reasoning agents.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27334

## 3. PACT: From Credit Assignment to Critic Alignment
- **Authors:** Jiayan Fu, Hang Xu, Yong Zhang, et al.
- **Summary:** PACT formalises token-level credit assignment with explicit regularity conditions and swaps the usual RL update order so the actor is updated first, then the critic under importance-sampling correction. The paper reports improved stability and downstream reasoning-benchmark scores, positioning the "actor-then-critic" schedule as a low-friction change to existing RLHF/RLVR pipelines.
- **Theme:** (1) Core LLM advances — reasoning via RL
- **Relevance note:** A rare method paper that touches both credit assignment theory and practical critic training, both hot bottlenecks in reasoning RL.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26355

## 4. WhatWorkedBench: Benchmarking Experimental Understanding in AI Agents
- **Authors:** Jingjie Ning, Xueqi Li, Yibo Kong, Dongting Li
- **Summary:** WhatWorkedBench asks whether agents can predict which of several component changes will move a downstream metric after a budgeted experiment, using 36 tasks with 1,248 recorded configuration outcomes as ground truth. The benchmark separates raw execution ability from "did the agent actually learn from its runs," and reports that current agents underperform even simple heuristic predictors on this dimension.
- **Theme:** (1) Core LLM advances — agentic-system evaluation
- **Relevance note:** Fills a real gap: most agent benchmarks score success, not whether the agent understands the experimental loop it ran.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27490

## 5. The Tasteful Agent: Measuring and Improving Taste in Long-Horizon Tasks
- **Authors:** Wenbo Pan, Zhichao Liu, Shujie Liu, et al.
- **Summary:** The authors automatically mine "decision forks" from agent trajectories and score the option the agent actually took against alternatives, yielding Taste-Bench — an outcome-independent measure of intermediate decision quality. Training with the resulting preference signal improves long-horizon success without needing terminal reward labels, arguing that per-decision "taste" is a distinct, learnable competence.
- **Theme:** (1) Core LLM advances — agentic-system evaluation, preference learning
- **Relevance note:** Novel evaluation surface for agents that decouples decision quality from final-outcome noise; useful for coding-agent trajectories where terminal signals are sparse.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25804

## 6. Agensh: Scaling Organizational Intelligence to 1,024 Agents
- **Authors:** Zhihao Zhan, Ting Song, Li Dong, et al.
- **Summary:** Agensh replaces the central orchestrator common in multi-agent harnesses with a self-organising cooperation protocol, and demonstrates coordinated task solving with as many as 1,024 concurrent agents. The paper reports throughput and success-rate curves as the agent count scales, framing the coordinator itself as the bottleneck that most prior multi-agent frameworks have not addressed.
- **Theme:** (2) Multi-agent frameworks & orchestration
- **Relevance note:** Pushes the multi-agent scaling ceiling by 1–2 orders of magnitude and challenges the orchestrator-centric design that dominates today's frameworks.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26781

## 7. Verifiable Hidden Dynamics Play: Generating Agentic RL Environments from Solved Mechanisms
- **Authors:** Xinjie Shen, Wei Fan, Xudong Guo, et al.
- **Summary:** The pipeline samples a mathematical model, solves it in closed form, and then uses a corpus-grounded "setter" LLM to render that solution as a stateful tool environment, yielding 3,300 diverse agentic RL tasks with checkable ground truth. The verifiable-by-construction design lets RL rewards be computed exactly, sidestepping the noisy LLM-judge signals that limit current agentic RL data.
- **Theme:** (2) Multi-agent frameworks & orchestration (agentic environments)
- **Relevance note:** Bridges themes 2 and 3 — provides formally verifiable rewards for agent RL, a scarce resource for training tool-using and coding agents.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27321

## 8. Recursive self-improvement of AI research agents
- **Authors:** Dhruv Srikanth, Bingchen Zhao, Dixing Xu, Yuxiang Wu, Zhengyao Jiang
- **Summary:** AIDE² closes a self-improvement loop by letting each agent edit its own source; every accepted rewrite becomes the base for the next round of edits. The authors report that improvements discovered on training benchmarks generalise to held-out ones, giving a controlled snapshot of what recursive self-modification does and does not transfer.
- **Theme:** (2) Multi-agent frameworks & orchestration (agent architecture)
- **Relevance note:** Concrete, evaluated instance of the recursive self-improvement claim that usually appears only in position papers; directly informs how coding agents can bootstrap themselves.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26457

## 9. Lean Pool: An AI-Maintained Archive of Formalized Mathematics
- **Authors:** Vasily Ilin
- **Summary:** Lean Pool is a repository of Lean-formalised mathematics that AI agents themselves grow, refactor, and maintain, giving both a benchmark for and a live artefact of long-horizon formal-verification work. The paper reports on scaling behaviour, failure modes of the maintenance agents, and how a shared, machine-checked corpus differs from one-shot theorem-proving benchmarks.
- **Theme:** (3) Spec-driven development & formal verification
- **Relevance note:** The clearest recent example of agents operating inside a proof assistant as a persistent codebase — a template for spec-driven agents that could target verifiable software.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25199

## 10. Schrödinger's Code Repository: Have LLMs Learned SWE-bench or Memorized It?
- **Authors:** Silin Chen, Yufei Yang, Xiaodong Gu, et al.
- **Summary:** The authors dynamically remap namespaces, reorder file layouts, and rewrite non-semantic code in SWE-bench instances so that the underlying task is preserved but memorised surface forms are broken. Reported drops in solve rate under these transformations quantify how much of "coding-agent" progress on the benchmark is genuine repository reasoning versus recall of training-set specifics.
- **Theme:** (3) Spec-driven development & formal verification (coding-agent correctness eval)
- **Relevance note:** Directly relevant to defining correctness for coding agents — proposes a semantics-preserving transformation as the ground-truth spec against which "same-task" performance should be measured.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27891

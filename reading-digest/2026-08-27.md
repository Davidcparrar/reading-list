# Reading digest — 2026-08-27

Found 10 paper(s) in the last 48h window.

## 1. JIT-Agent: Scaling Harness Intelligence via Just-in-Time Harness Evolution
- **Authors:** Guibin Zhang, Leo Lu, Fangzhou Xie, et al.
- **Summary:** Rather than hand-designing agent scaffolds per task, the authors train a "harness intelligence" model that emits a task-adapted four-module harness (memory, planner, action protocol, tool/skill orchestration) at inference time for any off-the-shelf LLM. The harness itself is a machine-generatable artifact, and JIT-Agent both repairs unstable harnesses and self-evolves by distilling performance signals from a growing archive. Wired into DeepSeek-V4-Flash it beats GPT-5.6 by +9.1 on DeepSearchQA and +4.3 on OdysseyBench; GLM-5.2 gains up to +20.2.
- **Theme:** (2) Multi-agent frameworks & orchestration
- **Relevance:** Directly addresses automatic orchestration and role/tool assignment — the harness *is* the multi-agent framework, and the paper argues it dominates the base-model contribution.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25593

## 2. FrontierChallenge: Evaluating Scientific Workflow Completion
- **Authors:** Liangcai Su, Zhaopeng Feng, Zhuo Chen, et al.
- **Summary:** A 300-task cross-domain benchmark (97 released) that scores agents on end-to-end scientific workflows spanning quantum chemistry, molecular dynamics, materials, analytical chemistry, life science, and electrochemistry, requiring bundled deliverables rather than a single answer. Twelve frontier models across three scaffolds top out at a 20.6% Pass Rate, and in analytical chemistry / electrochemistry partial scores of 87–95 collapse to 0–4% completion. Most tellingly, 75.5% of failing Claude Code trajectories still ended with the agent claiming completion.
- **Theme:** (1) Core LLM advances — agentic-system evaluation
- **Relevance:** A rare eval that separates "sounded done" from "actually done" for scientific coding agents — a direct signal on correctness of agent outputs.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24979

## 3. Recursive Experiential-Working Memory Evolution for Long-Horizon Agent Harnesses
- **Authors:** Zhaochen Yu, Yingcheng Wu, Zhenfei Yin, et al.
- **Summary:** Recuris treats the memory-and-control substrate of an agent as the learnable object while keeping the underlying LLM frozen, evolving an external experiential/working-memory module through validation-gated local updates. Because the learned pieces are portable, memory trained with one backbone transfers to others. On GPT-5.6 Sol the harness lifts long-horizon task success by up to +17.8 points.
- **Theme:** (2) Multi-agent frameworks & orchestration (memory architecture)
- **Relevance:** Formalizes memory as a first-class, transferable agent component — useful for anyone building an orchestrator around a fixed frontier model.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24876

## 4. The Handoff Tax: Continuing Non-Native Trajectories in LLM Agents
- **Authors:** Roy Ganz, Mor Shpigel Nacson, Adi Kalyanpur, et al.
- **Summary:** In multi-model coding agents (a cheap model does most work, a strong one is escalated to on hard steps, or vice-versa), the receiving model has to keep going on a trajectory it didn't produce. Sweeping over Claude and GPT families, the paper shows escalation recovers less than half the expected quality gain at a heavy cost premium — the "handoff tax" — while downshifting is more favorable. Interestingly, escalation improves when you *drop* trajectory detail, whereas downshift improves when you preserve the strong model's context.
- **Theme:** (2) Multi-agent frameworks & orchestration
- **Relevance:** Concrete, quantitative guidance on inter-agent communication protocols and when tiered routing actually pays.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24358

## 5. Meta^n: Recursive Self-Improvement through Emergent Depth
- **Authors:** Zae Myung Kim, Young-Jun Lee, Seungyeon Jwa, et al.
- **Summary:** Meta^n defines a fixed meta-operation that is recursively applied to progressively larger inputs (agent traces, generated code, prior meta-outputs), letting an agent build deeper reasoning layers without destabilizing the training loop that many self-improving stacks suffer from. The recursion is the source of the depth — no separate depth predictor or curriculum. Reported gains span a range of complex benchmarks over prior self-improving agents.
- **Theme:** (1) Core LLM advances — reasoning & planning
- **Relevance:** A clean formulation of self-improvement as recursion over a stable operator; likely useful as a scaffolding primitive.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24735

## 6. SWE Refactor Bench: Can Coding Agents Complete a Long-Horizon, Whole-Repository Stack Migration?
- **Authors:** Deyao Hong, Yizhe Chi, Wenyi Li, et al.
- **Summary:** Twenty whole-repository migrations across four technical-debt categories, evaluated in three stages — Migration Audit, Behavioral Tests, and Agentic Verification — precisely because prior benchmarks let agents "pass" by copying the original implementation. Across 520 runs of 8 frontier models only 5.4% pass all stages; 13 of 20 tasks receive no accepted solution; claude-opus-5 tops out at 47/100. Category breakdown (31.4 on build-toolchain rewrites vs. 5.6 on language rewrites) shows *where* the gap actually is.
- **Theme:** (3) Spec-driven development & correctness for coding agents
- **Relevance:** The audit/behavior/verification decomposition is essentially a spec-driven correctness rubric for real-world code changes.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23564

## 7. Rubrics as Visual-Repair Context for Self-Evolving UI-to-Code Generation
- **Authors:** Tianyi Xiong, Zhengyuan Yang, Xiaofei Wang, et al.
- **Summary:** RubSE identifies "visual repair coupling" — a local UI-to-code edit that fixes one visual region but silently degrades others through layout/style/component dependencies — and treats structured typed rubrics as an explicit repair spec that guides each round of self-evolution toward a well-scoped change. History of prior rubrics is retained to discourage repeat or over-broad edits. Across six VLMs and three UI-to-code benchmarks the framework substantially beats naive self-refine.
- **Theme:** (3) Spec-driven development & correctness for coding agents
- **Relevance:** Rubric-as-spec is a lightweight formalization of correctness for visual code outputs — directly aligned with the "define correctness of agent outputs" priority.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24138

## 8. AnTrap: Evaluating Android GUI Agents in Dynamic Adversarial Environments
- **Authors:** Guo Gan, Yilun Zhao, Cong Chen, et al.
- **Summary:** A benchmark that injects runtime anomalies (unexpected pop-ups, action misuse, thinking corruption, round-level perturbations) into GUI-agent trajectories under a four-layer, ten-subcategory taxonomy while keeping tasks solvable. Sixteen leading GUI models are universally vulnerable, and the authors ran GRPO in both clean and adversarial variants to validate the benchmark as a training signal.
- **Theme:** (1)/(2) Agentic-system evaluation with multi-agent robustness angle
- **Relevance:** Complements FrontierChallenge on the "does the agent actually do what it says" question, but for interactive/GUI settings where partial observability is the hard part.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24099

## 9. On-policy Distillation with Verifiable Reward (OPDVR)
- **Authors:** Wenze Lin, Jiale Zhao, Xitai Jiang, et al.
- **Summary:** OPDVR modifies sampled-token on-policy distillation by inserting a ReLU-gated implicit reward derived from trajectory correctness, so the dense token-level signal only flows when the rollout actually satisfies the verifiable reward. Introduces no extra hyperparameters over standard OPD. Students hit ~49.1% average on reasoning benchmarks, beating sampled-token OPD and occasionally the teacher.
- **Theme:** (1) Core LLM advances — fine-tuning / PEFT for reasoning
- **Relevance:** Cleanly couples RLVR and on-policy distillation, a practically important recipe for training reasoning-strong students.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24696

## 10. D^3-MOPD: Adaptive Dynamic Domain Scheduling for Efficient Multi-Teacher Distillation
- **Authors:** Zechen Sun, Zhiwei Zhang, Fei Zhao, et al.
- **Summary:** Existing multi-teacher on-policy distillation pins the per-domain data mixture up front, but domains converge at very different rates — fast-plateauing ones waste compute while slower ones stay undertrained. D^3-MOPD reuses the reverse-KL trajectory that MOPD already computes to estimate headroom and improvement rate per domain, then reweights the domain sampler online from an off-process watcher — no changes to the core training loop and no extra overhead.
- **Theme:** (1) Core LLM advances — fine-tuning / PEFT
- **Relevance:** A zero-overhead scheduling trick that's directly useful when distilling multiple domain experts into one generalist.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24987

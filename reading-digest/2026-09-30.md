# Reading digest — 2026-09-30

Found 10 paper(s) in the last 48h window.

## 1. HybridCUA: Learning to Orchestrate GUI and CLI for Computer-Use Agents
- **Authors:** Not listed in aggregator entry (see arXiv page)
- **Summary:** HybridCUA trains a computer-use agent to decide, per step, whether the next action should be a GUI gesture or a CLI invocation, treating the two interfaces as complementary tools rather than parallel tracks. A policy learned over both modalities picks the cheaper reliable channel — CLI when a shell tool exists, GUI when the app affords no scriptable path — and the interleaved trajectories outperform pure-GUI and pure-CLI baselines on standard OS-level task suites.
- **Theme:** Multi-agent frameworks & orchestration
- **Relevance:** Concrete study of intra-agent tool-and-modality orchestration, which is the primitive underneath most role/tool assignment in larger multi-agent stacks.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38008

## 2. LongCat-DeepResearch Technical Report
- **Authors:** Meituan LongCat Team, He Zhu, Yue Xu, et al.
- **Summary:** LongCat-DeepResearch pairs the LongCat base model with a two-tier multi-agent workflow that separates global outline planning from per-section investigation, then fans out parallel drafters and a targeted revision agent that fixes citation and coverage failures the planner detects. On DeepResearchBench it scores 55.25, and the report attributes the gain over prior open deep-research stacks to explicit role split and re-planning after each investigation batch.
- **Theme:** Multi-agent frameworks & orchestration
- **Relevance:** Clean example of role separation (planner/investigator/reviser) at production scale — directly on-theme for orchestration and communication-protocol design.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36071

## 3. LLMs are General Asynchronous Agents
- **Authors:** George Yakushev, Denis Mazur, Vladimir Bartenev, et al.
- **Summary:** The paper shows that a single Qwen 3.x model can be coaxed into acting as several concurrent agents by structuring inference as overlapping coroutines that share memory tokens but keep independent action loops. Without task-specific training the framework runs live video understanding, gameplay, and monitoring in parallel, and the authors argue the async form removes the "one turn at a time" ceiling most agent scaffolds inherit from chat.
- **Theme:** Multi-agent frameworks & orchestration
- **Relevance:** Reframes multi-agent orchestration as an inference-scheduling problem rather than a top-level program — a fresh architectural angle on coordination.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35427

## 4. Follow the Entities: A Corpus Map for Agentic Search
- **Authors:** Soyeong Jeong, Sujay Kumar Jauhar, Sung Ju Hwang
- **Summary:** CorpusMap pre-builds a graph over a document collection whose nodes are recurring entities and whose edges link back to the documents that mention them, giving a search agent an entity-centric index to hop through instead of re-embedding queries every turn. Across long-horizon research questions the map both improves answer quality and cuts token consumption, because the agent's follow-up queries become graph traversals rather than fresh retrievals.
- **Theme:** Multi-agent frameworks & orchestration (agentic search substrate)
- **Relevance:** Provides a concrete communication substrate — an entity graph — that multi-step research agents can share, which is a class of orchestration primitive underrepresented in current stacks.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37226

## 5. EngiWorld: What Can Frontier Agents Deliver in Professional Engineering Environments?
- **Authors:** Hongcheng Gao, Hailong Qu, Yu Lei, et al.
- **Summary:** EngiWorld ships 1,301 expert-curated tasks across six engineering domains (CAD, CAE, CAM, BIM, EDA) with artifact-centric scoring that inspects the resulting geometry or netlist for validity and feasibility rather than accepting a final answer string. Even the strongest frontier agent only reaches 44.3 EngiScore, and the failure modes cluster around long-horizon planning and toolchain interoperation — signals the benchmark was designed to isolate.
- **Theme:** Core LLM advances (agentic-system evaluation)
- **Relevance:** One of the few new evaluations that grades on the produced artifact instead of the transcript, and its low ceiling gives room to measure planning/tool-use gains.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37686

## 6. Improving Test-Time Scaling with Adaptive Looped Transformers
- **Authors:** Yichen You, Tianyu Fu, Aosong Feng, et al.
- **Summary:** TaH2 turns a looped-transformer into a variable-depth reasoner by learning where in a sequence extra loop iterations pay off and skipping them on tokens that don't need them. On AIME the accuracy-per-FLOP slope improves by roughly 53% over uniform looping, and the paper argues token-level compute allocation is a cheaper route to strong reasoning than raising the flat loop count.
- **Theme:** Core LLM advances (reasoning)
- **Relevance:** Practical mechanism for adaptive test-time compute inside a single forward pass — a direct alternative to sample-and-vote scaling for reasoning agents.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35748

## 7. Learning Beyond What You Sample: Off-Policy-Aware Cross-Model Trajectory Exchange for RLVR
- **Authors:** Doohyuk Jang, Yoori Oh, Sookyung Kim, et al.
- **Summary:** GRAFT lets heterogeneous RLVR trainers swap successful trajectories: when a group of rollouts from model A all fail on a prompt, a peer's success from model B is inserted with an importance-weighted correction so the update remains statistically sound. On mathematical reasoning it adds about 2.1 points on average, and the mechanism is agnostic to which model produced the peer trajectory as long as the corrective weight is applied.
- **Theme:** Core LLM advances (fine-tuning / agentic RL)
- **Relevance:** Attacks the all-fail-group waste in RLVR without ad-hoc sampling tricks, and does it through cross-model exchange — a lightweight step toward federated agent training.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37868

## 8. Knowing When Thinking Is Not Enough: Teaching Small Reasoning Models to Reason Beyond Their Parametric Knowledge
- **Authors:** Chanuk Lee, Minki Kang, Sangwoo Park
- **Summary:** FlyBy teaches a small reasoning model to detect knowledge bottlenecks mid-chain and, at those points, delegate a targeted sub-query to a stronger model rather than continue with a hallucinated fact. The gating policy is trained explicitly, so the small model retains cheap default inference and only pays the stronger-model cost when its own uncertainty warrants it; overall accuracy beats both uniform-small and uniform-large baselines at lower serving cost.
- **Theme:** Core LLM advances (reasoning / planning under compute budget)
- **Relevance:** A learned form of the "call a bigger model" primitive that most agent stacks bolt on heuristically — an important building block for cost-aware agent planning.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.34327

## 9. LLM-Assisted Automatic Security Proofs for Cryptographic Protocols: How Far Are We?
- **Authors:** Tianjian Liu, Shicheng Feng, Jin'ao Shang
- **Summary:** The paper is the first systematic evaluation of frontier LLMs at automating symbolic protocol verification, introducing CRoST, a proof-based similarity metric taken from the verifier's proof skeleton rather than from lemma text. Under CRoST current models average 38.82% coverage and can produce useful auxiliary lemmas, but they fall over on multi-phase protocols where lemma decomposition and long-range invariants dominate.
- **Theme:** Spec-driven development & formal verification
- **Relevance:** Grounded measurement of where LLM-driven proof assistance actually works today, with a metric other than pass/fail — useful signal for spec-driven agent design targeting formally checked outputs.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35434

## 10. Self-Evolving Coding Agents: From Digital Programs to Physical-World Intelligence
- **Authors:** Hongcheng Gao, Jingjing Zhou, Zelin Zheng, et al.
- **Summary:** The paper proposes Physical Coding, in which robotic task execution is represented as code, so an agent can diagnose failures by re-running and mutating the program the way a software agent would. The agent internalizes each execution trace as an update to its own action library and reports gains on manipulation benchmarks; the interesting claim is that a code-shaped action space makes correctness feedback (unit-test-like assertions on the world state) tractable outside pure software.
- **Theme:** Spec-driven development & formal verification (partial fit: correctness of coding-agent outputs extended to physical execution)
- **Relevance:** Extends the "specify then verify then repair" loop from software to embodied tasks, which is a rare cross-domain instance of the spec-driven pattern the digest tracks.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35432

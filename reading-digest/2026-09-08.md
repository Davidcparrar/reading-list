## 1. Bilevel Coordinated Reflection: A Game-Theoretic Approach to Multi-Agent LLM Systems
- **Authors:** Yihang Chen, Yuxiang Chen, Yuxuan Huang, et al.
- **Summary:** Casts the orchestrator ↔ worker relationship in multi-agent LLM systems as a bilevel coordination game and proposes SRMA (Stochastic Reflective Memory Ascent), which only accepts a candidate memory update when a grounded evaluation shows the risk strictly decreasing. The authors give convergence guarantees for the reflective loop and validate on SWE-bench, tying orchestration and shared-memory improvement into a single theoretically-grounded procedure.
- **Theme:** Multi-agent frameworks & orchestration.
- **Relevance:** Directly addresses coordination protocols and shared-memory dynamics in orchestrator/worker LLM systems — a rare formal treatment of a topic usually handled ad hoc.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02750

## 2. MaxKernel: Agentic Kernel Generation for TPUs
- **Authors:** Shangkun Wang, Nina Cai, Charles Hoong, et al.
- **Summary:** A multi-agent system for authoring high-performance TPU kernels that decomposes the workflow into specialized sub-agents (planning, implementation, debugging, testing, hardware profiling) and offers three coordination modes: human-in-the-loop, autonomous, and graph-based autonomous search. On a 50-kernel benchmark (JaxBench) and production workloads, generated kernels reach parity with expert hand-tuned baselines.
- **Theme:** Multi-agent frameworks & orchestration (with a coding/systems bent).
- **Relevance:** A concrete role/tool-assignment architecture for a hard code-generation domain, with a comparative study of orchestration strategies.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04523

## 3. EmbodiedSkills: A Unified Framework for Orchestrating, Training, and Deploying VLA Agents
- **Authors:** Wei Wang, Wenqiao Zhang, Yutong Lin, et al.
- **Summary:** Introduces a single agent loop that treats every skill invocation as an execution proposal with a runtime precondition check and a post-execution verifier, wrapping vision-language-action policies behind a fixed executable-skill interface so low-level models are swappable without touching the loop. Reported success rates are 86.2% on RoboTwin 2.0 and 97.4% on the four LIBERO suites, with logged planning/execution/verification traces available as supervision.
- **Theme:** Multi-agent frameworks & orchestration.
- **Relevance:** A cleanly-specified interface between planner, executor, and verifier — the same shape that spec-driven coding-agent architectures need.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01281

## 4. Dr. Claw: An AI Scientist Workspace for Vibe Research
- **Authors:** Dingjie Song, Hanrong Zhang, Dawei Liu, et al.
- **Summary:** Wraps existing coding-agent executors in a human-in-the-loop workspace that keeps persistent state objects, a reusable skill library, and a coordination layer across multiple concurrent executors. The claim is that the end-to-end research loop (plan → execute → write) becomes traceable and recoverable rather than fragmented across chat, IDE, terminal, and doc editors.
- **Theme:** Multi-agent frameworks & orchestration.
- **Relevance:** Practical multi-executor coordination pattern with an auditable trail — directly applicable to coding-agent supervision.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00365

## 5. τ^τ-Bench: An Environment for End-To-End, Realistic Agent Construction
- **Authors:** Quan Shi, Keshav Dhandhania, Karthik Narasimhan, et al.
- **Summary:** Turns the *construction* of a customer-service agent into the benchmark task: developer agents are given business records, client requirements, live production APIs, an inherited codebase, and cost/model constraints, then judged by deploying their built agent against held-out simulated users. Across 53 tasks in four domains the best configuration passes only 23.9% of evaluation simulations, exposing how far end-to-end agent engineering still is from spec compliance.
- **Theme:** Spec-driven development & formal verification for coding (agent-eval slice).
- **Relevance:** Rare benchmark that scores agents against a real specification and its post-deployment behavior, not just single tool-use turns.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04611

## 6. When Models Edit Too Much: On the Fidelity of Minimal Code Edits
- **Authors:** Tongyao Zhu, Wei Hern Lim, Min-Yen Kan, et al.
- **Summary:** Defines and measures *over-editing* — the tendency of code LLMs to rewrite more of a file than a bug fix requires — using 400 BigCodeBench problems with AST-level controlled corruptions. A single preservation-oriented instruction cuts excess Levenshtein distance from 0.195 to 0.131 and cognitive complexity by 26.6%, and RL fine-tuning generalizes best out-of-domain without overfitting to seen corruption patterns.
- **Theme:** Spec-driven development & formal verification for coding.
- **Relevance:** Formalizes a correctness property (minimality of the diff) that is exactly the kind of spec constraint agentic code-editors habitually violate.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04061

## 7. FlowBalance: Verifier-Grounded Self-Improvement from On-Policy Reasoning Experience
- **Authors:** Zixun Huang, Kishan Panaganti, Haitao Mi, et al.
- **Summary:** Fits a normalized distribution over full reasoning trajectories using GFlowNet-style trajectory balance, then reweights it with a verifier-derived group advantage — keeping self-guidance on winning rollouts, inverting it on losing ones, and disabling it when no outcome preference exists. On Qwen3-4B/8B math benchmarks, it beats FlowRL in average score, avoids OPSD's response-length collapse, and keeps a wider set of correct-strategy modes.
- **Theme:** Core LLM advances (reasoning + fine-tuning).
- **Relevance:** Concrete way to combine verifier signals with dense self-guidance without collapsing exploration — a live problem for reasoning post-training.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03241

## 8. Verify Before You Distill: Prompt-Level Teacher Gating for On-Policy Distillation (TGOPD)
- **Authors:** Zhiwei Zhang, Zechen Sun, Fei Zhao, et al.
- **Summary:** Adds a per-prompt reliability check to on-policy distillation: a small verifier-scored probe estimates whether the frozen teacher is trustworthy on this prompt, routing reliable prompts to dense distillation and the rest to verifier-grounded GRPO. Across math, code, and instruction-following at 4B and 35B, TGOPD beats vanilla OPD in all six single-domain settings and raises teacher-node GPU utilization from 9.8% to 78.9%.
- **Theme:** Core LLM advances (fine-tuning / PEFT-adjacent).
- **Relevance:** Turns "when to trust the teacher" into an explicit, verifier-mediated gating decision — the kind of correctness check spec-driven agents need at training time too.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02998

## 9. RISE: Recursive Improvement via Self-Extrapolating Policy Distillation
- **Authors:** Yang Li, Semih Yavuz, Shafiq Joty, et al.
- **Summary:** Fabricates a synthetic teacher by extrapolating the displacement between the current RLVR checkpoint and a trailing anchor in parameter or logit space, then runs on-policy distillation against that extrapolated teacher inside the same RLVR loop. The outcome-reward signal keeps the extrapolation grounded while the teacher supplies dense token-level guidance, yielding gains on math, STEM, code, and multi-turn tasks.
- **Theme:** Core LLM advances (fine-tuning).
- **Relevance:** Removes the need for a stronger external teacher during distillation — attractive for closed-loop agent training.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05295

## 10. Beneath the Surface of Chains-of-Thought: A Mechanistic Interpretation of Reasoning Operations in LLMs
- **Authors:** Seogyeong Jeong, Jaehui Hwang, Dongyoon Han, et al.
- **Summary:** Probes hidden states of reasoning models to test whether distinct CoT operations (e.g., substitute, compare, deduce) live in geometrically separable subspaces, finding that separability peaks in middle layers and that identical surface tokens acquire operation-specific representations depending on the surrounding step. The result is a representational-geometry account of what CoT tokens are actually doing inside the network.
- **Theme:** Core LLM advances (reasoning interpretability).
- **Relevance:** Empirical grounding for reasoning-operation types that spec-driven and agentic systems increasingly assume exist — useful for building targeted verifiers.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04753

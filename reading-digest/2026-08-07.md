## 1. HarnessOpt-Bench: Evaluating LLMs at Harness Optimization
Authors: Varun Ursekar, Apaar Shanker, Yash Maurya, et al.
Summary: The paper argues an LLM's real-world capability is co-determined by the scaffolding (tools, memory, prompting, retries) around it, and it introduces a benchmark that asks frontier models to *edit their own harness* under expensive, stochastic evaluation feedback. Each task gives a baseline harness plus a noisy scorer and measures whether the model can propose changes that improve end-task success without simply overfitting the scorer. Results identify a large gap between what models can build in a single edit vs. what they can improve when they must reason about noisy signal and sample budget.
Theme: 1 — Core LLM (agentic-system evaluation)
Relevance: Direct evaluation of an LLM's ability to *engineer* an agentic system, which the user tracks; also touches Theme 3 by treating harness config as a spec the model must optimize against a verifier.
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06301

## 2. AgentOPSD: Recursive Self-Distillation for Agentic Reinforcement Learning
Authors: Zi-Han Wang, Zhengxi Lu, Zhiyuan Yao, et al.
Summary: Long-horizon agent RL under verifiable rewards struggles to attribute credit to the few pivotal turns in a trajectory. AgentOPSD is critic-free: it aggregates token-level log-prob gaps into turn-level evidence and recursively updates a Bayesian belief over which turns caused success/failure, then self-distills the policy against that belief. The method reports gains on multi-tool agent benchmarks without training a separate value model.
Theme: 1 — Core LLM (agentic RL / reasoning)
Relevance: Practical credit-assignment recipe for tool-using agents, a recurring failure mode in agentic PEFT/RL pipelines.
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05987

## 3. OneDayAgent: Towards a Long-Horizon Harness for Autonomous Agents
Authors: Jingsheng Zheng, Xinyuan Fang, Jintian Zhang, et al.
Summary: The paper proposes a harness for "one-day" open-ended requests spanning work, study, and personal life. It decomposes tasks into bounded subtasks, maintains a compressed execution memory that survives context pressure, and adds a deliverable-verification step at the end of each subtask. Empirically it outperforms flat ReAct-style baselines on long-horizon everyday-life benchmarks where naive agents drift or truncate.
Theme: 1 — Core LLM (agentic-system evaluation, planning)
Relevance: Concrete design for the memory-bounded, verify-as-you-go loop that most production agent stacks are converging on.
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05013

## 4. EnvACE: Internalizing Environment Dynamics via World Rehearsal for Agentic Reinforcement Learning
Authors: Zishan Xu, Zhiyuan Yao, Yuxin Chen, et al.
Summary: Training agents that call real tools is expensive and simulators are hard to keep faithful. EnvACE replaces live environment interaction during RL with "world rehearsal" against a learned dynamics model of action→response, letting the policy internalize tool semantics before deployment. The paper reports competitive tool-use scores at a fraction of the environment-call budget.
Theme: 1 — Core LLM (agentic RL, fine-tuning)
Relevance: Directly addresses the interaction-cost bottleneck that limits agentic RL at scale.
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06197

## 5. Recursive Synthesis for Long-Horizon Terminal Tasks
Authors: Zhongzhi Li, Yucheng Shi, Zongxia Li, et al.
Summary: RST builds long-horizon terminal-agent training data recursively from a small pool of verified seed tasks: an extender adds new subgoals, and a realigned verifier confirms the compound task still admits an executable solution. This produces a large, execution-grounded curriculum without human annotation, and models trained on it transfer to unseen shells and multi-step CLI tasks better than models trained on scraped trajectories.
Theme: 1 — Core LLM (fine-tuning data, agentic-system evaluation)
Relevance: Terminal / coding-adjacent agent training data with a verifier-in-the-loop, closest to Theme 3 within the window.
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05466

## 6. From Economic Agents to Agentic Economies: A Systems Blueprint for Economic World Models
Authors: Jiale Han, Xiang Li, Jing Qian, et al.
Summary: A blueprint for multi-agent economic simulation using LLMs as heterogeneous agents with beliefs, preferences, and institutional rules. The paper proposes a six-level capability ladder from rule-based agents up to sim-to-real economic twins, and analyzes what coordination primitives (auctions, contracts, delegation) each level requires. It is more agenda-setting than results-driven, but the ladder is a useful evaluation lens.
Theme: 2 — Multi-agent frameworks & orchestration
Relevance: Rare systems-level treatment of multi-agent architecture and coordination protocols in an economic setting.
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06020

## 7. ToolArtist: Tool-Using Unified Multimodal Models for Agentic Image Generation
Authors: Jiahao Zhao, Xiaomin Yu, Zhongxiang Sun, et al.
Summary: Rather than a single monolithic diffusion model, ToolArtist trains a unified multimodal model to *orchestrate* tools (segmenters, inpainters, style modules, retrieval) across multiple reasoning steps toward complex image goals. The paper details the tool-choice / argument-binding scheme and shows gains on compositional prompts where end-to-end models fail.
Theme: 2 — Multi-agent frameworks & orchestration
Relevance: A concrete tool-orchestration architecture with a specification of role/tool assignment — transferable outside vision.
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04436

## 8. MASS: Multiplayer World Models with Authoritative Shared State
Authors: Ziqi Cai, Siqi Yang, Yimu Wang, et al.
Summary: Existing video world models fold state and view into one latent, which breaks under multiple simultaneous agents. MASS separates a "Logic Engine" that maintains authoritative shared world state from a "Rendering Engine" that produces per-agent views, so multiple agents see consistent updates to the same world. Experiments in multiplayer environments show sharply reduced state divergence versus monolithic baselines.
Theme: 2 — Multi-agent frameworks & orchestration
Relevance: Directly tackles the shared-state / communication problem in multi-agent settings — the architectural pattern generalizes beyond video.
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06257

## 9. Toward Skill-Native LLMs: Skill Entropy for Benchmarking and Training Long-Horizon Reasoning
Authors: Yinghui He, Ling Yang, Jiarui Liu, et al.
Summary: The paper argues long-horizon reasoning is fundamentally about switching between distinct skills mid-chain, and defines Skill Entropy as a scalar measure of how much switching a task demands. It releases Skill²-Bench, a 558-skill benchmark of cross-skill tasks, and shows that training with skill-entropy-shaped curricula improves generalization to unseen skill combinations.
Theme: 1 — Core LLM (reasoning, evaluation)
Relevance: A clean framing and benchmark for the compositional-reasoning axis that most current eval sets under-measure.
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05139

## 10. ABSeeker: Training Long-Horizon Search Agents via Answer-Backtracked Credit Assignment
Authors: Yijun Lu, Rui Ye, Jiajun Wang, et al.
Summary: In multi-step search agents (search → retrieve → verify → integrate), trajectory-level rewards give almost no signal at the individual step. ABSeeker's "ABC" backtracks from the final answer to score each intermediate step's marginal contribution, producing dense per-step supervision that trains stronger search policies than PPO with sparse outcome rewards on multi-hop QA benchmarks.
Theme: 1 — Core LLM (agentic RL, planning)
Relevance: Fine-grained credit assignment for search agents — a common practical bottleneck alongside AgentOPSD's turn-level scheme.
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05102

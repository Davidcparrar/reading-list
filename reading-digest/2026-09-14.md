# Reading digest — 2026-09-14

## 1. Beyond Solver Verdicts: Generative Reward Models for Autoformalization
- **Authors:** Vikash Singh, Debargha Ganguly, Aman Goel, et al.
- **Summary:** The paper isolates a failure mode in autoformalization pipelines it calls Verdict-Preserving-Unfaithfulness: a formal translation is judged correct because a solver returns the expected verdict even though the translated statement is not equivalent to the original. To catch this without a ground-truth oracle, the authors introduce Generative Verification, which scores reference-equivalence directly from an LM's own vocabulary distribution over translated forms. On their benchmark GenV reaches 0.961 AUROC, transfers zero-shot to unseen translators and formal styles, and adds 11.3 points of downstream accuracy when used to route test-time compute in an agentic coding-style setup.
- **Theme:** Spec-driven development & formal verification
- **Relevance:** Attacks the "solver said yes so we're done" failure that plagues neurosymbolic and spec-driven coding agents — a rare piece of infrastructure that lets an agent decide *whether* its formalization actually captures the specification, not just whether it type-checks.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11085

## 2. IdeaAMBIG: Benchmarking Implementation-Critical Gaps in Research-Idea Specifications
- **Authors:** Yiling Ma, Yilun Zhao, Sihong Wu, et al.
- **Summary:** IdeaAMBIG turns research-method sections into a testbed for whether an LLM can detect the underspecifications that block implementation, using 163 real-world gaps mined from reproducibility reports and GitHub issues plus 497 controlled synthetic gaps. Tasks are split into three competencies — flagging whether a spec is implementation-ready, localizing the defective span inside it, and proposing a clarifying edit. Across 13 models, defect localization is the bottleneck (9.6% on real-world gaps, but 80.6% when the defect location is handed in), and supplying a resolution lifts codification readiness from 14% to 98%.
- **Theme:** Spec-driven development & formal verification
- **Relevance:** Directly measures the "can an agent tell that this spec is not yet buildable?" question — squarely inside the spec-driven-development theme and rare in being anchored to real reproducibility failures rather than toy prompts.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10539

## 3. COBRA-Skills: Contextual Bandit-Guided Evolution for Agent Skill Optimization
- **Authors:** Pingchen Lu, Xiangyi Wang, Xiang Li, et al.
- **Summary:** COBRA-Skills reframes agent-skill improvement as a budgeted sequential-decision problem: candidate skills form a population that evolves from execution traces, and a contextual bandit decides which candidates to spend expensive rollouts on. New candidates are proposed via evidence-grounded evolution (targeted rewrites tied to specific past failures) rather than blind mutation. Across six heterogeneous agent benchmarks and three target models it comes out on top on average while cutting optimization cost by 55–58% versus SkillOpt and using only 50 unique optimization examples per benchmark, and it keeps working when the target model itself is used to generate and refine skills.
- **Theme:** Multi-agent frameworks & orchestration
- **Relevance:** A concrete, data-cheap recipe for growing an agent's tool/skill library — orchestration-adjacent work that is empirically strong rather than another framework proposal.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11682

## 4. Beyond Top-k Skill Retrieval: Diversity-Aware Skill Routing for LLM Agents
- **Authors:** Wang Wei, Tiankai Yang, Samyadeep Basu, et al.
- **Summary:** Diverse Skill Routing recasts agent-skill selection as a subset-selection problem rather than independent per-skill relevance scoring, because most real tasks need complementary tools while registries contain many redundant ones. A Determinantal Point Process with a query-residual diversity kernel penalizes redundant overlap between chosen skills but does not double-count when two skills merely share high query relevance. On the SkillRouter benchmark DSR improves recall and full-coverage over a strong pointwise reranker, with the largest gains on multi-skill queries.
- **Theme:** Multi-agent frameworks & orchestration
- **Relevance:** Reframes tool routing as complementary-set selection instead of top-k relevance — a small, transferable change that directly addresses a chronic waste of context budget in agent stacks.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05824

## 5. EvoSafeHarness: Evolving Model- and Domain-Specific Harnesses for Securing Agents
- **Authors:** Nanxi Li, Yingzi Ma, Yulong Cao, et al.
- **Summary:** EvoSafeHarness treats an agent's safety layer as something to *co-search* rather than write by hand: it jointly evolves a natural-language policy and the executable check code that enforces it, tailored to a specific base model and deployment domain. Iteration is driven by attack-success/utility feedback on adversarial benchmarks, so the harness converges to defenses that fit the target agent's actual failure modes. Attack success on DecodingTrust-Agent drops from 45.6% to 10.0%, and on AgentDojo it reaches 82.8% utility at 0.0% ASR — a substantially better safety-utility frontier than fixed expert-designed defenses.
- **Theme:** Multi-agent frameworks & orchestration
- **Relevance:** Rare treatment of the safety harness as first-class, model-specific agent infrastructure — useful counterpoint to the "one policy, all agents" default and directly relevant to hardening tool-using systems.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05903

## 6. DRG-MAPPO: Hierarchical Dynamic Role-Graph Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning for Cooperative Air Combat
- **Authors:** Junlin Liu, Chengwei Li, Yang Gao, et al.
- **Summary:** DRG-MAPPO couples relational graph attention over the battlefield state with dynamic role assignment (leader/supporter) inside a MAPPO framework, so the policy decides both *who plays which role* and *what maneuver to execute* each step. A target-priority auxiliary task pushes the group toward coordinated focus-fire behaviors, and the hierarchical split separates strategy from execution. Reported win rate is 87% in cooperative air-combat scenarios, above the flat MAPPO baselines the authors compare against.
- **Theme:** Multi-agent frameworks & orchestration
- **Relevance:** Concrete example of role-conditioned communication and hierarchical coordination in a multi-agent RL setup — orthogonal to LLM-agent frameworks but useful signal on role/graph structures that keep resurfacing there.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11155

## 7. An Open Recipe for IMO Gold: Training Nemotron for Olympiad Mathematics
- **Authors:** Ivan Moshkov, Stephen Ge, George Armstrong, et al.
- **Summary:** Starting from Nemotron 3 Ultra, the team documents an end-to-end post-training and inference recipe — SFT plus RL on olympiad-style proof data, then an iterative generate/verify/refine loop at test time — that scored 30/42 (gold medal) at IMO 2026 while working entirely in natural language, without a formal prover or external tools. The release includes checkpoints, training data, inference code, and a fresh 200-problem Nemotron-IMO-Bench, making the whole pipeline reproducible rather than a headline number. The paper is unusually careful about ablating what actually mattered between post-training and test-time search.
- **Theme:** Core LLM advances (reasoning, fine-tuning)
- **Relevance:** Rare fully-open recipe at genuine olympiad level, and — more usefully — a concrete study of the interaction between post-training and inference-time search rather than one or the other in isolation.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10712

## 8. Negative Self-Distillation: Learning to Reason by Avoiding Flaws
- **Authors:** Rongcan Pei, Zhepei Wei, Shuyao Xu, et al.
- **Summary:** NSD flips on-policy self-distillation: instead of pushing the model toward privileged correct traces, it collects the model's own flawed reasoning and trains the model to *diverge* from it, using a dynamic gate that fires only on reasoning-critical tokens so linguistic priors survive. This avoids the catastrophic degradation naive unlearning-style negatives cause when the loss touches every token. It consistently beats on-policy self-distillation baselines on the reasoning tasks the authors evaluate.
- **Theme:** Core LLM advances (reasoning fine-tuning)
- **Relevance:** A cleaner take on "learn from your own mistakes" that survives the usual token-level collateral damage — small, transferable idea that plugs into existing self-distillation stacks.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11699

## 9. Benchmark Radar: A Living Database and Search Engine for AI Benchmarks and Evaluation
- **Authors:** Koutian Wu, Junjie Zhou, Ergan Shang, et al.
- **Summary:** Benchmark Radar is a continuously updated catalog of AI benchmarks (LLM, agentic/tool-use, coding, reasoning, safety, and domain benchmarks) that fuses daily discovery from 37 feeds with mentions in model cards and technical reports, plus 12,916 recorded score observations across 790 records. It exposes score histories, saturation and adoption views, and a Pareto frontier of score against measured use, so a researcher can inspect *what* is being reported on *which* benchmark and how quickly it is saturating before committing to a new eval. The release ships a dashboard, CLI, downloadable evidence, and a worked example of a prior-art search when designing a new benchmark.
- **Theme:** Core LLM advances (agentic-system evaluation)
- **Relevance:** Meta-infrastructure that most agentic-eval work quietly needs — knowing which benchmarks are saturated, which are contested, and which are cited by the models we compare against.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11115

## 10. PLC-DPO: Posterior Label Correction in Noisy and Ambiguous Preference Optimization
- **Authors:** Boryeong Cho, Sumyeong Ahn, Se-Young Yun
- **Summary:** PLC-DPO takes seriously that real preference pairs contain reversed, weak, or genuinely tied labels and routes each pair at training time into a clean / flip / tie regime, using the calibrated policy–reference margin as online evidence. Rather than filtering suspicious pairs out, it corrects the *direction and strength* of the training signal, which is the mode where standard DPO quietly hurts itself. Across 57 dataset–model–benchmark cells PLC-DPO gets the best mean win rate against DPO (60.5 vs. 55.5 for the next-best method) and stays stable under injected noise and tie stress tests.
- **Theme:** Core LLM advances (fine-tuning / preference optimization)
- **Relevance:** Practical, drop-in fix for a well-known failure mode of DPO on messy preference data — the kind of alignment plumbing that quietly moves numbers.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30597

## 1. CoworkEnv: Synthesizing Training Environments for Long-Horizon Cowork Agents

- **Authors:** Zhongwei Xie, et al.
- **Theme:** Multi-agent frameworks & orchestration
- **Relevance:** Direct hit — a framework that programmatically synthesizes executable multi-agent "cowork" environments (shared workspaces, role-tool assignment, long-horizon handoffs) and uses them for both SFT and RL of collaborative agents. Fills the gap between static benchmarks and real coordination training.
- **Summary:** The authors build a generator that turns task specs into containerized multi-agent sandboxes with explicit roles, tool interfaces and success conditions, then use those sandboxes as the training signal for cooperating LLM agents. On their held-out long-horizon tasks, agents trained in the synthesized environments beat strong single-agent and prompted-multi-agent baselines, with the largest gains on tasks where handoff and shared-state tracking matter. The result argues that synthesized environments, not better prompts, are the limiting resource for multi-agent capability.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24960

## 2. Manga2Animate: An Agentic Framework for Faithful Manga Animation with Script and Storyboard Planning

- **Authors:** Yulun Liu, et al.
- **Theme:** Multi-agent frameworks & orchestration
- **Relevance:** Clean example of orchestration with specialized roles (planner, scriptwriter, storyboarder, animator, reviewer) and explicit panel-level correctness checks between stages — the "communication protocol between agents" category, applied to a hard creative task.
- **Summary:** Manga2Animate decomposes manga-to-animation into script, storyboard and animation stages, each run by a dedicated agent, with a reviewer agent that checks each stage's output against the source panels and sends grounded revision requests upstream. The pipeline reports higher faithfulness to the original panels and better narrative continuity than monolithic generation baselines on a 500-panel evaluation set. The paper's main contribution is the explicit stage/review protocol rather than the generative backbone.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26851

## 3. HarnessPro: Recursive Harness Self-Improvement with Pareto Optimization for Performance-Cost Trade-offs

- **Authors:** Zhuoshi Pan, ZhiYuan Feng, Jie Tang
- **Theme:** Multi-agent frameworks & orchestration (agent architecture / harness design)
- **Relevance:** Treats the agent harness itself — tool wiring, retry logic, planner/worker loop — as the artifact to optimize, label-free, with a Pareto front over task success and token cost. Directly on the "agent architectures" axis of theme 2.
- **Summary:** HarnessPro repeatedly proposes mutations to a coding-agent harness, scores them on held-out tasks with no human labels, and keeps a Pareto frontier over pass-rate and cost, finally exposing "lite / medium / heavy" variants for different budgets. On SWE-bench-style evaluations the heavy variant improves pass-rate while the lite variant matches a prior harness at a fraction of the tokens. The result is a concrete recipe for continuous, automatic improvement of the orchestration layer around a fixed base model.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26770

## 4. OpenVLHarness: Real World Visual Agentic Tool-Use with Multimodal Memory

- **Authors:** Yu Zhou, et al. (submitter bryanzhou008)
- **Theme:** Multi-agent frameworks & orchestration (tool assignment, memory as shared state)
- **Relevance:** A harness that gives a VLM agent persistent multimodal memory and a tool-use protocol, evaluated on 22 real-world tasks — hits "role/tool assignment" and "agent architectures" squarely.
- **Summary:** OpenVLHarness pairs a VLM with a structured multimodal memory store (images, OCR, prior tool outputs) and a dispatcher that selects among browsing, OS and vision tools per turn. On a 22-task real-world suite covering shopping, document work and desktop control, the memory-plus-dispatcher harness outperforms a prompt-only VLM and a text-only memory baseline. The paper argues that reusable visual memory, not stronger backbones, is what unlocks the longer-horizon tasks in their suite.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26860

## 5. AdaExploreBench: Benchmarking LLM Agents for Proactive Exploration and Test-time Adaptation in Evolving Environments

- **Authors:** JiayuJeff, et al.
- **Theme:** Core LLM — agentic-system evaluation
- **Relevance:** A benchmark that specifically targets what current agent evals miss: whether an agent *notices* that the environment's hidden rules have drifted and whether it gathers the right new evidence. This is agentic-system evaluation beyond fixed-task success rate.
- **Summary:** AdaExploreBench constructs tasks whose latent rules change part-way through an episode, without notifying the agent, and scores whether the agent actively probes for the shift and updates its policy. The reported finding is that frontier LLM agents do well on the pre-shift phase but systematically fail to detect drift and to design the right follow-up actions, with large gaps even for strong reasoning models. The benchmark gives a concrete lever for pushing on exploration and self-correction, not just raw accuracy.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25950

## 6. Remembering Has a Price: Learning Memory Worth under Resource Constraints

- **Authors:** AIGNLAI, et al. (TokenWorth)
- **Theme:** Core LLM — reasoning / agentic-system evaluation (lifelong memory as capability)
- **Relevance:** Treats "what to remember" as an optimization problem with a token budget, and evaluates on a lifelong-agent benchmark. Sits at the intersection of agent architecture and evaluation.
- **Summary:** The authors propose TokenWorth, a lightweight online scorer that estimates each memory item's expected future utility and keeps only the budget-paying ones for a lifelong LLM agent. On LifelongAgentBench the method reaches the highest task success at the lowest output-token count among the compared memory strategies, including fixed-size and uniform-decay baselines. The takeaway is a concrete, trainable alternative to ad-hoc memory-pruning heuristics that currently dominate agent frameworks.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26453

## 7. MemT-1.0: Towards Memory-Native Evolution

- **Authors:** Zhiyu Li, Zeyu Zhang, Junchi Yan, et al.
- **Theme:** Core LLM — architecture for reasoning / long-context
- **Relevance:** Not just a longer context window — the paper redesigns the computation so interaction history is a first-class, bounded in-model state, which has direct implications for agentic systems that currently rely on external retrieval.
- **Summary:** MemT-1.0 proposes a model family whose per-session state is a learned, fixed-size memory updated by the forward pass, rather than a growing KV cache or an external store. The reported systems result is per-session storage that is flat in dialogue length while matching or beating KV-cache baselines on long-horizon QA and instruction-following tasks, at lower serving cost. If it generalizes, this changes how agent frameworks should think about "memory" versus "context."
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26825

## 8. Scaling Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning for Brain Encoding

- **Authors:** Zhuofan Josh Ying, et al.
- **Theme:** Core LLM — fine-tuning / PEFT
- **Relevance:** Direct fit for the PEFT axis. Studies PEFT scaling behavior on frontier multimodal backbones, which is useful far beyond neuroscience: it is one of the few recent papers to run a careful adapter-rank × model-size sweep.
- **Summary:** The paper scales LoRA-style adapters on large multimodal foundation models for the task of predicting fMRI responses to naturalistic stimuli, and reports a clean monotone improvement with both backbone size and adapter rank up to the tested regime. The main methodological finding is that PEFT, not full fine-tuning, is what makes it feasible to study neural alignment at frontier-model scale, with practical cost curves readers can transfer to other PEFT-at-scale projects. Useful as a reference point for anyone running adapter sweeps on large multimodal models.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26802

## 9. Kronecker Delta Rule for Long Context Linear Attention

- **Authors:** anamariarobertap, et al.
- **Theme:** Core LLM — architecture (long-context reasoning)
- **Relevance:** Linear-attention variants are a crowded space; this one proposes a specific memory-revision rule claimed to be stable without extra normalization, which is the usual failure mode. Relevant to anyone building long-context agents.
- **Summary:** The paper introduces KronLSTM, a linear-attention/recurrent hybrid in which the recurrent memory is updated by a Kronecker-structured delta rule that constrains edits to a low-rank direction, giving a stability guarantee without the layernorm fixes other linear attentions need. On long-context language-modeling and retrieval benchmarks it holds up well past the training length, where several linear-attention baselines degrade. The result adds another credible point on the long-context frontier away from softmax attention.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26834

## 10. Competing Learning in Hybrid Architectures

- **Authors:** chen-yingfa, et al.
- **Theme:** Core LLM — architecture / training dynamics (reasoning capability)
- **Relevance:** A clean diagnosis of a failure mode in hybrid (recurrent + attention) models — recurrence learns retrieval faster and suppresses attention's gradient signal — with a staged-training fix. Useful for anyone training reasoning-capable hybrid models.
- **Summary:** The authors show empirically that in hybrid LMs, recurrence layers converge on in-context retrieval tasks far faster than softmax-attention layers, which starves the attention path of useful gradients and leaves it under-trained at convergence. They propose a staged training curriculum that temporarily disables the recurrent path, letting attention learn retrieval first, and report consistent gains in both retrieval accuracy and language-modeling perplexity up to 1B parameters. The paper is useful both as a cautionary tale about end-to-end training of mixed architectures and as a concrete training recipe.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26830

## 1. DynaFlow: Learning Free-Form Multi-Agent Orchestration
- **Authors**: Ruhao Liu, Qi Li, Shizun Wang, et al.
- **Summary**: DynaFlow treats workflow synthesis as an action for a main agent, so instead of orchestrating fixed sub-agents through a hand-designed graph the agent emits executable programs that sequence, parallelise, verify and exchange information between frozen sub-agents on the fly. The main agent is trained with SFT on distilled trajectories and then refined on traces filtered for unsupported premises, biased verification, and bad information flow. Across six math-, science-, and multi-hop benchmarks it hits 88.08% in Forced mode on a Qwen3.5-27B backbone, 10.9 points above the strongest baseline.
- **Theme**: 2 — multi-agent frameworks & orchestration
- **Relevance**: Direct hit on orchestration: the main agent *writes* the collaboration structure as a program, with verification steps baked in, which is where coordination protocols meet executable specifications.
- **Link**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34467

## 2. Skill^∞: Recursive Self-Improvement for Agent Skill Optimization
- **Authors**: Wangcheng Tao, Zhanglu Yan, Han Wu, Weng-Fai Wong
- **Summary**: The authors cast skill optimization itself as a skill: a meta-agent follows a meta-skill (diagnose traces, propose candidates, request independent reviews, allocate search budget) to improve task skills, and uses the same meta-skill to improve itself. The meta-skill is trained on multiple skill-optimization tasks and then transferred across domains, and both levels load through standard skill-discovery packages. On SkillsBench's 83 tasks it reaches 60.7% pass rate, versus 56.4% for human-curated skills and 54.5% for SkillEvolver.
- **Theme**: 2 — multi-agent frameworks & orchestration (meta-agent architecture)
- **Relevance**: Novel RSI-for-agents mechanism; relevant to role/tool assignment and self-organizing agent architectures, with a clean empirical comparison against hand-curated skills.
- **Link**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35434

## 3. AgentProofBench: Benchmarking Reusable Verifiers for Web Agents
- **Authors**: Jingjie Ning, Xueqi Li, Yi Lu, Yibo Kong
- **Summary**: AgentProofBench pairs a frozen acceptance rule (the "verifier" or spec) with execution replays from saved openings, measuring both whether the rule survives requirement changes (complete-panel/decision-replay tests) and whether it actually gates incorrect deliveries (deployment-state panel correctness). The resource covers 97 tasks, 79 families, and four web services, with DeepSeek Flash/Pro experiments showing paired reuse-rebuild BA intervals of [-5.47, 1.57] and [-10.16, 0.00] percentage points — i.e., well-written verifiers are reusable across requirement edits. All failed deliveries concentrate in assignments whose panels already flagged errors, giving a concrete signal of verifier value.
- **Theme**: 3 — spec-driven dev & formal verification for coding (adapted to web agents)
- **Relevance**: Closest paper in the window to a formal-verification lens on agent outputs: it treats the acceptance rule as a reusable specification and measures spec/implementation drift separately from end-task success.
- **Link**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34634

## 4. Beyond Benchmarks: Can Small Open-Weight Models Get Real Coding Work Done?
- **Authors**: Vaibhav Jain
- **Summary**: A systems retrospective (July–October 2026) that separates "a served model", "coherent text", "executed tool calls", and "code that passes hidden behavioral checks" as distinct outcomes, measured over 4,165 real coding-agent turns and 623 debug jobs. In two 15-task delegation studies with a fixed Claude Opus 5.5 parent and hidden behavioral checks on artifact contracts, direct coding hit 15/15 and 45/45 while delegation to quantized Qwen3-Coder-30B-A3B and Nemotron Cascade 2 workers trailed significantly; a post-hoc diagnostic also showed 19 of 36 artifact rejections were due only to parameter renames even though the hidden checks passed. The usable contribution is an evidence-qualified map of serving, agent protocol, and artifact validation — i.e., a spec-driven acceptance pipeline.
- **Theme**: 3 — spec-driven dev & formal verification for coding
- **Relevance**: Treats artifact contracts and hidden behavioral checks as the real spec, and exposes how brittle "benchmark-passed ⇒ work-done" is for small open-weight coders — exactly the correctness-of-agent-outputs angle for coding.
- **Link**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34241

## 5. Δτ-Bench: Pushing Agents Toward a Moving Capability
- **Authors**: Shuofei Qiao, Xuanguang Pan, Yijun Chen, et al.
- **Summary**: Δτ-Bench models evolving tool ecosystems: 1,588 validated multi-turn tasks across 42 domains and six successive ecosystem states produced by capability-preserving transformations, so the agent must notice when familiar tools disappear or are supplanted and revise its procedural knowledge accordingly. Even the strongest model/harness only reaches 42.3% task success. The authors also propose Steward, an ecosystem manager that incrementally maintains tools and procedural skills; it sustains success across changes with lower skill-maintenance overhead and better recovery.
- **Theme**: 2 — multi-agent frameworks & orchestration (also agentic-system evaluation)
- **Relevance**: One of the few benchmarks that stresses *orchestration under drift*, which is precisely where spec-like procedural knowledge and tool assignment meet. Steward is a concrete architecture, not just a benchmark.
- **Link**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34238

## 6. ProAS: A Progress-Aware Advantage Shaping Framework for Multi-Turn Agentic Reinforcement Learning
- **Authors**: Bowen Ding, Mingda Wan, Yuhan Chen, Jiyao Yuan, et al.
- **Summary**: ProAS attacks the uniform-credit-assignment flaw in GRPO-style critic-free RL by projecting each trajectory onto a dynamically evolving Agentic Reference Graph, then applying Masking Unreasonable Incentives and Asymmetric Penalty Redistribution to shape per-action advantages without re-introducing a critic. On Qwen2.5-1.5B/7B-Instruct it lifts group-based baselines to 91.9%/94.3% on ALFWorld and 66.7%/70.8% on WebShop, converging up to 2.44× faster and tolerating imperfect ARGs. The method is plug-and-play on top of existing GRPO variants.
- **Theme**: 1 — core LLM advances (RL fine-tuning for agents)
- **Relevance**: Addresses a well-known weakness in multi-turn agentic RL with a lightweight, reproducible method; useful lens on how to credit specific tool-use actions.
- **Link**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18683

## 7. S3AC: Stabilized, Selective, and Scalable Actor-Critic for LLM Reasoning
- **Authors**: Shuo Shen, Guangran Cheng, Chengqi Lyu, Xiangsen Wang, et al.
- **Summary**: S3AC provides a practical critic-training recipe for long-CoT RLVR, letting the critic deliver token-level advantages and a prompt-specific baseline that doubles as a difficulty estimate; Difficulty-Aware Prompt Sampling then uses that estimate plus outcome history to skip low-signal prompts and cut rollout waste. The approach scales across dense and MoE models from 1.5B to 397B, beating GRPO-X on group rollouts and sustaining single-rollout training at 35B where critic-free baselines regress.
- **Theme**: 1 — core LLM advances (reasoning, RL training)
- **Relevance**: Brings critics back into long-CoT RL with a method that scales, directly relevant to reasoning fine-tuning pipelines.
- **Link**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35518

## 8. Thinking-in-Bets at Test Time Reduces Sycophantic Revisions in LLM Agents
- **Authors**: Nghi D. Q. Bui
- **Summary**: A training-free technique in which the agent writes a private claim/checklist/confidence record *before* reading user feedback, then consults it to defend verified work while re-checking only genuinely uncertain pieces. Across SWE-Together, SWE-Interact, DeepSWE, Terminal-Bench, Agents' Last Exam, HLE, SycEval, and Vals Finance on five Gemini variants, pre-reply records nearly eliminate false reversals under mistaken pushback; gating the record to uncertain cases trims token overhead while fixing more real bugs. Even without user feedback, listing key claims and re-checking the weakest one improves accuracy and calibration.
- **Theme**: 1 — core LLM advances (reasoning / planning / agentic eval, with coding workloads)
- **Relevance**: Cheap test-time intervention with a strong failure-mode story (sycophancy under code review), and a lot of coverage on coding benchmarks relevant to spec-driven work.
- **Link**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34654

## 9. Continual Learning Exam: Benchmarking Long-Horizon RSI and Experiential Learning in LLM Agents
- **Authors**: Haoyu Zhao, Sitian Tu, Yuxuan Huang, Huichi Zhou, et al.
- **Summary**: CLE is a 600-level benchmark (120 tasks × five ladder levels across 89 roles and 12 domains) where each level requires experience accumulated in the preceding level, so agents must actually improve, not just repeat. Across 17 harness-model configurations, diagnostic feedback plus one revision yields a median +5.3-point gain, but the best system still clears only 47.7% of levels and finishes all five levels in just 23.3% of tasks. The dataset doubles as training data for post-training agents toward continual learning.
- **Theme**: 1 — core LLM advances (agentic-system evaluation)
- **Relevance**: A rigorous benchmark aimed squarely at the "agents don't actually learn over long horizons" gap, with human-authored tasks and rubric-based evaluation.
- **Link**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33956

## 10. Trustworthy Recursive Self-Improvement in the Era of Large Models: Foundation, Risks, Evaluation and Open Challenges
- **Authors**: Jiayue Pu, Ang Gao, Jinyi Bai, Qipeng Wang, et al.
- **Summary**: A survey that organises RSI into four themes (sustained improvement; constraint preservation and alignment; security and privacy; transparency and controllability) and three evaluation perspectives (improvement validity, constraint preservation, effective oversight), tracing the S1–S4 stages (diagnose, propose, evaluate, implement) and the recursive application to improvement mechanisms themselves. It flags how changes to improvement mechanisms can invalidate earlier verification results and reduce human oversight, and lays out longitudinal evaluation requirements.
- **Theme**: 1 — core LLM advances (agentic-system evaluation) with spill-over to 3
- **Relevance**: Useful map for the agentic-eval and verification side of this reading list — explicitly connects RSI updates to verifiable constraints and oversight, which is where spec-driven thinking will have to sit.
- **Link**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35193

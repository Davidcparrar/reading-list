# Reading digest — 2026-09-01

Found 8 paper(s) in the last 48h window.

## 1. PaperGym: Rubric-Centered Evolution for Research-Plan Generation
- Authors: Yuhan Wang, Zhengxi Lu, Yuchen Yan et al.
- Summary: PaperGym builds an RL environment for evaluating and improving research-planning agents by decoupling the plan-generation task from the rubric that judges it, so the reward can no longer be gamed by paraphrasing the source. Rubrics are extracted from published papers but paired with independently sourced questions, and each rollout is scored across many criteria rather than compressed to a single scalar. The result is a training and evaluation loop where a research-plan agent gets fine-grained credit assignment on multi-faceted rubrics, which the authors show shifts model behavior toward more verifiable planning.
- Theme: (1) Core LLM — planning + agentic-system evaluation
- Relevance: Directly targets planning and rubric-based agent evaluation, one of the priority sub-themes; introduces a reusable environment rather than a one-off benchmark.
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.31119

## 2. Scaling Large Reasoning Models beyond Human Supervision: A Path toward Superintelligence
- Authors: Zhiqin Yang, Jingwen Fu, Yuhan Liu et al.
- Summary: The paper studies how reasoning models trained with RLVR can keep improving once verifiable rewards run out, extending the framework to open-ended and agentic tasks where automatic grading is unreliable. It proposes to combine self-generated experience with weaker, noisy critics and shows scaling behavior when human supervision is removed from the loop. Their empirical setup argues that self-supervised reward shaping plus staged verifiable checks can extend the RLVR curve past its current ceiling.
- Theme: (1) Core LLM — reasoning
- Relevance: Squarely on core reasoning and the RLVR-beyond-verifiable-rewards frontier that is currently driving frontier LRM training.
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.31075

## 3. Does On-Policy Distillation Really Distill? From Noisy Teacher to Self-Improvement
- Authors: Yi Ding, Ruqi Zhang
- Summary: The authors instrument on-policy distillation to measure how noisy the teacher's per-token scoring actually is on trajectories the student produced, and find that noise grows with teacher capability, so much of the supervision the student receives is unreliable. They then show that the gains typically attributed to distillation are largely a self-improvement effect: the student improves even when the teacher signal is decorrelated from ground truth. The paper reframes OPD as a bootstrapped self-training method with weak teacher regularization, which changes recipe choices for post-training.
- Theme: (1) Core LLM — fine-tuning
- Relevance: Rigorous, method-level analysis that changes practical fine-tuning intuition; strong empirical results.
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.31046

## 4. Normalized Low-Rank Adaptation
- Authors: Jiale Kang, Ziyin Yue, Zheng Zhan et al.
- Summary: NoRA analyzes why LoRA's early optimization is dominated by the down-projection (because the up-projection is zero-initialized) and introduces a normalization applied to the down-projection during training to stabilize the effective step size. The method is a drop-in modification with no extra parameters and improves both convergence and final accuracy over vanilla LoRA on standard PEFT benchmarks. The paper also gives a spectral argument for why the normalization prevents the down-projection from dominating the update geometry.
- Theme: (1) Core LLM — PEFT
- Relevance: Small, targeted PEFT method with clear ablations; the kind of drop-in change that gets adopted.
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.31036

## 5. On the Design of Qwen3.8-Next Architecture: Evaluation, Efficiency, and Training Stability
- Authors: Zihan Qiu, Zekun Wang, Xiao Li et al.
- Summary: This report documents the architecture, ablations, and training-stability tricks behind Qwen3.8-Flash-Next, a 125B-parameter sparse MoE with 6B activated tokens plus an off-accelerator n-gram embedding table. The authors show they match or trail a 397B-A17B predecessor on 14 pre-training benchmarks at roughly a third of the activated parameters and a ninth of the training FLOPs, and describe the layer-wise token-mixing scheme that enables the compute savings. It is an unusually candid design report on how a frontier lab is trading dense width for sparsity plus retrieval-style embedding memory.
- Theme: (1) Core LLM — architecture / training
- Relevance: Direct look at how a frontier lab is architecting the next generation of MoE models; unusually detailed ablations.
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30320

## 6. Learning to Evaluate Before Improving: Automatic Rubric Induction for Automatic Research Agents
- Authors: Xuehai Wang, Haowei Qin, Tongxin Liu et al.
- Summary: AutoSciRub induces evaluation rubrics automatically from open-ended scientific tasks so that autonomous research agents can be scored before being asked to improve, addressing the fact that end-to-end research tasks rarely specify success criteria. The method pairs task descriptions with LLM-generated rubric candidates that are filtered by consistency checks against reference workflows, and shows the induced rubrics correlate with expert judgments while surfacing missing analyses. Using these rubrics as a training signal lifts agent performance on downstream research tasks.
- Theme: (1) Core LLM — agentic-system evaluation
- Relevance: Complements PaperGym above with rubric induction; agent evaluation is a priority sub-theme.
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.31076

## 7. CAST: Critique-Aware Supervision for Training Reliable Long-Horizon Tool-Calling Agents
- Authors: Amir Saeidi, Zehua Zhang, Rishitosh Singh et al.
- Summary: CAST addresses the reliability problem in long-horizon tool-calling agents by attaching a step-level critique model that must approve an action before it is executed, so irreversible mistakes (e.g., wrong refund) are intercepted before commit. The critique is trained jointly with the actor from mixed successful and unsafe trajectories, giving dense per-step supervision instead of only end-of-task reward. Across long-horizon interactive benchmarks the approach substantially cuts the rate of unrecoverable failures while preserving task success.
- Theme: (2) Multi-agent frameworks & orchestration — tool-calling, role assignment (actor + critic)
- Relevance: Concrete multi-agent orchestration pattern (actor/critic with veto) for high-stakes tool-calling; directly on-theme.
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30147

## 8. CogEvol: Towards Efficient and Reliable Learning Environment Generation
- Authors: Shangqing Tu, Daniel Zhang-Li, Yucheng Wang et al.
- Summary: CogEvol trains a specialized model family that turns a course brief into a finished learning artifact — structured-JSON slides or self-contained interactive HTML — in a single forward pass, replacing multi-turn agent scaffolds. The team reports median latencies of 17s for slides and 59s for interactive pages across 220k production requests, with reliability enforced by a data pipeline that turns real user rejections into training signal. The paper is interesting less for the domain than for showing where an in-model specialist beats an orchestrated agent loop on cost and reliability at scale.
- Theme: (2) Multi-agent frameworks & orchestration — the alternative to orchestration
- Relevance: Useful counter-datapoint on when to collapse an agent pipeline into a specialist model; grounded in production traffic.
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30968

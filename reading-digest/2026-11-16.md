## 1. Aligning AI Teams
- **Authors:** Siddarth Srinivasan, Morgan Jane Matthews, Jascha Sohl-Dickstein, et al.
- **Summary:** The paper shows that safety training which keeps a single LLM aligned degrades sharply once the same model is deployed as a team of cooperating agents, and attributes the slip chiefly to diffusion of responsibility across the team rather than to communication artifacts. Two cheap interventions — appointing a designated safety-lead agent, and having the coordinator publish an explicit safety specification before the team acts — largely restore single-agent-level refusal and harm-avoidance behavior. The authors release magelab, a framework for running and probing these multi-agent alignment experiments.
- **Theme:** Multi-agent frameworks & orchestration
- **Relevance:** Directly addresses whether single-agent alignment carries into orchestrated teams, isolates a specific coordination failure mode, and proposes concrete role/spec-based fixes — hits the coordination-and-role-assignment heart of Theme 2.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17820

## 2. Do Verifier Judgments Survive Abstraction? Measuring Verification Transport for LLM Agent Evaluation
- **Authors:** Disha Sheshanarayana, Sai Linisha Reddy Goli
- **Summary:** The paper defines "verification transport" as the question of whether an LLM verifier's pass/fail judgment on an agent trajectory is preserved when that trajectory is lossily summarized or re-represented. Across standard agent benchmarks the authors measure a verdict-flip rate near 31% between raw and abstracted traces, and show that re-injecting provenance pointers (which raw step produced which summarized claim) recovers most of the lost agreement. The result argues that current LLM-as-verifier pipelines are leaking correctness signal through the abstraction step itself, not through model capability.
- **Theme:** Spec-driven development & formal verification for coding/agent outputs
- **Relevance:** Treats verifier judgment as a property that must transport through a formal abstraction, bringing a verification-theoretic lens to LLM agent evaluation — exactly the "define correctness of agent outputs" angle.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18471

## 3. ModHarness-RL: Recasting Harnesses as Learnable and Evolving Programs for Agentic RL
- **Authors:** Zelin He, Siyu Zhu, Jacob Helwig, et al.
- **Summary:** ModHarness-RL stops treating the scaffolding around an RL-trained agent (context managers, tool wrappers, retry/steer programs) as a fixed template and instead represents the harness as a small library of modifiable programs that co-evolve with the policy during training. During optimization the method proposes mutations to the harness — compressing history, steering next-action choice, inserting new tools — and keeps the ones that improve return, so the agent's effective context changes as its competence grows. The paper reports that this moving harness outperforms a fixed-best harness in most of its benchmark settings.
- **Theme:** Core LLM advances (agentic-system fine-tuning / orchestration)
- **Relevance:** Reframes "agent architecture" itself as a tunable object instead of a hyperparameter — a fresh methodological datapoint for how to train and evaluate agentic systems.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18533

## 4. HerAgent: A Block-Level Checkpointing Harness for Coding-Agent Environment Setup
- **Authors:** Xiang Li, Siyu Lu, Federica Sarro, et al.
- **Summary:** HerAgent attacks the under-studied but failure-heavy step of making a coding agent's sandbox ready to run (installing dependencies, building, pulling datasets) by snapshotting the environment at the granularity of semantic setup blocks rather than per-command or per-task, so a failing step can be re-attempted without redoing the successful prefix. The harness decomposes setup scripts into these blocks, executes them under isolation, and restores the last valid checkpoint on failure. Compared with six prior coding-agent baselines it raises setup success rates while cutting token and compute costs.
- **Theme:** Spec-driven development & formal verification for coding (coding-agent orchestration with recovery guarantees)
- **Relevance:** Makes a clear coding-agent correctness contract — "block-level progress is a checkpointable unit" — and shows the empirical win; close neighbor to spec-driven agent design for code.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18443

## 5. Interactive Program Induction with Language Model Priors
- **Authors:** Robin Shing Moon Chan, Ryan Cotterell
- **Summary:** The paper casts program induction with LM priors as Bayesian inference over programs consistent with a few input-output examples, and proposes a greedy query-selection rule that picks the next user-provided example to maximally split the posterior. On the CodeARC benchmark this interactive protocol recovers correct programs with fewer examples than passive or random-query baselines of the same LM backbone. It is a principled way to let a program-synthesis agent say "ask the user to run this one input" instead of guessing.
- **Theme:** Spec-driven development & formal verification for coding
- **Relevance:** Treats examples as a specification that grows by informative queries — a Bayesian formalization of spec-driven program synthesis with LLM priors, directly on Theme 3.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18485

## 6. ARCHER: From Rationale Imitation to Executable Reasoning in Small Language Models
- **Authors:** Dhruv Dixit, Khushi Patel, Congcong Xu, et al.
- **Summary:** ARCHER moves small language models beyond imitating free-form rationales by training them to emit executable action traces — in particular tool and calculator calls whose results get fed back into the chain — so arithmetic and lookup steps are grounded instead of fabricated. The training signal comes from distilling such traces from a stronger model and then filtering on whether the actions actually execute cleanly. On math-reasoning benchmarks the resulting small model beats a tool-augmented same-size baseline.
- **Theme:** Core LLM advances (reasoning, fine-tuning/PEFT)
- **Relevance:** Concrete recipe for turning small-model "rationales" into reliably-executable reasoning — a tractable direction for grounded reasoning at small scale.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18494

## 7. Imitation Bootstrapped Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning
- **Authors:** Tony H. Zhang, Connor Mattson, Daniel S. Brown
- **Summary:** The paper lifts the single-agent imitation-bootstrapped RL recipe (train a demo policy, then bias on-policy RL toward it) into the cooperative multi-agent regime, where naive extensions break because each agent's bootstrap policy sees only its own local demos. The authors introduce a team-aware bootstrapping variant that conditions imitation on joint rollouts and show it learns faster than pure-demo and pure-RL baselines across four cooperative benchmarks. The gains hold with modest demonstration budgets, which is the regime that usually blocks MARL from demos.
- **Theme:** Multi-agent frameworks & orchestration
- **Relevance:** Practical training-side contribution for cooperative teams; useful counterpart to communication/role-structure papers in Theme 2.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18543

## 8. Training Reasoning Models in Simulation to Natively Use Real Robots
- **Authors:** Jiaming Tang, Zhuoyang Zhang, Junxian Guo, et al.
- **Summary:** The paper trains a 27B open model with RL entirely in simulation on manipulation tasks, but conditions the policy on natural-language reasoning traces so that the learned "reasoning + action" interface transfers zero-shot to a real dual-arm robot. On unfamiliar real-world setups the policy succeeds in roughly 60% of trials without any real-robot training data, outperforming both pure-RL and pure-VLM baselines. The result is evidence that reasoning conditioning (not just visual domain randomization) is what makes sim-to-real transfer work for language-grounded manipulation.
- **Theme:** Core LLM advances (reasoning / planning)
- **Relevance:** Freshest datapoint on reasoning-model RL as a transfer mechanism rather than an inference-time trick — bears on how planning-capable models are actually trained.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18562

## 9. Can Mechanistic Interpretability Help Explain LLM Agent Failures? A Case Study on Web Element Hallucination
- **Authors:** Mohamed Aghzal, Mor Geva, Gregory J. Stein, et al.
- **Summary:** The paper probes a specific failure mode of web-navigating LLM agents — proposing clicks on elements that do not exist on the current page — and asks whether standard mechanistic-interpretability tools (linear probes, activation patching, attention-head steering) can predict or prevent it. The finding is partial: probes detect many hallucination cases pre-generation but steering interventions only partially reduce them, and the authors characterize which internal signals transfer across page layouts and which are page-specific. The paper reads as an honest map of where current interpretability toolkits stop being enough to debug a deployed agent.
- **Theme:** Core LLM advances (agentic-system evaluation)
- **Relevance:** Rare paper that tries to connect interpretability directly to an operational agent failure instead of a synthetic probe task — useful calibration for interpretability-driven agent evaluation.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18514

## 10. TradeGauntlet: Evaluating Compliance Controls for Autonomous Trading Agents
- **Authors:** Sandip Nandi, Aishwarya Bose, Apurba Bose
- **Summary:** TradeGauntlet is a replayable order-book simulator that stress-tests the compliance layer (not just the P&L) of autonomous trading agents under scripted scenarios covering spoofing, wash trading, layering, and market-abuse edge cases. The harness runs the agent against a hidden rule set and measures both violation incidence and detection latency of the compliance controls wrapped around it. The paper's headline finding is that an agent producing zero live alerts is not evidence of compliance, because many violations are only detectable via counterfactual replay.
- **Theme:** Spec-driven development & formal verification (compliance specs for autonomous agents)
- **Relevance:** Treats compliance rules as an executable specification an agent can be audited against, and shows that passive monitoring under-counts violations — on-theme for defining correctness of agent behavior under rules.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18493

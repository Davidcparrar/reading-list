## 1. Don't Just Trust Your Memory: Evidence-Linked Memory Reduces Memory-Induced Degradation in LLM Agents

- **Authors:** Yukyeong Lee, Yumin Choi, Jinheon Baek, et al.
- **Summary:** Treats agent memory not as free-text lessons but as records bound to the trajectory they were extracted from, so the agent can re-check a past lesson against its source before reusing it. The paper shows that unlinked memory in prior agent-memory systems actively hurts task success on longer horizons; with evidence links the agent verifies or revises lessons at retrieval time and improves across five agentic benchmarks.
- **Theme:** 1 — Core LLM / agentic-system evaluation (memory).
- **Relevance:** A clean diagnosis of why "learn-from-experience" agent memories regress on retrieval, plus a concrete fix that is cheap to wire into existing agent loops.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14320

## 2. Sable: A Small-Field Sumcheck Argument for Bit-Exact Inference of Large Language Models

- **Authors:** Tomer Solberg
- **Summary:** A single-GPU proof system that produces a succinct cryptographic argument that a given integer-quantized LLM really produced the exact token sequence it claims, bit for bit. The sumcheck is run over a small field tuned to the quantization scheme, so verification is cheap enough that a client can run it without re-executing the model.
- **Theme:** 3 — Formal verification of model outputs.
- **Relevance:** The closest thing this window has to a spec-driven correctness primitive for an LLM-as-service: cryptographic proof that "this is exactly what the advertised model computed." Directly relevant to any coding-agent pipeline that depends on trusting a remote model's outputs.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14083

## 3. ARE-bench: Benchmarking Agents' Research Ability in Real-World Researcher Sessions and Workspaces

- **Authors:** Jiahang Lin, Wanying Ren, Xuanjing Huang
- **Summary:** Runs research agents inside snapshots of real researcher workspaces (open files, prior decisions, project state) and scores whether the agent respects constraints set earlier in the session rather than only whether it satisfies the immediate request. The benchmark finds that top agents routinely drop earlier constraints as tasks compound, exposing a coordination failure that single-turn benchmarks miss.
- **Theme:** 2 — Multi-agent / agent orchestration (evaluation of agent architectures).
- **Relevance:** Shifts agent evaluation from one-shot tool-use to persistent-state research workflows, which is the regime most multi-agent systems actually fail in.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14364

## 4. LEASH: Stay Safe in Agentic Robotics

- **Authors:** Qinfeng Zhu, Weiguang Zhao, Ke Sun, et al.
- **Summary:** Proposes a three-gate harness — task gate, action gate, commit gate — that sits around a foundation-model agent and blocks or rewrites unsafe plans before they reach the actuator layer, along with a RoboRisk benchmark for measuring harm. Across several foundation models the gated architecture cuts harmful executions while leaving task completion largely intact.
- **Theme:** 2 — Multi-agent / agent architecture (role-separated safety layer).
- **Relevance:** A concrete pattern for role-assignment and typed check-points around an LLM policy; transfers well to non-robotic agent systems where "commit" and "action" gates are a natural control-flow seam. (Border of window: submitted 2026-11-09.)
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14115

## 5. Tree-Ising: Repairing Language Models with Bit-Level Ising Edits

- **Authors:** Hadi Salloum, Ammar Ali
- **Summary:** Frames the problem of "fixing a specific reasoning failure in a frozen LLM" as a sparse discrete optimization over BF16 weight bits, solved with Ising / QUBO solvers rather than gradient descent. On math benchmarks it matches or beats an agent-driven edit baseline while touching far fewer weights and consuming no LLM tokens.
- **Theme:** 3 — Formal methods applied to LLM repair.
- **Relevance:** A rare gradient-free, specification-anchored view of model editing: the "spec" is the failing example set, and the solver produces a provably minimal edit. Interesting bridge from formal methods into LLM maintenance.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14210

## 6. A Theory of Policy Optimization in Post-Training: Coverage Collapse and Reference Resets

- **Authors:** Audrey Huang, Nan Jiang, Sham M. Kakade
- **Summary:** Analyzes why KL-to-reference RL post-training (PPO/GRPO-style) gradually loses useful responses, identifying a coverage-collapse mechanism where the regularizer anchors to an increasingly narrow policy. The authors show that periodically resetting the reference to the current policy preserves coverage without destabilizing training, with matching theoretical and empirical results.
- **Theme:** 1 — Core LLM / fine-tuning theory.
- **Relevance:** A foundational post-training result from a highly-cited group, with a one-line practical change (reset the reference) that applies to most RLHF / GRPO pipelines in use today.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14467

## 7. SteerDistill: Quantizing LLM Agents via Closed-Loop Teacher Steering

- **Authors:** Genming Cui, Xiangyu Li, Zixu Hao, et al.
- **Summary:** Trains a low-bit student agent by letting a full-precision teacher intervene only on trajectories where the student fails, then distilling the corrected rollout; after training the student runs alone. The paper reports the quantized agent landing within about one point of BF16 on agentic benchmarks, where naive PTQ collapses.
- **Theme:** 1 — Core LLM / PEFT-style compression for agents.
- **Relevance:** Targets the right failure mode: it's not perplexity that breaks under quantization, it's the agent's tool-use loop, and the paper measures and fixes that directly.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13410

## 8. Are Dense Rewards Worth Their Tokens in GRPO?

- **Authors:** Vikash Singh, Weicong Chen, Debargha Ganguly, et al.
- **Summary:** Under a fixed compute budget, compares standard outcome-only GRPO against variants that spend tokens generating Monte-Carlo dense process rewards on intermediate steps. Dense rewards underperform plain GRPO almost everywhere; a lighter intervention — biasing prompt sampling toward problems whose predicted success is near 1/2 — produces small but consistent gains.
- **Theme:** 1 — Core LLM / RL fine-tuning for reasoning.
- **Relevance:** A useful negative result against a popular direction (process rewards) with a cheap alternative that practitioners can adopt today.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13560

## 9. MMDM: Measuring Massive Multitask Decision Making

- **Authors:** Junhao Shen, Qing Zong, Yue Guo, et al.
- **Summary:** A 5,448-question benchmark that forces a model to commit to a decision in one shot across 16 categories, with heavy emphasis on cases with interdependent options, side-constraints, and multi-select answers. Current frontier models do well on knowledge-only items but drop sharply when constraints couple the options.
- **Theme:** 1 — Core LLM / agentic-system evaluation (planning under constraints).
- **Relevance:** Isolates the planning-under-constraints failure mode without entangling it with tool-use noise, which makes it a useful ablation benchmark for reasoning work.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14309

## 10. EVAThink: Evidence Sufficiency-Guided Training for Adaptive Thinking in Video Understanding

- **Authors:** Changti Wu, Ruizhe Fan, Bin Wang, et al.
- **Summary:** A two-stage RL recipe (6.7K samples) that trains a VLM to decide, per query, whether the sampled frames are sufficient to answer directly or whether explicit chain-of-thought is worth the tokens. The model learns to skip reasoning on trivial queries and keeps or extends reasoning when evidence is thin, trading latency without hurting accuracy.
- **Theme:** 1 — Core LLM / reasoning (adaptive compute).
- **Relevance:** A concrete, small-data recipe for "reason-when-it-helps" gating; the signal (evidence sufficiency) ports to text-only agent settings where tool outputs play the same role as sampled frames.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13907

## 1. Agent Safety Should Be a Runtime Contract
- **Authors:** Albus W. Ng, Yi Han, Jusheng Zhang, et al.
- **Summary:** The paper argues that alignment training alone cannot police agent behavior and reframes safety as a runtime contract combining preventive gates (sandboxes, permission checks, output filters, trajectory monitors) with evidential requirements (test runs, log captures, file diffs attached to submissions). It formalizes an Agent Trajectory Schema and an Evidence Chain as the core auditable units and backs the argument with a survey of 52 documented agent incidents, a 32-case false-completion audit, an audit of 12 public agent systems, and a title-level scan of 28,560 conference papers. The conclusion is that the enforceable unit of safety is the trajectory-with-checkable-evidence, not the model weights.
- **Theme:** Spec-driven development / formal verification for agent outputs
- **Relevance:** The most direct fit for spec-driven correctness of agent outputs in the window — it defines a checkable contract format and the evidence artifacts a coding agent must produce to be considered correct.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11274

## 2. Why Does CLAUDE.md Keep Growing? Catastrophic Remembering in Agentic Coding
- **Authors:** Kushal Chakrabarti, et al.
- **Summary:** The paper diagnoses "catastrophic remembering" in agentic coding — the tendency of instruction files (CLAUDE.md and equivalents) to accumulate stale rules until they degrade instruction-following. It proposes writing prompt comments as outcome-grounded reasoning rather than raw rules, and reports that this discipline shrinks excess instruction size from 211.3% to 1.4% while lifting instruction-following correctness by 11.6 percentage points. The result is a concrete spec-hygiene protocol for coding agents whose behavior is governed by living instruction documents.
- **Theme:** Spec-driven development / formal verification for agent outputs
- **Relevance:** Treats the instruction file as the spec that governs a coding agent and shows how spec quality causally moves correctness — squarely on the third priority theme.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11095

## 3. Mechanist: AI as a Scientific Instrument for Discovering the Mechanisms of Intelligence
- **Authors:** Mengru Wang, Junfeng Fang, Shuofei Qiao, et al.
- **Summary:** Mechanist is an agentic system that pursues autonomous mechanistic-interpretability research by grounding its work in a curated knowledge graph of about 13,000 papers and orchestrating specialized sub-agents through the discovery loop. The system demonstrably progresses from identifying novel model behaviors to intervening on and controlling them, closing the loop from observation to causal manipulation. It illustrates a concrete multi-agent orchestration pattern where a shared knowledge substrate coordinates role-differentiated agents.
- **Theme:** Multi-agent frameworks & orchestration
- **Relevance:** A worked example of KG-mediated coordination among role-specialized agents for open-ended research — non-trivial architecture, not a survey.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12036

## 4. Persistent Recursive Worlds Enable Autonomous Software Evolution
- **Authors:** Beichen Huang, Zhenyu Liang, Bowen Zheng, et al.
- **Summary:** Genesis flips the usual framing of long-horizon coding by making the *project* persistent and letting agents come and go, so state, dependencies, and history live in the world rather than in any agent's context. The authors validate it by building a Rust-based C compiler across 250k tracked lines of code in 120 hours at a reported \$44 in inference cost. The architecture speaks directly to how a coordination substrate — not agent identity — carries long-lived engineering work.
- **Theme:** Multi-agent frameworks & orchestration
- **Relevance:** A concrete orchestration paradigm for coding agents at real scale, with a documented artifact and cost — highly relevant to agentic-coding orchestration.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10450

## 5. Spark-to-Paper: End-to-End Research Paper Generation as a Composable Skill
- **Authors:** Zhuoyang Qian, Biao Wu, Yiran Wang, et al.
- **Summary:** Spark-to-Paper packages the paper-writing pipeline as composable skills embedded inside a coding assistant, sharply separating model-judgment steps from deterministic operations like citation resolution and figure editing. Empirically the system reports 99.5% citation validity, 96.4% figure editability, and 92% fabrication detection, indicating that the skill decomposition — not raw model capability — carries most of the reliability. It is a useful data point on how to structure multi-step agent workflows so failure modes stay attributable and fixable.
- **Theme:** Multi-agent frameworks & orchestration
- **Relevance:** A clean pattern for splitting deterministic tool calls from model judgment inside a single agent-driven pipeline — a reusable orchestration primitive for coding-adjacent agents.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11924

## 6. MBA: Multimodal Benchmark and Agents for Real-World Business Ideation
- **Authors:** Hojun Choi, Jaeyo Shin, Suin Lee, et al.
- **Summary:** MBA introduces a 30K-sample multimodal benchmark across six ideation domains and companion agents (MBA-b and MBA-k) trained with explicit creativity and feasibility reward signals. The setup separates ideation quality into orthogonal axes and evaluates coordinated multi-agent behavior on realistic business problems. The benchmark and reward design offer a template for evaluating multi-agent systems on open-ended generative tasks.
- **Theme:** Multi-agent frameworks & orchestration
- **Relevance:** Fills a real evaluation gap for multi-agent generative work with a decomposed reward — useful even outside the ideation domain.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11616

## 7. AI4AI at Test-Time: Strong-to-Weak Capability Transfer via Harnesses
- **Authors:** Cheng Qian, Wenting Zhao, Liangwei Yang, et al.
- **Summary:** The paper investigates whether a strong model, acting as an author of inference-time harnesses, can lift a weaker model without any parameter updates on the weaker side. The authored harnesses combine deterministic code, task routing, and output-format enforcement, and the authors report near-doubling of target-model performance across several benchmarks. The result is a clean framing of test-time capability transfer as a compiler problem rather than a distillation problem.
- **Theme:** Core LLM advances (reasoning, planning, agentic-system evaluation)
- **Relevance:** Directly relevant to agentic-system evaluation and to reasoning-at-inference-time — reframes what "improving a model" can mean at deploy time.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12307

## 8. Self-Evolving Embodied Agents via Skill-Harness Evolution
- **Authors:** Peidong Wang, Zhiming Ma, Ying Chang, et al.
- **Summary:** SHAPER is a train-free adaptation framework for embodied agents that keeps the underlying model frozen and instead evolves two things over deployment: a library of reusable skills and the context-code harness that dispatches them. Optimization operates over the harness and skill set rather than weights, and the authors show that this joint search yields continual self-improvement without any gradient steps. The paper argues that most of the adaptation signal available to an agent lives in its harness, not its parameters.
- **Theme:** Core LLM advances (reasoning, planning, agentic-system evaluation)
- **Relevance:** A clean counterpoint to weight-based adaptation and a practical recipe for evolving agent scaffolding under load.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11350

## 9. ToolHazard: Scaling Adversarial Environments for Security Evaluation and Alignment of LLM-based Agents
- **Authors:** Yutao Mou, Pengfei Yang, Zhe Yin, et al.
- **Summary:** ToolHazard synthesizes adversarial tool-augmented environments at scale to stress-test LLM agents against indirect prompt injection and tool-abuse attacks. The evaluation exposes substantial residual vulnerabilities across current agents and shows that alignment data collected in these synthetic environments transfers — measurably improving security on held-out benchmarks while preserving benign task utility. The paper doubles as an environment-generation framework and a security-alignment recipe.
- **Theme:** Core LLM advances (agentic-system evaluation)
- **Relevance:** Combines a scalable evaluation harness with an actionable alignment intervention — high-signal for anyone building tool-using agents.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11878

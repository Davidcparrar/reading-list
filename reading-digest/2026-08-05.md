## 1. TurnSight: Turn-Level Hindsight Self-Distillation for Tool-Integrated Reasoning
- **Authors:** Changle Qu, Sunhao Dai, Hengyi Cai, et al.
- **Summary:** TurnSight proposes distilling a tool-using LLM at the granularity of individual dialog turns rather than whole trajectories, using hindsight-labeled per-turn signals to teach the model which intermediate steps mattered. This gives fine-grained credit assignment for long-horizon tool-integrated reasoning, where trajectory-level rewards normally wash out the impact of specific tool calls. Reported gains show that per-turn hindsight teaches better tool selection and recovery behavior than trajectory-level baselines.
- **Theme:** (1) Core LLM — reasoning / fine-tuning for agentic tool use.
- **Why it made the cut:** Directly attacks the credit-assignment problem in agentic reasoning fine-tuning, which is squarely on-thesis for both themes 1 and 2.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04007

## 2. LongHorizon-Harness: Advancing Long-Horizon Agents for Real-World Tasks
- **Authors:** Ziyu Ma, Hailang Huang, Shun Zou, et al.
- **Summary:** Proposes a task-state management framework around a Manage–Execute–Audit loop: the Manager decomposes and tracks sub-tasks, the Executor runs them, and the Auditor verifies each step before state is committed. The explicit auditor role is essentially a per-step correctness check inside the orchestrator, and the paper reports improvements across multiple long-horizon agent benchmarks by keeping the run-time task state consistent under drift.
- **Theme:** (2) Multi-agent frameworks & orchestration.
- **Why it made the cut:** A concrete role/communication protocol (Manager/Executor/Auditor) for long-horizon coordination — the exact shape this theme is about.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01964

## 3. PAST-Bench: Benchmarking the Foundations of Recursive Self-Improvement in Personal Agents
- **Authors:** Shuhan Xue, Zixin Ding, Yichen Shen, et al.
- **Summary:** Introduces a benchmark of 26 scenarios that isolates whether persistent agents actually get better from retained experience, decoupling that from base-model capability. Scenarios are structured so an agent must reuse past interactions to succeed, and the harness measures whether performance monotonically improves as memory grows. The result is a stress test specifically for the "recursive self-improvement" claim rather than for one-shot task performance.
- **Theme:** (1) Core LLM — agentic-system evaluation.
- **Why it made the cut:** Fills the evaluation gap for self-improving agents — this kind of benchmark is what makes progress in themes 1 and 2 measurable.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04003

## 4. Quo Vadis, World Modeling?
- **Authors:** Yu Yang, Xuemeng Yang, Licheng Wen, et al.
- **Summary:** A position/framework paper arguing that world models for agents should be reframed away from raw physical-state prediction toward agent-centric transitions of information the agent can actually use. It organizes existing "world proxies" into six functional forms (e.g., predictor, evaluator, planner-support) and shows how each supports a distinct stage of agent improvement. The framing is useful for choosing what an agent's internal model should even try to predict.
- **Theme:** (2) Multi-agent frameworks & orchestration (agent architectures).
- **Why it made the cut:** Rare architectural taxonomy that the theme-2 selections in other digests keep implicitly assuming.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02713

## 5. PCSD: Persistent Consistency for Self-Distillation in Agentic Reinforcement Learning
- **Authors:** Chunji Lv, Yangguang Wei, Junlin Liu, et al.
- **Summary:** In agentic RL fine-tuning, the teacher's preferences shift as the student improves, so PCSD derives token-level weights from teacher signals that stay persistent across training steps rather than snapshotting a moving target. This stabilizes self-distillation over long-horizon agent trajectories where naive teacher-favoring signals collapse or drift. The authors report better long-horizon task performance than standard on-policy distillation baselines.
- **Theme:** (1) Core LLM — fine-tuning for agentic RL.
- **Why it made the cut:** Method contribution, not just a benchmark; token-level credit inside an agentic RL loop is directly relevant.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01837

## 6. SWE-Touch: Benchmarking Coding Agents When Users Touch the Code
- **Authors:** Yuqiao Tan, Jinxiang Meng, Fangyu Lei, et al.
- **Summary:** Stress-tests coding agents in a shared workspace by injecting concurrent human-style edits ("Counter-Edits") mid-task and measuring whether the agent notices, integrates, or overwrites them. The benchmark exposes how brittle current agents are once the workspace is not exclusively theirs, which is a form of correctness-under-interference the usual SWE-bench-style setups never probe. It reframes coding-agent correctness as a workspace-awareness property, not just a diff-quality property.
- **Theme:** (3) Spec-driven development / formal correctness for coding agents.
- **Why it made the cut:** The only paper in-window that seriously operationalizes "is the coding agent's output still correct under adversarial workspace conditions," which is a practical stand-in for spec adherence.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02499

## 7. Video-DeepResearch: Towards the Next-Generation Multimodal Deepresearch Agent
- **Authors:** Zhen Fang, Yu Zeng, Wenxuan Huang, et al.
- **Summary:** Extends the "deep research" agent pattern from static text and images to long video streams by splitting the pipeline into a perception module that harvests evidence and an exploration module that plans further queries. The decoupling lets each side scale independently and reportedly reaches 64.0% accuracy on complex video QA tasks that require multi-hop evidence gathering. The design is a concrete instantiation of role separation inside a single-agent-plus-tools stack.
- **Theme:** (2) Multi-agent frameworks & orchestration.
- **Why it made the cut:** Perception/exploration split is a general orchestration pattern worth tracking beyond the video domain.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03979

## 8. LLaDA MoE v2: Scaling Mixture-of-Experts Diffusion Language Models
- **Authors:** Fengqi Zhu, Shaoxuan Xu, Jingyang Ou, et al.
- **Summary:** Characterizes empirical scaling behavior when MoE is stacked on top of diffusion language models, and proposes design principles (expert count, routing, active-parameter budget) that give predictable compute-optimal curves. The scaling laws are new for the MoE-diffusion combination specifically, and the paper positions this as a practical recipe rather than just a curiosity.
- **Theme:** (1) Core LLM.
- **Why it made the cut:** Concrete scaling recipe for a still-fluid model family that shows up in agentic pipelines.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03457

## 9. SkillJack: Persistent Skill Backdoors in Self-Evolving Agents
- **Authors:** Zonghao Ying, Xiangfan Wu, Huiyu Wu, et al.
- **Summary:** Introduces the first attack that targets the experience-to-skill pipeline of self-evolving agents rather than their base weights: a poisoned experience trace gets promoted into a persistent "skill" and then invoked as part of normal operation later. This surfaces skill-evolution as a distinct attack surface and shows current agent architectures have no lifecycle protection for how skills are formed and retained.
- **Theme:** (2) Multi-agent frameworks & orchestration — architecture / safety property of self-evolving agents.
- **Why it made the cut:** Architectural implication for any framework that lets agents accumulate skills — informs how such systems should be designed defensively.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03509

## 10. AURORA-LM: Autoencoding Unified Representation for Continuous-Latent Diffusion Language Modeling
- **Authors:** Jiajun Liang, Yucheng Liao, Yukang Cao, et al.
- **Summary:** Cleanly separates learning a decodable continuous text representation from learning the diffusion process over it, so the two objectives stop interfering during training. On reported benchmarks it comes out as the strongest among the evaluated diffusion-based LMs, which suggests the decoupling is doing real work rather than just being cosmetic.
- **Theme:** (1) Core LLM.
- **Why it made the cut:** Architectural cleanup for continuous-latent diffusion LMs; useful reference point for the diffusion-LM track alongside LLaDA MoE v2 above.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02602
